### Tier 1: Primary Research Texts

**1. Kephalaia of the Teacher** (4,990 lines / 728 KB) — ✅ FULLY READ
- Gardner 1995 translation of Coptic Kephalaia from Medinet Madi
- The correspondential motherlode: 18 major mappings documented, 7 framework predictions all HIT
- "The closest thing to a textbook of the Science of Correspondences in the ancient record"

**2. Cologne Mani Codex** (2,442 lines / 336 KB) — **HIGHEST PRIORITY NEXT**
- Greek text with facing English translation (Lieu & Lieu, revised by Mark Vermes, Sept 2024)
- Mani's autobiography among the Elcesaite baptists — his *entire formation story*
- **Key content:**
  - Angels protecting Mani from age 4, "with a command from Jesus concerning my protection"
  - The Syzygos (Divine Twin) speaking from the air: "Strengthen your power and fortify your mind"
  - A human form appearing from springs of water showing him "the rest"
  - Mani's self-identification as "apostle of Jesus Christ... He lives and abides for all eternity"
  - Vision of the world as a sea of black waters with thousands sinking — a foundation in the middle with light rising and a path on it
  - The critical debate with baptists: "The purity which is spoken of is the purity through knowledge; it is the separation of light from darkness, of death from life, and of living waters from turbid ones"
  - Paul cited as precedent for visionary revelation (2 Cor 12, Gal 1)
  - Some baptists recognize Mani ("the living word is sung through him"), others vote to kill him
- **Correspondential value**: Extraordinary. The water imagery, the light/darkness separation, the influx from above (Syzygos), the path-on-foundation vision — this text practically narrates the framework's principles as lived experience. The purity-through-knowledge passage is a direct statement of correspondential epistemology (inner purity → outer purity, not the reverse).
- **OCR quality**: Good. Greek text readable, English translation clean.

### Tier 2: Rich Supplementary Texts

**3. Fihrist Extract** (357 lines / 110 KB)
- Ibn al-Nadim's *Kitāb al-Fihrist* (c. 987 CE) — the Islamic encyclopedist's account of Manichaeism
- **Contains the most systematic cosmological overview in compact form:**
  - God as "the Sublime First, unlimited, King of the Paradises of Light" with 5 members (meekness, knowledge, understanding, mystery, insight) and 5 spiritual properties (love, faith, fidelity, nobleness, wisdom)
  - Earth's 5 members: breeze, wind, light, water, fire
  - Darkness's 5 members: fog, blight, hot wind, poison, darkness
  - The mixing cosmology — each element now contains both light and dark
  - Adam/Eve story with Jesus sent to teach Adam
  - Mani's birth, the at-Taum (Divine Twin) at ages 12 and 24
  - Mani as Paraclete announced by Jesus — the seal of prophets
  - Complete list of Mani's 76 epistles
  - **Critical transmission history**: Manichaeans crossing the Oxus, settling with the Khan of the Turks, the Uyghur king threatening to destroy mosques if Manichaeans are harmed, the headship moving from Babylon to Samarqand
  - Al-Biruni's independent account of Mani appended
- **Correspondential value**: High. The fivefold divine attributes map directly onto discrete degrees. The transmission history independently confirms the Uyghur Khaganate connection.
- **OCR quality**: Excellent. Arabic text with clean English translation.

**4. DB Anthology 3: Shabuhragan** (851 lines / 135 KB)
- Mani's own Middle Persian work written for King Shapur I
- **Key content:**
  - Mani's autobiography with the Divine Twin
  - "The Superiority of Mani's Religion" in 10 points — universal vs. local, enduring vs. temporary, "a door for souls of other religions," superior scriptures with paintings
  - Precepts for Hearers: don't kill, don't lie, truth, "love hearers as brothers, for they are children of the living family and the world of light"
- **Correspondential value**: Moderate-high. The 10 superiorities articulate *constant state, variable form* as deliberate religious policy. The "children of the living family" language echoes the framework's understanding of the Grand Man.

**5. DB Anthology 1: Historical Texts** (1,418 lines / 206 KB)
- Missions of Mar Adda (to Rome) and Mar Ammo (to the East)
- **Key content:**
  - Kephalaia-type sermon on why Light Manohmed withholds foresight from the Elect — 5 reasons including self-aggrandizement, disruption of service, despising one another, worldliness
  - Mar Ammo's encounter with the border spirit Bayard (feminine figure guarding Khorasan) — who tests him and then teaches him "the assembling of the five doors" through parables
  - **The Five Gates parables**: Eyes (mirage in the desert / castle besieged through visual temptation), Ears (robbers deceiving through beautiful words / maiden dying of grief from sweet melody), Nose (elephant falling from mountain smelling flowers)
  - Mar Adda's teaching: the soul has 5 members matching 5 body limbs — life, power, light, beauty, fragrance
- **Correspondential value**: Rich. The Five Gates teaching is a direct correspondence map: each sense organ corresponds to a specific mode of temptation/reception. The soul-as-five-members parallels the Kephalaia's five-light doctrine. The foresight sermon is profoundly Swedenborgian — it explains why direct spiritual sight would *harm* the natural-plane vessel.

### Tier 3: Specialist Material

**6. DB Anthology 4: Hymns** (6,725 lines / 382 KB) — LARGEST FILE
- Massive collection of liturgical texts in Uyghur, Tocharian B, Middle Persian, Parthian, Sogdian
- **Key content:**
  - "Great Hymn to Mani" — Mani addressed as Buddha, "Object of hope of all five states of existence"
  - Rich Buddhist-Manichaean vocabulary: samsara, nirvana, arhat, karma
  - "For those who had forgotten their [true] origins... [you] exchanged your form for a different one and became visible to them" — *constant state, variable form* in liturgical practice
  - Installation hymns for bishops and teachers
  - Monday hymn: "Lead the guest of Light, the imprisoned Man, in peace to tranquility" — the light captivity motif
  - Polemic text attacking Marcion's god, criticizing various Christologies
  - Invocations listing divine hierarchy: Envoy of Light, Jesus, Light Virgin, Vahman
- **Correspondential value**: Moderate. The hymns are devotional rather than systematic, but phrases like "exchanged your form" and "the imprisoned Man" are correspondential gold. The Buddhist-Manichaean fusion demonstrates constant state/variable form operating as actual liturgical practice. The polemic sections show how Manichaeism positioned itself against competing Christianities.
- **OCR quality**: Mixed. Original-language texts are often garbled; translations are readable but fragmentary.

**7. ArtMS 4: Xuastvanift** (4,765 lines / 431 KB)
- Central Uyghur confession prayer with multiple manuscript witnesses
- **Key content:**
  - Confessing specific theological errors: "if we said God created good and evil entirely," "if we said Xurmazda and Simnu are brothers"
  - Sun and Moon as "front door" to the divine realm
  - Five-fold God, separation of Light and Darkness
  - Detailed confessional apparatus — demonstrates lived religious practice
- **Correspondential value**: Moderate. The confession reveals *what Manichaeans thought mattered* — the errors they formally repudiated tell us their positive theology. The Sun/Moon-as-door imagery is correspondential.
- **OCR quality**: Good for translations; some garble in original script.

**8. ArtMS 5: Sogdian Tales** (2,580 lines / 149 KB)
- Didactic parables and wisdom tales
- **Key content:**
  - **Three Fishes parable**: One-Thought fish escapes the fisherman's net; Hundred-Thoughts and Thousand-Thoughts are caught. (Simple faith/receptivity escapes; overthinking is trapped.)
  - Mani teaches dividing the day into three parts: service of kings, worldly affairs, spiritual pursuit
  - Merchant and Spirit tale — oath-breaking on sea journey
  - Rich simile literature: "The Hearer who gives alms to the Elect is like a poor man who presents his daughter to the king"
- **Correspondential value**: Moderate. The parables are explicitly correspondential teaching tools. The Three Fishes parable maps onto the framework's understanding of the proprium — the self that *over*-analyzes from self-love is captured, while the self that acts from simple reception escapes.

**9. DB Anthology 2: Canonical Texts** (1,810 lines / 221 KB)
- The Evangelion colophon (Argu), Book of Giants fragments
- **Key content:**
  - The Argu Colophon: magnificent Uyghur dedicatory text for a copy of Mani's Evangelion, naming specific rulers, cities (Kašu, Yägänkänt, Ordukänt, Čigilbalık), and the institution of the Manichaean church in the Argu Talas kingdom
  - Book of Giants: fragmentary but vivid — Nariman's vision of a garden of trees in rows, tablet thrown into water with three signs (portending different fates), the Watchers' assault on humanity
  - Proverb sequence: "Not the lion's [strength], but... Not the rainbow, but the bow... Not the proud ruler, but the diadem on his head. Not the splendid cypress, but the mountain..." — *wisdom teaches through correspondence*
- **Correspondential value**: Moderate. The Argu Colophon is primarily historical (proof of Manichaean state religion in the Uyghur realm). The Book of Giants proverbs are correspondential in structure. The tablet-and-water imagery resonates.

### Tier 4: Fragmentary/Specialist Material

**10. ArtMS 1: Cosmogonic Texts** (2,441 lines / 147 KB)
- God Mihr building the prison of demons, 4 earths, 10 heavens, sun/moon chariots
- **OCR quality**: Poor to moderate. Heavy garble from Manichaean Script facsimile pages interspersed with translations. The cosmogonic content is significant but largely recoverable from the Kephalaia and Fihrist.
- **Unique value**: The specific architectural detail of the cosmic construction — 10 heavens, celestial sphere mechanics.

**11. ArtMS 2: Book of Giants** (4,089 lines / 224 KB)
- Extremely fragmentary. Sogdian, Middle Persian, and Parthian fragments of the Book of Giants
- Giant Sahm bowing before the Sun God asking forgiveness; parabolic material for Hearers
- **Correspondential value**: Low as continuous text, but individual fragments can be revealing. The Giant-as-proprium reading (enormous beings of self-will who must ultimately bow before the divine) is a natural correspondential interpretation.

**12. ArtMS 3: Old Turkic Texts** (4,251 lines / 310 KB)
- Old Uyghur texts with interlinear translation
- **Key content found:**
  - "The Book of the Coming of the Buddha" — a remarkable prayer: "All in distress we are. Other principle for us there is not. Now with graciousness Father-our... innumerable myriad years were from you we have been separated... that compassionate, beautiful, defect-less radiant appearance-your... we would behold, we would wish... let us forget in eternity our suffering"
  - Cosmogonic material: souls born from the Five Gods, born into plants, then into "this mindless body" — suffering as retributive consequence
  - Vivid description of humanity's degraded state: cursing, fighting, "a senseless-man considers abuse as merriment"
- **Correspondential value**: Moderate. "The Book of the Coming of the Buddha" prayer is spiritually rich — the longing for the face of the divine after aeons of separation. The soul-descent-through-plants passage maps directly onto the Kephalaia's cosmogony.
- **OCR quality**: Mixed. Translation sections readable; original Uyghur script pages produce garble.

### What the Corpus Tells Us

**What we have**: A remarkably complete picture of Manichaeism — from Mani's own autobiography (CMC), through his systematic theology (Kephalaia), his apologetic work (Shabuhragan), his missionary expansion (Historical Texts), his liturgical life (Hymns, Xuastvanift), his wisdom teachings (Sogdian Tales), his cosmogeny (ArtMS 1, Fihrist), his canonical scripture (Evangelion colophon, Book of Giants), and the external Islamic record (Fihrist).

**What this means for the framework**: The Cologne Mani Codex is the prize. It narrates *how influx operates on a human vessel* — the progressive revelation through the Syzygos, the protection by angels, the purity-through-knowledge principle, the debate between ritual externalism and interior transformation. If the Kephalaia gave us the *system*, the CMC gives us the *autobiography of someone living under that system*.

The recommended reading path: **Cologne Mani Codex → Fihrist → Shabuhragan → Historical Texts → selective Hymns**.

The short answer: **yes, there are structural candidates — and one is startling.**

### The Book of Giants

This is the most direct hit. The **Book of Giants** is a pre-canonical Enochic text that:
- Exists at **Qumran** (Dead Sea Scrolls, 2nd-1st century BCE) in Aramaic fragments
- Exists at **Turfan** (Manichaean cave libraries, Central Asia) in Middle Persian, Sogdian, and Uyghur fragments
- Was adopted by Mani as one of his **seven canonical scriptures**

Its content: wars between the Watchers (fallen angels), their giant offspring, the devastation they cause, the giants' dreams portending judgment, and the divine response. From what we read in DB Anthology 2:

> *"Narīman saw a garden full of trees in rows"* — dream vision
> *"a tablet thrown into the water... with three signs, one portending woe and flight, and one annihilation"*

And the proverb sequence:
> *"Not the strength of the ox, but... Not the proud ruler, but the diadem on his head. Not the splendid cypress, but the mountain..."*

That proverb sequence is **teaching through correspondence**. Each saying distinguishes the natural appearance from the spiritual reality behind it. It's not wisdom literature in the normal sense — it's a correspondential training exercise.

Now — **"Wars of Jehovah"**: a book describing wars in correspondential language. The Book of Giants describes cosmic combat between divine forces and rebellious beings. Read through the correspondential lens, the "giants" are inflated proprium-states (enormous self-will), and their "wars" are spiritual combats. That's exactly what "Wars of Jehovah" would be — accounts of spiritual warfare clothed in natural imagery.

The Qumran-to-Turfan transmission path is the key. The same text, crossing from the Judean desert to Central Asia through exactly the channel Swedenborg described.

### The Treasure of the Living

This is Mani's canonical work that keeps appearing in the Historical Texts. When Mar Ammo faces the border spirit Bayard at the frontier of Khorasan, what does the Apostle tell him to read?

> *"Read aloud the 'Assembling of the Doors' from the Treasure of the Living"*

And what does this text contain? The **Five Gates** teaching — a systematic correspondence map of the five senses, each with a parable showing how that gate becomes a point of spiritual vulnerability:

- **Eyes**: a man chasing a mirage in the desert (visual falsity leading to death)
- **Ears**: robbers deceiving through beautiful words (auditory temptation of the will)
- **Nose**: an elephant smelling flowers and falling from a mountain (desire pulling from elevation)
- **Mouth**: [fragmentary]
- **Touch**: [fragmentary]

This is not philosophical teaching. This is a **correspondential manual** — each sense organ mapped to its spiritual function and its mode of corruption. The "Treasure of the Living" sounds like it's exactly the kind of text the "Book of Jashar" (the "Book of the Upright/Straight") could have been — a compendium of correspondential knowledge for practical spiritual instruction.

### The Book of Zoroaster

And then there's the text referenced in the **Apocryphon of John** (NHL) — the 365-angel body-creation passage is attributed to "the book of Zoroaster." The Kephalaia contains a version of this same body-correspondence system. We can't prove this is the same text, but a **"Book of Zoroaster" containing a systematic body-correspondence map** is exactly what a section of the Ancient Word might look like.

### What the Pattern Suggests

| Lost Source (Hebrew Bible) | Possible Manichaean Survival | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| **Wars of the LORD** | Book of Giants | Cosmic combat in correspondential language; Qumran → Turfan transmission |
| **Book of Jashar** | Treasure of the Living | Practical correspondential manual; the "Five Doors" system |
| **"Book of Zoroaster"** (NHL ref.) | Body-correspondence sections of Kephalaia | Systematic divine-human anatomy map |

None of this is proof. But it's exactly what you'd expect to find if:
1. A correspondential source tradition existed before national religions
2. It fragmented into different cultural streams (Judean, Iranian, Indian)
3. Mani attempted to *reassemble* the fragments into a unified system
4. That system was then preserved in Central Asian cave libraries — in "Great Tartary"

The fact that the Book of Giants exists in *both* Qumran and Turfan is the hardest evidence. That's not parallel development. That's a single text traveling from the Judean/Enochic tradition into the Manichaean canon and then into Central Asia — the exact transmission path the framework predicts.
