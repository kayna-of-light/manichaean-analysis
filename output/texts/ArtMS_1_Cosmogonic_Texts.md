ŠĀBUHRAGĀN RELATED TEXTS ON MANICHAEAN COSMOGONY
Foreword
The idea of producing an edition of Manichaean cosmogonic texts in Middle Iranian has been with me since the work first began on the compilation of the Database of Manichaean Texts more than two decades ago. Some of us are old enough to be subjected to what I call the 'MM-test' set by an earlier generation of Manichaean scholars, viz. to learn Middle Persian and Parthian by working one's way through the pioneering work of F. C. Andreas and W. B. Henning, Mitteliranische Manichaica aus Chinesisch-Turkestan I, SPAW 1932, 10, 175-222; II, ibid. 1933, 7, 294-363 and III, ibid. 1934, 27, 848-912. For me the fact that the Middle Iranian texts in the first two of he three fascicles were transliterated into Hebrew script was a near unsurmountable problem and to my mind also a thoroughly unnecessary and unwarranted one. It was possible, as Albert von Le Coq's editions have shown, for texts to be published in the Manichaean script. It was, I believe, due to an unfortunate fire in printery of the Verlag der Akademie der Wissenschaften (Berlin) ca 1912 which destroyed most of the leaded 'slugs' needed specally cast for printing in the Manichaean font that scholars like Andreas and Henning had to follow Salemann in printing the texts in the Hebrew script which to my mind is a poor typographical and potentially misleading substitute. To my mind to learn to read the Manichaean script was a far more sensible and practical proposition.
The appearance of a well designed (by Harvard University?) Manichaean Font at the turn of this century is a game changer and I have over the last decade begun to re-enter a number of key texts in the Database of Manichaean Texts in the Manichaean font. This means that scholars, especially earlier career researchers, will no longer have to master the Hebrew script before being able to use the excellent editions of Andreas and Henning. The availability of digital photographs of Turfan-texts in most in cases in full colour is another game changer. In this edition photographic images of the texts are an integral part of the edition and not an addendum. Some may question the need to present the text mechanically in the Manichaean script but this is necessary as not all the photographs are clear enough to allow for easy decipherment.
Two of the three groups of texts published in this volume (M98 I + M99 I and M7980-M7984) are in Middle Persian. Though from different codices, both have long been regarded by Manichaean scholars as belonging to the same text and are either part of Mani's Šābuhragān or closely related to this semi-canonical work. M506, a small additional fragment belonging to the same group of texts as M7980f., has also been included in this collection and assigned to its correct textual position. The third text is in Sogdian ([MIK] III 4990 = M178) but it is likely to have been a translation from Middle Persian version of the same semi-canonical work of Mani. The Sogdian text fills two important gaps in the cosmogonic drama as presented in the two Middle Persian texts and is therefore included as the first text in the collection. Because the recto and verso of the first page give an account of the Kingdom of Light before it was invaded by the forces of Darkness it has been given here as the first of the three groups of texts.
I am grateful to Dr Lilla Russell-Smith for permission to reproduce the photogaphs of MIK III 4990 which is housed in the Museum of Asiatic Art in Berlin and which she had kindly made available to me some years ago for the purpose of research. I would also like to thank (Em.) Professor Prods Oktor Skjævø of Harvard University for permission to use his yet unpublished English translation of the texts in this volume.
Samuel N.C. Lieu FBA
Cambridge UK

(1) M178 (= [MIK] III 4990) (Sogdian)
Because of its excellent state of preservation and exquisitely executed calligraphy and the unusual fact that it was written on vellum and not on paper, M178 (now MIK III 4990) had attracted the attention of scholars at the early stages of the study of Manichaean text fragments from Turfan. Several lines of the text were published in facsimile format by Müller in his pioneering study of the Manichaean script1 long before the Sogdian language in which it was written was successfully deciphered.
1 F. W. K. Müller, 'Handschriften-Reste in Estrangelo-Schrift aus Turfan, Chinesisch-Turkistan, I', SPAW 1904, 348-52

F.C. Andreas and W.B. Henning
MIDDLE IRANIAN MANICHAICA FROM CHINESE TURKESTAN
I Preamble
In 1918, in a foreword to translations of Middle Iranian Manichaica, which were intended for a publication planned together with R. Reitzenstein, F.C. Andreas wrote: "In my opinion, translations of Manichaean Turfan fragments should only be published with simultaneous publication of the originals and a linguistic and factual commentary that vindicates the translation and clearly separates the uncertain from the certain ... If, contrary to my better conviction, I now merely provide translations then this results exclusively from the request of my colleague Reitzenstein, who felt that he could not do without the publication of my translations for his work." It must appear doubtful whether Andreas would have considered the text edition, which is hereby given to the public under his name, as being ready for publication at this stage. Nevertheless, it seemed justified not to let the results of his many years of intensive work fall into oblivion.
This edition is based on a relatively small part of the writings by Andreas himself; what was available was sent to me at the instigation of Prof. Herman Lommel of the Göttingen University Library. Essentially, it is based on his own notes from Andreas' lectures in the years 1926-30, supplemented by impartations from two other of Andreas's pupils, Dr. Lentz and Dr. Polotsky, and many quires that were thankfully left. Wherever there were gaps in the material at hand, to the best of my ability, and whenever it seemed harmless so to do, I have made supplements of my own. In the few cases where I was not certain that I had expressed Andreas' opinion, I have indicated my authorship with the letters W.H. I would also like to note that I have uniformly organised the transcription in Latin letters: for example, where Andreas fluctuated between abär abar, oßor, ußor, ufur, I consistently write afar, since I was not able to decide on which renderings Andreas preferred to use in his last years.
I express my respectful thanks to Councillor Lüders as chairman of the Oriental Commission of the Prussian Academy of Sciences for his kind permission to publish.
W. Henning.
