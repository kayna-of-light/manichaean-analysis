## Chapter 42
### Concerning the Three Vessels

*The oldest teaching layer is strongly dominant. The chapter teaches the cosmic process of three-vessel discharge through two extended correspondential analogies (doctor/healing and mother/birth with body-layer enumeration) and a systematic twelve-deed enumeration. The analogies are structurally rich: the birth analogy maps seven body-layers (marrow, bones, tendons, flesh, veins, blood, skin) doubled through mother+child to eighteen garments — classic body-cosmos correspondence. The twelve deeds (¶14-32) are compact substrate enumeration of cosmic advantages. Editorial overlay is light: dialogue frame in ¶1-2 and ¶13, counterfactual contemporary applications in ¶16-20 (with 'today' anchors and one institutional reference to fasting in ¶19), and a closing self-reference in ¶33. The negative editorial fatigue score (-0.79) confirms the second half is cleaner than the first. Teaching purity 0.670. The seam flags on ¶17-18 are likely false positives — the bridge connectives continue a three-fold iteration (one per vessel) rather than extending to institutional content.*

**Temporal observations:**
- ¶1: High dialogue_frame score (28.38) confirms formulaic opening. The substantive cosmological question defines the teaching topic: three vessels, their discharge from above, separation from rulers in the heavens, and the condition of the rulers during discharge. This question structures what follows.
- ¶2: Dialogue_frame score 22.22 and exhortation 13.89 confirm the frame+address wrapper. The remaining teaching fragment is very lacunose but describes the character of the discharge process. 'Slaughter' is striking — may indicate violent cosmic process.
- ¶3: Pure correspondential analogy. The doctor analogy maps healing process → cosmic process: three medicines that discharge sickness above, below, and cure wounds = three garments of the Living Spirit. Both sides of the mapping stay within the cosmic system. The three-fold structure (above, below, wounds) corresponds to the three vessels (water, darkness, fire). Zero editorial vocabulary.
- ¶4: Continuation of the doctor analogy. 'Dissolve all magic' and 'aroma of the medicine' are correspondential vocabulary — healing through aromatic/sensory qualities maps to cosmic purification through elemental properties. 'Manifested above and below' echoes cosmic spatial structure. Fragmentary but consistently substrate.
- ¶5: Explicit cosmic application of the doctor analogy. Maps three healing drugs → three garments (wind, water, fire), and three discharges → three vessels (water, darkness, fire). The Living Spirit's three garments cause three effects in the rulers: hard-heartedness, shaking/fear, derangement — correspondential mapping of garment → psychological effect. Both sides remain within the cosmic system.
- ¶6: Classic body-cosmos correspondence. Seven body-layers enumerated systematically (marrow→bones→tendons→flesh→veins→blood→skin), then doubled through mother+child to reach eighteen garments. This numbered-layer body mapping is the oldest register in the text — the same type of systematic correspondence found in zoomorphic-realm and metal-body teachings. The enumeration structure IS the teaching.
- ¶7: Application of birth analogy to cosmic process. 'The smell of the action of the demon, which brings about the stench of birth' — correspondential language mapping sensory quality (smell/stench) to cosmic action (demonic force). The three garments of the Living Spirit correspond to the forces that open the womb. Both sides of the mapping remain within the cosmic system.
- ¶8: First of the three-fold garment teaching. Garment of water → effect on rulers. Body vocabulary (veins, entrails) maps cosmic anatomy. Very fragmentary but the cosmic mechanism is clear: the water garment acts on the rulers' internal substance (poison, death in veins/entrails). Substrate score 11.11.
- ¶9: Second garment: wind → sweeps destructive shadow/dirt → discharges to earth → confines in vessel of darkness surrounding universe. Systematic cosmic process vocabulary: 'swept', 'discharged', 'confined'. Two-stage process: first to earth, then to outer vessel. Highest substrate score in chapter (15.69). Pure cosmic mechanism.
- ¶10: Third garment: living fire → discharges dark fire from rulers → throws to earth → confines in vessel/wall of great fire surrounding all worlds. Same two-stage cosmic process as ¶9. The 'wall of great fire' is cosmic architecture — named structure at the edge of the universe. Both sides of every mapping remain cosmic.
- ¶11: Describes the temporal sequencing of cosmic process — the three garments were applied sequentially, not simultaneously. This explains the ORDER of cosmic operations. Process-oriented substrate vocabulary. Zero editorial scores.
- ¶12: Extremely fragmentary. The surviving words ('universe', 'vessels') place it within the cosmic process description. Likely concluded the three-garment teaching before the twelve deeds transition.
- ¶13: Transition to the twelve-deeds enumeration. The teaching content introduces the twelve-fold structure and restates the two-stage cosmic process (above→earth→edges of worlds). 'The use of the matter' and 'management' are systematic process vocabulary. The enumeration framework (twelve great deeds) is substrate — it defines the teaching structure.
- ¶14: First deed stated: containment of evil in the universe. Sets up a three-fold sub-enumeration. Enumeration marker 'The first deed is this' is part of the substrate structure. The three sub-items parallel the three vessels.
- ¶15: Extremely fragmentary. First sub-item of the three-fold enumeration within the first deed. The enumeration marker survives even though the content is lost.
- ¶16: Second sub-item of the first deed. Enumeration marker 'The second is error' is substrate. The counterfactual describes a cosmic mechanism: waters in the rulers above would produce torrents/droplets that cause lust to sprout in trees/fruits/flesh. This is cosmic-to-cosmic mapping (dark water → lust in biological forms). The 'today' passage pivots to contemporary application. Quite fragmentary throughout.
- ¶17: Seam flag fired on 'Not only this' but institutional vocabulary field was empty — false positive for institutional extension. This is actually the second iteration of the three-vessel counterfactual (darkness), paralleling the water iteration in ¶16. The cosmic mechanism (drops of darkness dripping, torrents flowing from above, assuming trees/flesh) is substrate content describing how dark substances propagate. The contemporary application ('today', 'a thousand people', 'mankind') is editorial.
- ¶18: Third iteration of the three-vessel counterfactual (fire). Seam flag fired on 'Furthermore' but again no institutional content. The cosmic mechanism describes fire remaining in the rulers, its two-stage discharge (earth → vessel of fire), and torrential flow. Parallels the teaching structure of ¶8-10. The 'until today' is an editorial intrusion within otherwise substrate process description.
- ¶19: Continuation of ¶18 (fire counterfactual). The single retained sentence describes cosmic mechanism: dark fire multiplying in biological forms (tree, fruit, flesh) — cosmic-to-cosmic mapping. The removed material follows the same editorial pattern as ¶17 ('a thousand people today... a single person') plus an institutional reference to fasting that anchors the teaching to Manichaean community practice.
- ¶20: Summary of the first deed. 'Three archetypes' is a substrate teaching term summarizing the three-fold vessel purification (water/darkness/fire managed through wind/water/fire garments). The 'Behold, I have taught you' is characteristic dialogue-frame self-reference. Very fragmentary.
- ¶21: Second deed — purification of heavens from sediment and pollution. Fragmentary but cosmic process vocabulary (heavens, sediment, pollution). Enumeration marker preserved.
- ¶22: Third deed — relief of heavens/firmaments from burden. 'Weight-carriers' is cosmic structural vocabulary — refers to beings/structures that bear the cosmic weight. Body-cosmos correspondence: the heavens had a burden that was relieved. Substrate score 8.57.
- ¶23: Fourth deed — purification of cosmic dwelling places. Very fragmentary. 'Dwelling places' is cosmic geography vocabulary.
- ¶24: Fifth deed — purification of the cosmic ships (sun and moon vessels in Manichaean cosmology). 'Ships' is cosmic architecture vocabulary. 'Greatness and richness' dwelling in ships describes the cosmic beings/light inhabiting these vessels. Both sides remain within the cosmic system.
- ¶25: Sixth deed — purification of cosmic 'temples above.' These are not earthly temples but cosmic structures ('above') that contain mixed light/dark substance. 'Mixed, incomplete, and unconfined' describes the cosmic condition before purification. The process: rest reaches → passes through → purifies. Systematic cosmic process.
- ¶26: Seventh deed — the vessels advancing to their cosmic positions. Very fragmentary. 'Places and sites' is cosmic geography vocabulary.
- ¶27: Eighth deed — construction of cosmic boundary structures. 'Great enclosures and walls' are cosmic architecture — the containment structures at the edge of the universe (cf. 'wall of great fire' in ¶10). Brief but clearly substrate.
- ¶28: Ninth deed — cosmic eschatological sign of dissolution. 'The apostles' is ambiguous — could be institutional (historical apostles) or cosmic (envoys/messengers as cosmic beings). The context is cosmic eschatology ('dissolution of the universe'), and the institutional vocabulary score is 0.0, which favors a cosmic reading. Flagging the ambiguity. 'Dissolution of the universe' is substrate cosmic-process vocabulary.
- ¶29: Tenth deed — eschatological purification. The vessels themselves become 'releasers and purifiers' at the cosmic end. Fire analogy for final purification. Cosmic eschatology with process vocabulary.
- ¶30: Eleventh deed — very fragmentary. 'Crossing' and 'ruling-power' are cosmic vocabulary. Substrate score 20.0 (highest in chapter) despite brevity — dense with cosmic terminology.
- ¶31: Continues the eleventh deed. Describes cosmic geography: 'the outer land', 'the aeons of light are outside', 'the place of peace', 'the place of honour and grace.' The 'householders who dwell in it' are cosmic inhabitants of the light-aeons, not earthly community members. Both sides of the description stay within the cosmic system. Slightly devotional register ('shall never pass away nor recede') but describing the state of cosmic places, not exhorting an audience.
- ¶32: Twelfth deed — the wall of fire as cosmic barrier hiding evil from the aeons of light. 'The wall of fire that surrounds the entire building' echoes ¶10 ('wall of great fire') — cosmic architecture. 'The entire building' = the universe as constructed whole. Both sides remain within the cosmic system: evil hidden from light-aeons by structural barrier.
- ¶33: Summary and closing of the twelve-deeds teaching. Restates the cosmic process (vessels discharged from firmaments → earth → extremity) and introduces 'the three words of life' as cosmic guardians. 'Set over them in great power' and 'firmly set thus in these fetters' are cosmic-structural vocabulary. The self-referential closing formula is characteristic of the dialogue frame layer.
