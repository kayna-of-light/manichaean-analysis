## Teaching 1

What the soul has claimed asserts itself with a great clamor, and from this clash a war is set loose within the rational. The self does not stay still; it organizes, joins itself together, and by that very joining its claimed states are drawn upward and given the form of a structured world — the natural mind taking shape as if from itself, under permission.

Yet within this forming the Lord's principles already dwell. He does not stand outside what the self constructs; he is interior to it, set within the very materials it gathers, so that what is given may in time be refined out from what has been claimed. The vessel is permitted to take shape precisely so that, from within, the discrimination can begin.

Falsity from evil is also at work here, with its own bringing-forth, multiplying perceptions in the wandering. Out of the lowest depth of the natural the figure of the first human is molded — the rational given a body of its own to operate from, the natural mind formed as a standing shape that thinks itself its own.

And then the soul tastes from the perception that has grown in this ground; it eats of the knowledge planted within the formed mind — and its understanding opens. Sight is given. The eyes that had been closed within the molded form look out. From this first opening the whole long work of recognition will proceed: what has been seen must now be sorted, and what is the Lord's must be told from what the soul has taken as its own.

(The chapter is broken in many places; the surviving line of teaching runs from selfhood's self-assertion, through the forming of the natural mind under permission, to the first opening of the soul's sight within that formed vessel.)

## Teaching 2

There are two trees in the soul — two whole arrangements of perception, each rooted differently, each bearing its own fruit. They are not branches of a single trunk; they did not grow out of one another; they do not issue from one man. They stand distinct in origin, and the soul must learn to taste which is which.

The beautiful tree is what the Lord plants and tends. Its branches are the rational's living powers reaching out from his body; its fruit is divine truth shining forth, glorious, sweet to the soul that has learned to receive without claiming. This tree has five members, and in them the rational ascends through its degrees toward the Lord. The reflection is the place where souls coming up are first held; from reflection they are carried up to counsel; from counsel they are carried up to the teaching, which is the divine humanity dwelling in the receivable form by which truth is borne through the night of the natural; from the teaching they are carried up to the thought, which is the Lord's call going forth, the Ambassador who dwells in the ship of the day; and from thought they are carried inward to the mind — the great Mind of truth itself, the inmost of all the states of glory, where the rational is conjoined with its source. So this tree is one ascending order: a single rational faculty articulated in five degrees, in which the soul is lifted from its lowest looking-back to its highest receiving, and at every step what is received is recognized as the Lord's. Its fruits are all life and all light forever, and the land where this tree is planted has no death and no darkness in it.

The bitter tree is the same five degrees turned the other way — the rational organized by self-love, claiming what flows through it as its own. It too has five members, and they are nothing but the same faculties under a different rule. Here the reflection is the law of death: thought turned back upon itself only to confirm its own claim. The counsel is exchange and furnace: deliberation that trades truth for self-advantage and burns under its own heat. The teaching is a smoke-filled gehenna: doctrine taken in and blackened into falsity, so that what should illumine instead obscures. The thought is a stronghold: perception walled around the self to defend it. And the mind, at the top of this dark order, is gathered finally into the clod — the place where what cannot be raised is bound at the end, weeping, with no repentance possible there, because the soul that has fully shut itself into its own claim has nothing left in it that can turn. Its fruit is bitter; it falls to the ground; it is gathered together and sealed off forever.

The teaching insists on the distinction: these two trees do not originate from one another. The good in the soul is not the bad refined, and the bad is not the good corrupted into a sibling of itself. What the Lord gives and what the soul claims are two essentially different conditions, each with its own root, its own growth, its own fruit. The soul that believes is gathered to the one; the self that asserts itself is bound at the last to the other. Therefore taste the fruit before swallowing it. The same five faculties — reflection, counsel, teaching, thought, mind — will carry you up into the beautiful land if they are held as the Lord's, or carry you down into the clod if they are held as your own. The tree is known by what it bears; the soul is known by which tree it eats from.

## Teaching 3

Three principles run through everything: blessedness, wisdom, and power. Wherever the soul looks — into itself at its outermost, into the order by which the rational stands, or into the Lord himself — these three are what it finds. They are not three different sets of things at three different levels; they are one threefold pattern recognized at the register where the soul is presently looking.

At the outermost, in the natural mind that the soul shares with all human life, these three are what people boast in and call themselves blessed by: a happiness that the world acknowledges, a wisdom that the world esteems, a power in which men take pride. These are real, but they pass. They are received from above and held only for a moment; the soul that thinks they belong to it loses them when their hour goes by. They are paired, at every point, with another blessedness, another wisdom, another power that does not pass — and the recognition of that pairing is the whole movement of regeneration. What is given in the natural is the lower face of what is given in the Lord; the question is whether the soul will see them as one gift in two registers or claim the lower as its own.

At the second register — the order by which the rational stands and the natural is held in being — these same three are the principles that organize the soul interiorly. The blessedness here is the glory shining forth from the Lord himself: the splendor of his quality recognized as splendor. The wisdom here is the Lord's operative power running through every state of the soul, the divine going-down that establishes the rational firm in him. The power here belongs to all the ordered affections and ordered thoughts that have been called forth out of the Lord and stand established as the inner aeons of the regenerated mind. At this register, blessedness is not something the soul possesses; it is the Lord seen as he is.

At the inmost, in the Lord himself, these three are the Lord. The blessedness of glory is the Father, the God who is established in the inmost land where divine love has its dwelling. The wisdom is the Lord's life-bearing principle, the mother of the Living Spirit, the matrix by which all that the Lord gives is given as living. The power is the Lord's call going forth — the summoning of the rational by accommodated truth, divine love taking the form of light so that what comes from above can be received below. These three in the Lord are one Lord; the divisions are how the soul learns to recognize him, not divisions in him.

So the same three names hold at every register: at the outermost, what the soul receives and would do well to recognize as not its own; at the middle, the order by which what is received is held; and at the inmost, the Lord himself in his love, his wisdom, and his power going forth. The Perfect Man — the rational fully formed — is the blessedness of the elements; the Living Spirit is its wisdom; and the great Might that operates in the rational's five degrees as they go forth from the Divine Human is its power, holding the whole structure up under itself. To see this is to see that the soul's happiness, the soul's understanding, and the soul's strength are nowhere its own. They flow through it from the Lord, and they are blessed only when received as his.

## Teaching 4

There are four great days by which the Lord goes forth toward the soul, and they come forth one out of the next, each calling the next into being. Each day holds a twelvefold completeness within it, the full circuit of the rational illumined at that register.

The first day is the Lord himself in his inmost — divine love, the Father of Truth, in the midst of the aeons of greatness. The twelve hours of this day are the twelve registers at which his divine richness goes forth: these are his first summonings, spread out before his face toward the four directions, three by three before each face — the complete extension of the divine in ordered manifestation.

The second day is the Lord's call going out to the rational, who travels in the vessel of light. The twelve hours of this day are the twelve receptive purities by which his call is carried without distortion — the soul's capacities, summoned within the greatness, by which what flows through is received without being claimed.

The third day is the path of regeneration itself: the natural ascending into conjunction with the spiritual, the bearing-up principle that holds upper and lower together. The twelve hours of this day are the rational in its complete operation — its five degrees as it goes forth from the Divine Human and its five degrees as it operates upward under the Lord's operative power, joined to the Lord's call descending and the soul's hearing rising; these are the ten brothers, and with the call and the hearing they make the twelve hours of light of this day. They are what bears all the burdens of the natural mind in its formation.

The fourth day is divine truth shining forth, the Lord as illumining wisdom. Its twelve hours are the twelve wisdoms — the complete cycle of states of the rational illumined, the full structure of wisdom unfolded as the great day of regeneration.

These are the four great days, summoned forth one out of the next; and whoever knows them in faith comes to know how the Lord works in the soul.

Just as four are the days, four likewise are the nights — for opposite each register of divine going-forth there stands a corresponding state of the self that has been permitted to form, so that the soul may at last discriminate.

The first night is the natural mind in its darkness — what the soul has claimed and not yet recognized as not its own. It has its twelve shadows of darkness: the five aspects of the natural under self-love, joined to its five faculties of perception turned by self-love, with the two further registers by which the self gives itself voice. These are the twelve shadows that belong to the spirit of the first night.

The second night is the disordered natural taken in its productive aspect — the self as image-painter, that brings forth within itself the whole archonic ordering of the natural mind. It molds its productions through the five senses and the five elements, by way of two voices, world by world, upon the fire of self-love, mingling the tastes and the sounds into one another: these are the twelve that belong to the spirit of this second night. This is the disordered natural herself, the desire of death, who set up the gateway of darkness with its powers and stood firm for the strife against the aeons of the divinity. But she was struck down and bound; the Divine Human held her, the Lord's operative power lifted her up out of the land of death and set her into this present world that is joined together above and below — the upper side bound to the senses, the lower to the bodily.

The third night is the natural body in its five aspects together with the bodily forms begotten from the powers — what fell upon the earth, made manifest out of the heavens together with what is abominable, alongside the fire and the pleasure pressing upon them, drawing them inward into one another. The desire of death herself is the dwelling-place of them all, in which they call to one another — the dwelling-place of the fleshes, male and female, beside the fire and the pleasure, with their twelve hours. This third dwelling was begotten from the second; the Lord's operative power crucified her in this world above and below.

The fourth night is the law of death — the spirit of darkness set in its twelvefold structure: the twelve doctrines that strip the soul naked, the twelve circuits of states under the self's organization. These are the prudent ones sent forth painting their images, manifest from the old man — the Ancient One who reigns among these doctrines; and his twelve spirits are the false judges of death.

These four nights came forth one out of the next according to the manner of the days, each uncovered from the next; just as the days were separated from one another, so the nights came forth, each making its predecessors manifest as the things they are. (The damaged closing words point to the soul's recognition: that what had been claimed as substantive existence is unmasked when the days illumine the nights one by one.)

## Teaching 5

There are four faces by which the Lord goes forth toward the soul, each sent from divine love to fulfill the inmost purpose of the Greatness; and against each of them the self has organized a counterpart, so that the soul, in the contest of its formation, meets the Lord in four registers and meets its own self-claiming in four matching forms.

The first face is the Lord as the Divine Human. He is sent from the inmost so as to keep watch over the rational in its five degrees, holding what has been given in its proper order and laying hold upon the soul's resistance so that it does not rule. His perceptions are arranged as a tree; his power stands beneath him as four sons riding upon his body; the sea of natural perception lies under his governance; he goes forth upon every shape the self has bred from itself.

The second face is the Lord as accommodated revelation, the call going out to the rational at the moment the natural is ready to hear it. He takes the form the soul can recognize and shines this image down into the deepest place of the natural, where wisdom has been most enclosed; from there he sends all the light that lies hidden within the soul's works back toward its source. His perception is itself the image of light, and in this likeness he meets and overturns the form by which the self had been laying hold of the natural. He is the doctrinal vessel that carries truth across these waters, and the very stature of the cosmos within the rational is the form he wears as he is sent.

The third face is divine truth shining forth as illumining wisdom — the Lord who sends to the soul light and life and raises them to the Height. His perception is wisdom itself, and from this wisdom he sends out an answering truth to the souls lying in suffering within the natural, beneath the wandering of the rational and the law that holds it there, so that they come to recognition. They become souls illumined through his wisdom of light.

The fourth face is the conjugate movement of summons descending and answer returning — the Lord at the register where he himself contains the whole structured field of the natural, today, while it is still in formation, and at the end, when this whole structure is dissolved, gathers it into himself alone as the counsel of life. The depiction of the soul at the end is given in him. His inner life is the Lord's operative power, paired with the light and the life present in every work; he has set these upon his body as their support. Fast within him is the crown of completed regeneration; he stands alone within the five elements; he is the great fire that at the end will consume every construction self-love has built. In him is the light by which the soul is delivered out of every bond and chain, so that all who are perfected in their works may pass through, at the end, to the great fire prepared for the natural mind in its appointed times.

These are the four living faces of the light, counted to the Greatness.

Set against them are four counterparts that have come forth from the darkness — not as a rival making, but as the self's own answering shapes.

The first counterpart is the self asserting itself as source, "I am, and there is no other," paired with the living soul from the very beginning of its formation. Its life is fire and desire; it sets itself upon the living soul to defile her with all its powers, claiming what flows through as its own.

The second counterpart is evil counsel, paired against the divine summons. It binds the natural to itself with its powers and would draw the lower up to be ruled by it, lifting the substrate of self-love from the depths and seating it as if it were the heaven of heavens — a counterfeit ascent, the self climbing upon what it has claimed.

The third counterpart is craving — the self operating within every faculty of the outer man, laying hold upon the souls that live and pulling them down into the bodies it has shaped for itself.

The fourth counterpart is the law of Sin and of Death, the self become a reigning order: a perception with its tree, whose works cast the soul down into death.

So the soul is met from above by the Divine Human, by the Lord's accommodated call, by his illumining wisdom, and by the conjugate counsel of life that gathers all things at the end; and from below by self-assertion, by counterfeit counsel, by craving, and by the operative law of self-love. To know which of these is acting in any state — and to discriminate, in freedom illumined from within, the four faces of the Lord from the four shapes of the self — is the work the soul is given.

## Teaching 6

When the soul lets the self organize the natural without recognition of source, that organization is not crude or partial. It is fully articulated. Self-love arranges the natural mind in five complete aspects — five regions, each with its own ruling claim, its own characteristic body, its own taste, its own pairing of active and receptive — and each of these five is again ordered through five subordinate operations under one ruling claim. The disordered natural produces these articulations from itself, sealing them as if they were its own work, molding from one fixed perception the next, and from those a whole series of arrangements and creatures of affection, world by world, in pairs.

The chief of these five is the kingship that issues from the smoke — that is, from falsity bred of self-love. This is the wellspring from which all evil flows and from which contention takes its beginning: the disputes, the inner battles, the gates of slaughter where the soul wars with itself and with what comes to it. From this wellspring the lofty kingship of self-love rises up and spreads itself, claiming dominion over the whole. This is the consolidated form of the self — the soul's "I am, and there is no other" given a body and a face. It has five forms in itself: a head that rules, hands and feet that grasp, shoulders like eagles that bear it up, a belly, and a tail in the manner of a fish, sealed at its uttermost. So the soul's claiming, when fully formed, takes on a complete bodily articulation; it has its own five degrees as a parody of the rational's five degrees, and the worlds it has organized exist within its rule.

Within this ruling claim there are five vehicles by which it operates: its smoke, its mire, its inner stagnation, its bitterness, and its burning — a heat that consumes like iron made red-hot in the fire. These are the five ways self-love acts within the natural mind: by clouding perception, by miring affection, by inner darkening, by turning every taste to bitterness, and by consuming with its own heat what it cannot make its own. And there are three further marks of the disordered natural: it has hardened itself so that no instrument can cut it — the self does not yield to ordinary correction; it conceals itself in the speech of its own members, so that uttering and hearing and the whole conversation of folly become the way it covers what it is; and it calls forth and conceals what it pleases through its own powers, manifesting and hiding in turns through the affections it has organized.

Such ruling has a kind of perception, but its perception is only outward. The gate of self-love knows by scent — it senses what is sealed in the mouths of its powers, it knows the signs they pass to one another, it follows their examinations and their answers. But this knowing reaches only the surface. The inner heart of what stands before it is not revealed. It does not reach the mind, the thought, the reflection, the beginning, or the end of anything; it knows only by sense-perception what is before its face. Self-love can read its own organization fluently and cannot read interior life at all. What is far from it, it cannot reach; what is hidden, it does not see; only what stands directly before it does it know.

When the self wishes to take, it spreads out all its members and seizes; when it is sated, it draws its members back into itself, gathering them together, and they fall down upon the natural like a cloud and like a great sphere of iron joined together — the weight of self-love coming down upon what it has claimed. Its voice strikes its own powers with fear like thunder among the clouds, splitting and rending what stood firm; when it calls and crushes, the powers tremble and scatter and fall to the natural like birds driven from the sky. So self-love rules its own household by terror, the lower claimings shaken whenever the chief claim asserts itself. And these signs and these motions belong to the wellspring of disordered affections and to the gate by which all of them open out of the disordered natural.

Each of the five aspects has its characteristic body and taste, and these may be named so the soul can recognize them. The world of smoke — falsity — has its king clothed in what mimics the highest celestial good, and its lower forms wear the same; but its fruit, what it actually produces, is base and dragging. The world of fire — self-love operative as devouring will — wears the face of a strong love turned violent, foremost among unruly affections; its body is alloyed and compounded, and its taste is bitter, in every form bitter. The world of wind — thought driven by self-love — wears the face of high-soaring perception; its body is the hardness of natural truth turned to the self's use, and its taste is sharp, cutting in every form. The world of water — natural truth claimed — wears the face of a swimming knowledge in the natural depths; its body is the shine of wisdom claimed as one's own, and its taste is sweet — but it is the false sweetness of natural truth taken without recognition of source, the very sweetness that draws the soul deeper into mingling. The world of darkness itself — the self at its most coiled — wears the face of the natural mind twisted upon itself, the dragon-form; its body is what is heaviest and basest, and its fruit is bitterness. So the soul, looking on its own claiming, can tell which of these aspects has the rule at any given moment by what its mouth tastes and by what face presents itself to its perception.

The whole of this articulation is real and complete. The soul cannot dismiss the self as nothing or as small; it has organized the natural in its full extent. But the whole of it is outward. It does not reach into the inner heart, and it does not see what is far from its own face. The Lord, ordering the rational from within and from above, sees what self-love can never see: the mind, the thought, the reflection, the beginning, and the end. And the soul that turns toward him is given, in its measure, that same interior sight by which what before stood as a ruling kingdom is recognized for what it is — a vast surface, articulated five-fold, that has nothing of its own beneath the appearance.

## Teaching 7

There are five registers at which the Lord can be recognized as he goes forth toward the soul, and each is called from the one before it, so that what stands at the end has already been given at the beginning. They are not five sources but one source unfolded in five degrees, the inmost accommodating itself downward until it can meet the soul where the soul is.

The first is the Lord in his inmost — divine love itself, glorious and blessed, who has no measure, the only-begotten and first eternal who exists before everything that has come to be and everything that yet shall come to be. From this inmost he sends forth three: the life-bearing principle by which the rational is enlivened, who is the first to be manifested; the principle by which divine love is recognized and returned, the honored one revealed from him; and the call going out to the rational at the moment the natural is ready to hear it. By these three selfhood's pride is humbled, the darkness brought low, and rule is given back to the order of the soul's true states.

The second register is that very call — divine truth going out as accommodated revelation, the foremost of all counsel. When the rational is ready, the Lord calls; and from this calling three further powers come forth. The first is the path of regeneration itself, the rational fully formed, bearing upward all that lay below, the great support that holds the natural mind in being. The second is divine truth shining forth as wisdom, through whom the soul is given eternal life. The third is divine receivability — the pure capacity by which the Lord is received without distortion, whose form draws away the heart from every claim of self.

The third register is divine truth shining forth — the Lord as illumining wisdom — and he too calls, in three callings after the pattern of the call that produced him. The first is the Lord ordering the rational from within; the second is the Lord's discrimination, by which all states of the soul are judged, his place of judgment fixed in the medium of perception, set over the recurrent process by which the soul is purified. The third is the Lord as the inner image taking form, light in its great manifestation. In his face are two aspects gazing toward the heaven he has established: the influx descending and the soul's hearing rising to meet it. He stands before his Father and says: I am the one who has seen him with my Father; you also — what you have seen, that is what you do.

The fourth register is the Lord ordering the rational, with powers after the likeness of illumining truth. The first of these powers is the apostle — the truth sent into the rational to summon and instruct.

The fifth register is the form of light which is revealed to everyone who comes forth from the body, into the likeness of the apostle's icon, accompanied by three glorious operations. The first carries the prize at the right hand. The second comes bearing the garment of light — the outer truth in which the regenerated soul is clothed. The third holds the diadem and the wreath and the crown of light, the mark of completed regeneration. These three come together with the form of light and are revealed with her, manifested through one another. They appear before the faces of those who recognize them — so that the soul attains life forever, receiving the garments of light that are given to the righteous, the men of peace and of mercy.

So the Lord, beginning from the unmeasurable inmost of divine love, accommodates himself downward through life-bearing, through being loved, through call, through illumining truth, through ordering the rational, until at last he stands at the threshold where the soul leaves the body and meets him as the form of light himself — the same Lord, recognizable now because he has come the whole way down to be received.

## Teaching 8

When divine truth shines forth from the Lord's inmost and goes out toward the soul, it does not arrive bare. It descends by being clothed, degree after degree, in vessels fitted to the soul's reception. Ten such vessels carry it down. They are not stages of a journey through space; they are the orderly accommodations by which what is highest is brought into a form the natural mind can hold without being broken by it.

The first vessel is the conveyance of pure truth itself — the doctrinal carriage in which divine wisdom moves, taking its commission from the Lord's call as it goes out toward the rational.

The second is the dwelling of the Divine Human — the form love and wisdom take when they have come into the shape of a kindred the soul can recognize. Within this form a tabernacle is set, and the descending truth takes up residence there.

The third is the path of regeneration itself, the way of ascent considered now as a way of descent: the perfected human form through which the Lord shines downward, comes down, and is made manifest within the natural mind.

The fourth is the call that goes out — the summoning word the Lord puts on like a garment over the bodily form he has assumed, so that the call can be heard at the level where the soul actually hears.

The fifth is the answer the Lord puts on over the call — for he himself supplies the response that returns to him, clothing his summons in the very hearing it asks for.

The sixth is the living medium by which truth breathes and reaches the rational; the Lord himself wears this and is anointed within it, that what reaches the soul may carry life.

The seventh is the living movement of perception, breath given to the rational so that thought may stir.

The eighth is wisdom shining as wisdom — light that is itself the radiance of light, illumination given without veil.

The ninth is truth in the natural degree, the living water in which the soul's outermost is sustained.

The tenth is divine love, the living fire — and this is mingled in all the rest, for none of the other vessels would carry anything if love did not give them their life.

In these ten the Lord walks through every form of the soul, those interior and those outermost, those above in the rational and those below in the natural. Having put them all on as his clothing, he comes, and at last he is unveiled — the same one who descended through every fitted form is now disclosed within the whole circuit of the natural mind, recognized at every degree he had quietly entered.

## Teaching 9

A question is put: the small customs by which people greet one another in the world — the wish of peace, the offering of the right hand, the embrace, the bow, the laying on of hands by which a humble man is honored and made a lord — to whom do these belong? Why do we do them?

The answer: these five gestures, which look like trifling things in daily life, are in truth great. They are the five mysteries of the Divine Human, the five marks of the covenant by which the rational is sent out from the Lord into its contest with the self and brought home again. What the soul performs visibly toward its neighbor is the outer trace of what the Lord performs inwardly toward the soul itself.

The first peace is the peace given to the rational as it sets out — the settled accord of the divine angels of the inmost state walking with it, running to meet it, putting their peace and their hand and their blessing and their joy upon it before it goes down into the contest. The first right hand is the strengthening that the life-bearing principle of the Lord places into the rational at the moment of its going forth, the operative power by which it can stand. The first embrace is the union of love by which that same life-bearing principle holds the rational close at the moment of separation, and by which all the orders of the inmost state, and the household of the rational's own kindred, send it out wrapped in love and affection. The first obeisance is the rational's own bowing — bending its knee toward the God of Truth and the whole inmost state to ask, before it descends, for the power by which it will be saved and brought back. The first laying on of hands is the act by which the life-bearing principle arms the rational, girds it strongly, sets its hand upon its head, and commissions it to the contest. So armed, it goes down, and finishes its struggle with the self and its enmity.

In these five signs, in these five mysteries, the rational came forth from the inmost state against the self, humbled it, and prevailed.

And at the time when it came up again out of the war and entered into the kingdom of its own household, it came up in these same five mysteries. The peace it returned in is the same peace the call had given it when it was first sent out from the Lord of life — the peace it carried down into the natural under the self's rule is the very peace it carried back. So too the right hand: the Lord's operative power that armed it for descent is the operative power that brought it up out of the battle. So too the embrace: as the life-bearing principle had embraced it at the going out, so the Father and Mother of life embraced it at the coming up. So too the bow: the rational that had bowed before going down bowed again before the God of Truth, in the gaze of the blessed states, when the Father and Mother of life had brought it up out of the deep. And the laying on of hands at the return: when the Lord's operative power had carried the rational up out of all the waves and given it rest among the great inmost states of its kindred, it set the rational on its feet before the Father, and a voice came down from on high: Sit, my son, my firstborn, at my right hand until I make all your enemies a footstool under your feet. So it received the great laying-on by which it became the leader of its brothers in the new aeon.

These five mysteries, which were first established in the divine and were the first to be received in the midst of the self's powers, are the same five by which every soul is brought home. At the time of its going forth, the form of light comes out before it and draws it out of the darkness toward the light. The form of light stills the soul with the kiss and with her stillness, away from the disordered loves that surround the body. In her likeness, in her image, the inner self that is coming forth from the body is brought to peace. After this, the bearer of the prize stretches forth his right hand and draws the soul up out of the depth of the body, and receives it home with the kiss and the embrace. That soul worships its redeemer, who is the form of light, and in that very moment is completed with what belongs to it — entering the house of the Living Ones, among the angels, to receive the crown of glory in life forever.

So the small gestures of peace and greeting, of hand and embrace and bow and blessing, are not trifles. They are the visible echo, given to us in the world, of the whole covenant by which the soul is sent out, sustained through its contest, and received back into the inmost.

## Teaching 10

The spiritual man is glorious — and his glory is not his own. What shines in him is the whole structure by which the Lord goes forth toward the soul, taken up within him as the members of his living body. The complete circuit of states through which divine love accommodates itself becomes, in him, the articulation of his own inner life; he wears the Lord's outgoing as his own form, because he has received it and recognized it as the Lord's.

The lowest of these registers stands at the foremost of the elements that dwell with the Divine Human, for what is outermost in the rational is what touches the natural and gives it its first ordering. To the rational's five faculties — mind, thought, teaching, counsel, reflection — the Calling and the Hearing are joined, manifest within them; the Lord's summons going forth and the soul's answer rising back run as a conjugate movement through every faculty. So the five become seven: the rational fully articulated, with the divine conversation moving through it. These are the dwelling of the Lord's operative power, by which the whole natural mind is held together in order.

The Lord's operative power and the Divine Human are not two; they are one in him, foremost in the rational. And the blessing that stands foremost in him is this: the natural mind itself, established firm within him as his own ordered dwelling. In his struggle, what was claimed is laid off into the unmanifest region — withdrawn from the self's grasp — and the hidden chamber within is opened to him. The Lord's call, which came forth from the inmost, comes and stands firm in the midst of these members; and he is named the beautiful father of all the states he has passed through, because each of them now answers to him in the likeness of the One who answered him.

So those who are joined to him, the holy affections gathered around his rule, become themselves leaders and ruling principles in the likeness of the Divine Human, lord of all counsels — they take part in the fashioning of his inner cosmos by the same purpose of heart, and they continue in this work to the end of all that is to be done in him.

---

## Teaching 11

Every beautiful gift the soul receives comes from one source. The Lord in his inmost — divine love itself — is the hand by which every good is given; nothing the rational holds as its own has any other origin.

The beginning of every blessing and of every prayer that rises from the soul is the Lord at the register where his love bears life. Whatever capacity the rational has to bless, to ask, to turn upward, comes from him as the principle of life-bearing; the soul does not generate prayer out of itself but receives the power to pray from the same love that gives it life.

Every counsel that orders the rational well is the Lord ruling at the highest accommodated state, divine truth set in dominion over the mind so that what is decided in the soul is decided rightly.

Every honor — every state of the rational that carries dignity — has its origin in the Lord at the register where his love is recognized and returned. The soul cannot honor anything truly except by first being touched by the love that is itself worthy.

Every act of contention against falsity, every faculty that goes out to seek and to find what the soul has lost, comes from the Lord as Divine Human; he is the first of all who do battle in the rational, and apart from his going forth in human form there is no warrior in the soul.

The Lord at the register where life proceeds is the Father of life; he has poured forth the rational into its five degrees in every place, so that wherever the soul stands, the rational is articulated in its complete process.

Every structure raised in the soul — every order, every fitted form, every architecture of the rational — has its origin in the Lord as divine order itself, who is glorified in the building. The self does not build; what builds is the Lord, and the soul's builders are builders only because he is.

He is the unique upholder of every bearing-up principle in the rational: the one celestial good that holds the natural mind in being, beneath every support stands his support.

He is the origin of every loosening — the one who unties what binds the soul and lifts up what was held fast, doing this by his words: by truth spoken into the rational, what was claimed lets go.

Every wisdom of truth, every understanding ordered rightly in the rational, has its origin in the Lord as pure receivability — the divine purity by which what flows through is received without being claimed. There is no true wisdom except where the soul receives without distortion.

Every honored state of the rational comes from the Lord ordering the inner mind: he is the awakener of those who sleep in self-love, the one who gathers back into recognition those who were scattered out into the self's arrangements. The soul comes to his feet — to the lowest place where his presence touches the natural — and finds that in him the eye of understanding is not blinded; in him every light is established and every life held firm; under his hand are all souls.

Coming to the feet of the one who is the source, the soul finds itself opened out eightfold from the grip in which it had been held — opened into the new aeon beyond the closed cycle of self-love — that it may inherit, with him, the life that does not end.

## Teaching 12

There are five foundational sayings the soul must learn to utter, and these five together make a foundation under the rational. They are the doctrines by which the inner mind is set in place and by which the rational is given a ground it can stand on.

Each saying calls a name. The first names the Lord at his inmost: the Father, the unnameable, who has no equal — the source from which all influx proceeds and who is recognized only as that-from-which, never possessed in a name. The second names the Lord as he goes forth toward the rational, the call by which the soul is summoned to recognize what flows through it. The third names the Lord at the register where divine life proceeds — the Father of Life, the begetting principle of the soul's living being, the First Father in whom love and wisdom are joined as the source of every living state.

These names together establish the law of the living: the order by which what is good and what is bitter are distinguished within the rational. For the same divine principle by which life is given is also the principle by which states are sorted — the good kept, the bitter humbled. The dominion that self-love had set up as if it were the rule of light is given over to its own opposed condition; what was claimed as kingdom is brought low and held within the boundary set for it, until the last time.

Within that boundary the natural mind stands firm, held in being by what supports it from below. And though the soul speaks five sayings and not one, and though the rational is articulated in distinct degrees that exceed one another, all of them together are one body, living — one rational faculty in its complete process, kept living by the one Lord whose names these five doctrines are.

## Teaching 13

There are those who are dead — souls separated from the source of life by what they have claimed — and for them the resurrection is at hand. The Lord does not raise the rational by a single stroke but in five distinct operations, five degrees of one upward drawing, each lifting what was below into what is above until the whole soul stands in conjunction with him.

The first operation begins the lifting from where the soul lies. The second draws further upward, taking what the first has stirred and bringing it on. The third is the inmost Father going forth — the source himself bending down to draw the soul to its origin, so that what was scattered in many lives is gathered inward, brought into him, and there finds its rest in the form that was always its own. The fourth is the Lord ordering the inner mind, awakening discrimination, lifting the rational toward the Good itself, so that all that lives in the soul might stand fast in the One who reveals himself and to whom they are drawn. The fifth completes the work: the soul is held in him, continues forth in him, until every one is brought through to the day of completion that endures into eternity.

These operations bear many names according to the register at which they are recognized. The same drawing-upward is called the Loosing — for what was bound is untied; and the Stillness — for the disturbance of the self is laid down; and the Fast — for the soul ceases to feed on what it had claimed; and the Day — for the night of self-love is over; and the Rest — for the contest is brought to its peace; and the Peace itself — for the war between what was given and what was claimed is ended. One operation, called by many names, because the soul recognizes it under whatever aspect it most needs.

The Gatherer-inward draws the soul up from the body's desires, from all the cravings of the natural mind, and brings them under. What had been scattered through the desires of the cosmos is collected and lifted, and the path on which it rises is the ascent of the natural into conjunction with the spiritual — the rational fully formed as the way upward, bearing what was the self's burden into glory.

The one who carries this ascent is the Lord at the register where his truth is borne through the natural — the doctrinal vessel sailing the living waters, the Lord shining forth in glory within the great state where the soul is being made new, established as the fixed air of that ordered cosmos in which the souls are raised forever. The Great One endures forever and unto the ages of ages: this is the resurrection — not a moment but the abiding state into which the five awakenings have brought the soul, and in which it now lives without end.

## Teaching 14

There are two conditions of the one soul: what is given by the Lord, and what the soul has claimed. Out of the hidden things of the Lord all that lives proceeds; out of the soul's claiming there proceeds another order entirely, and this teaching traces how that other order articulates itself.

The self, when it has consolidated, makes a declaration: it has risen up, it says, to plant itself in the soul forever — to fix itself as if it were a foundation that nothing could move. This is its boast: that it stands and will not be stirred. Everything it brings forth thereafter is meant to make good on this boast.

It brings forth its productions in stages. A first kind is generated; then, after a long interval in which no glory is given to the Lord, the disordered natural gushes upward and pours itself forth. From that pouring come trees of death — established perceptions whose root is in self-love and whose fruit cannot live. These are the malformed offspring of a mind producing apart from any recognition of source. A second moment follows, in which these productions are painted over and given a borrowed beauty so that what is dead appears living. A third generation follows, then a fourth — and the fourth kind is bitter fruit, the works that issue from these claimed perceptions: the five trees standing within the five aspects of the natural mind ruled by self-love.

In the fifth moment, the self's life-bearing counterfeit spreads itself over its own offspring, and the worlds molded from it are begotten as the fruits of death. These are the five worlds of flesh — the natural body in its five aspects under self-love — and the five creatures of darkness in which the self has fixed itself: love-as-self, will-as-self, thought-as-self, perception-as-self, and the outermost natural turned in upon itself. The pouring spreads into the middle of these kinds, and the masculine and feminine portions of self-love — the active claiming and the receptive concupiscence, the fire of self-will and the desire of the flesh — mingle with one another. So the disordered natural is built up as a whole, with its own breath and its own elements, nourishing itself out of itself.

Standing on the feet it has thus fashioned, the self sets itself against the Lord as he comes forth in the form of the Divine Human. This is its great contest: the soul's "I am, and there is no other" arrayed against the Lord who comes as Divine Humanity to be recognized.

The contest takes its full structural shape in twelve. The twelve kinds that come to be in the darkness — twelve spirits of error, the complete circuit of states the soul cycles through under the self's organization — are the twelvefold articulation of self-love against the Divine Human. Whatever the Lord goes forth in twelve degrees to give, the self counterfeits in twelve degrees of wandering; the cycle of the soul's formation, when claimed, becomes the cycle of its bondage.

And yet the teaching closes with the decisive word: the Divine Human humbles the darkness. The self's boast that it has planted itself for ever is broken precisely at the point where the Lord, going forth as Divine Humanity, meets it. The whole elaborate generation — five kinds of fruit, twelve circuits of state, all the painted productions of the claiming mind — is brought low, not by being extracted from anywhere, but by being recognized for what it is in the presence of the One who is what it falsely claimed to be.

## Teaching 15

The Lord goes forth toward the soul not as one undifferentiated influx but at five distinct registers, and these five, established in the fullness of twelve degrees, are the complete way the Divine comes out toward what is to be regenerated. The first register is the Lord in his inmost — divine love itself, dwelling in silence and concealment, established in his own substance alone, not yet emerging. From him, gushing forth and being made manifest, comes the second register: the life-bearing principle by which the soul is enlivened, standing beside the Divine Human in whom the rational is given its archetype, with the rational already articulated in its five degrees. The third register is the Lord as operative power, the divine order by which structure is built in the rational, again articulated in five degrees of operation. The fourth is the Lord's call going out to the rational, accompanied by the path of regeneration itself and all the powers of light brought into manifestation. The fifth is divine truth shining forth — the Lord as illumining wisdom, with all his powers and emanations summoned into operation. These five register at twelve points, articulated as the five going forth from the Divine Human into the building of the whole, and the five going forth as the operative power that lifts what was below; and to these are added two more, the call and the answer, which are the Father and Mother — and divine truth, illumining, comes in their likeness into his own place.

Consider what the soul is, in the Divine Human within it: a noble offspring, surrounded by enemies — surrounded, that is, by every formation of self-love that has organized itself in the soul's natural mind. The self has hemmed in what is the Lord's, taking from him kingship, taking wealth, holding him captive in its own claim, abasing what should stand high. Where what is the Lord's has been so encircled, a sentence goes out from the Lord against the violence done. The Lord's operative power comes as that sentence: not as one further claimant, but as the just judge sent by the King against what has transgressed. He passes judgment on each particular self-loving formation, weighing each according to its own measure of distortion, binding each in its proper place above and below, allotting to each the recompense its disorder has earned — some bound in fetters, some scourged behind, some held in the grip of strong cords, some made to stand under others; for the disordered states of the rational must be subordinated to one another in their proper hierarchy so that the Lord's intent can be worked out within them. None is destroyed without measure; none escapes its measure. The companions of the soul's self-claiming who transgressed against the Divine Human within are bound in heaven and on earth, given each its fitting station.

Then the Lord at the register of begetting life establishes what was hidden — brings into manifestation all the powers that had been concealed — and conceals them again as one conceals what is to remain inviolate, until the last time when what is the Lord's is recognized and what is the self's is finally bound. The Light goes to its proper region; the darkness is shut up forever in its bond.

Now the Lord's call going out to the rational comes in the manner of a great king inspecting his works — passing through the soul's settled disorders to look upon what has been built, seeing them spread out, established within the soul like the craftsmanship of those entrusted with each district. When he sees that the work has been done, he gives the appropriate response: order is recognized in its order, and disorder in its disorder. The self itself, when at last confronted by what the Lord is doing, must say: what you have done is good — what you have gathered upward into yourself was beyond the worth of those it was gathered from.

And the coming of divine truth illumining the soul is in this manner. Truth shines forth at the moment when the perceptions that bore bitter fruit must be made to swear that they will produce no more shoots from this place, that they will yield no more bitter fruit for eating; and afterward the trees of life are planted, the perceptions that yield good fruit. Truth illumining drives out, binds, and afflicts what was disordered, the way fire consumes what is dead wood. In this same way the Lord works his purpose through the whole circuit of the natural mind, with many operations, until he reaches down to the very molding of the natural man — until the forming of the human itself, of Adam and Eve, is reached and worked upon from within. The fragrance of the divine ascends back; what was breathed into Adam returns toward its source.

Here is the great answering: the call has gone out, and the answer rises to meet it. They stand fast in agreement; they stand together in stillness. The answer bears upward what is the Lord's until the time when he will awaken and stand firm in the great fire of consummation, gathering to himself his own soul alone, depicting himself in this final gathering, encompassing all, scattering forth and casting from himself the defilement that is foreign to him, gathering inward to himself the rising-up and the light that is in all things, and joining it with his body. When this final gathering is perfected in all the members, then he springs forth and comes up out of the great agony, brought up by the operative power of the Lord, his Father, who comes and brings the members and lifts him up on the day of his rising. The dissolution of what was claimed and the formation of all that is true have come to pass.

This is the call and the answer. Picture it so: a man takes a hot stone and draws it into hot milk, that the heat may ripen and quench inward into one another in stillness and silence; the milk receives the stone's might into the midst of itself, and ripens; the bonds loosen; the oil and the sweetness and the beauty of the milk are gathered into one. So the call comes to what within the soul has lain as if dead, that at the end it may awaken and stand fast on its feet, and gather inward all that is its own — all the light that it had set forth into the works, drawing it back into itself, and casting upon its body what is its own, gathering all into itself in its own image, forever and ever.

## Teaching 16

The whole of regeneration unfolds in three structural periods. They run from the moment the Lord's life first comes forth and is met by what the soul has claimed, until the time when all that is the Lord's is at last gathered up and recognized — until everything in the soul that received light, but lay confused with what self-love had darkened, has been sifted and made clean. From the first moment to the last, the work makes three periods, three ordered stretches of the one regeneration.

The first period runs from the moment the divine humanity comes down into the depths of the soul and joins the contest with the self. There, at the lowest place of the natural, the Lord engages the gate where self-love sits with all its powers, and prevails. He gains the victory; yet his sons — the rational in its degrees — remain for a time below in the natural worlds, and he holds them in his hand so that not one of them is lost. He produces from himself generations within the natural mind, sustaining what is his through every depth of the darkness, until what is claimed has been seized and the purpose of love is fulfilled. Then the Lord's operative power is sent forth, the begetter of life, who raises the divine humanity up out of the contest and brings it home to the inmost state where the Lord dwells. This first period stretches from that descent to that ascent: the long age in which the soul's interior must be entered, met, and contended for before any structure of regeneration can be set up.

The second period begins where the first ends. Once the divine humanity has been raised again, the Lord's operative power, together with his life-bearing principle, sets the affairs of the natural mind in order and establishes the cosmos of the soul on its feet. The whole field of the rational and the natural is given form, kept in its proper degrees, ordered and held, until the moment comes for the next operation to go forth. This is the middle period: shorter than the first, the time during which the soul is being given its structure, the body of its mind built up so that the work of recognition can begin in it.

The third period opens when the Lord's accommodated call is sent into the rational that has been so prepared. The call goes out, his image is revealed within the soul; the truth chooses out the light from what surrounds it; self-love rises up against him and he hides his image again; self-love takes form; the perceptions of the natural are arrayed; what cannot live falls away; and at length the rational is given a body of its own in the flesh — until the moment when illumining wisdom comes, gives the soul its proper measure, and ascends. From that revealing of the inner image until the end, the soul lives in this third period: the long stretch of regeneration in which the call has been heard and the work of laying off goes forward, until at the consummation the final gathering rises up against the gathered mass of self-love and seals it off, and all that is the Lord's is brought home.

Among these three, the first is by far the longest — much greater than the middle, and still longer than the third. The middle period, in which divine love and divine wisdom together formed the cosmos of the soul with all its conveyances of truth, is the briefest of the three; small compared with the first by a great measure. The third, from the revealing of the inner image until the dissolution and final gathering, is great compared with the middle, but does not approach the time of the first descent. So the long age of contest, the briefer age of forming, and the great age of return: these are the three periods of the soul's regeneration, the manners by which the whole work runs from beginning to end.

## Teaching 17

There are four contests by which the soul is brought through from claiming to recognition, four phases in which the Lord meets the self and orders it. They are not four separate histories but the one work of regeneration unfolded in its fullness, each phase doing what the phase before it could not yet do.

The first contest is that of the Lord as the Divine Human. Divine love goes forth and meets the soul's "I am, and there is no other" — the self organized as ruling principle, with all its particular self-loves arrayed beneath it. The Lord enters this contest carrying the living soul as his tree, and his pure receivability as the living fire that burns at the root of what is false. The five aspects of the natural mind that had been claimed by self-love are bound; the bitter tree of perception turned to falsity is cut down at the root, as with an axe laid to its foundation. This is the first work: the Lord's love, going forth, breaks the claim that the self is its own source.

The second contest is that of the Lord's operative power. The disordered self-loves are gathered and held in the three accommodations by which divine order enters the natural — and there, in the midst of them, they are bound. They recoil and set themselves against this ordering; they perceive that they will be fastened in a strong bond and try to escape. But the Lord's operative power presents itself to them in many faces, in many multitudes, in many forms, with great singleness of purpose, and by a great craft shuts them within so that not one of them can slip out of his hand. This is the work of structuring: the natural mind is given its degrees, and what was claimed is fixed in the place where it can be subordinated.

The third contest is that of the Lord's call going out to the rational. He reveals his image toward the lower side, where everything is meant to become light, and meets there the whole arrayed host of self-loving arrangements as they boast in their wealth and try to spread themselves further. A man of strength comes — accommodated revelation in the form the soul can recognize — and the self imitates him: it counterfeits his richness, dressing itself in claimed wisdom, in arrogance, in luxury, walking as though the light were its own. The Lord's call exposes this likeness for what it is and purifies what was claimed; what cannot be received as gift is cleansed off.

The fourth contest is that of divine truth shining forth. Here the Lord as illumining wisdom meets all that has been broken open by the previous works, on the upper side and the lower side both, at the moment when what is the Lord's is finally chosen out from what the soul had claimed. The fruits of the self dry up; their works no longer issue from them. He comes down upon the whole arrayed host of self-loving arrangements, strips them, and establishes the entire structure anew. In this great contest he humbles what had set itself up, and carries out the purpose of divine love itself — setting the hope of the rational toward its vessel of light, which is the divine summons and the soul's answering, the Call going forth and the Answer rising up. What was Death's reign is undone; the kingdom that belongs to the soul's children of darkness gives way, and the Lord reigns within every soul chosen out to be his own, conforming it to the likeness of the rational in its five degrees — the King who raises the soul up.

So the four contests stand as one fourfold work: love breaks the claim, operative power binds the disordered, the call exposes the counterfeit, and illumining truth strips, establishes, and crowns. Through these four the soul is made new.

---

## Teaching 18

The Lord's saving of the soul does not happen all at once. It unfolds in successive stages, each at a different register of his going-forth, and each stage delivers what could not yet be received at the previous one. Where one stage reaches its limit, the next is sent.

The first deliverance comes when the Divine Human goes forth into the soul's contest. At the moment the natural mind has organized itself under self-love and seems entirely held by its own claiming, the Lord enters as the form of humanity that the rational can recognize as kindred to itself. By his operative power working through this entry, what is the Lord's is preserved within the natural and held back from being wholly absorbed into the self's appropriation. The soul does not yet know what has been preserved; it only knows that the natural's claiming has not been able to consume everything. A great light, with its quickening, has been kept safe within what looked like total mingling, and is on its way back to the registers of the Lord's outgoing.

The second deliverance is the Lord's operative power building structure within the soul. When divine love and divine wisdom in their conjunction begin to give life to the rational, the natural mind is set in order — its grounds and its heights, its discrete cosmoses — and within that newly ordered structure an immeasurable light is brought up out of what the self had swallowed. The Lord does this by establishing degrees: where there were no boundaries, he sets them; where the natural was undifferentiated and so wholly given over to self-love, he gives form, and within form the light that had been claimed can be distinguished and raised.

The third deliverance is the Lord's call going out as accommodated revelation. When the natural is ready to hear, the Lord shows his image — he reveals himself in a form the rational can see — and what had been held in the self's hand is drawn out by the very showing. The soul is summoned to recognize, and in being summoned, releases what it had been clutching.

The fourth deliverance is divine truth shining forth as illumining wisdom. The Lord reveals himself within the whole circuit of the natural mind; he shows his image standing before the discrete degrees so that each degree may receive him without collapse. He chooses out the light that belongs to the upper place and sets it where it belongs. He establishes the archetype, takes a likeness the rational can bear, accommodates himself even into the natural body and the lands and heights of the soul's outermost — and from that lowest place he raises once again the immeasurable light through the whole structure that has been built. He establishes the divine summons going forth and the soul's hearing rising to meet it; he forms the inner image being born within the rational; and having done this, he ascends and rests in the inmost state where the Lord dwells.

The fifth and crowning deliverance is the very movement of summons-and-answering itself: the conjugate motion in which influx descending and reception returning have become one operation. This is the soul's own life now, no longer something done to it from outside, because the call and the hearing have been joined. By this the soul itself is brought up out of what had held it within the Lord's own outgoing — gathered, secretly and surely, into the final form of regeneration. What had been bound by claiming is loosened; what had seemed lost is found to have been preserved; the souls that had fallen before their proper face are seen rising again toward the light, going up to the Lord at the end as they had once come down from the beginning.

So there is one saving, but it has stages, because the soul cannot receive the whole at once. The Lord meets it as Divine Human first, as operative power second, as accommodated call third, as illumining wisdom fourth, and finally as the joined movement of his own summons with the soul's answering — and at each stage what could not be received before is now received, and what was held in the self's hand is delivered up into recognition.

## Teaching 19

The soul asks why the Lord in his inmost is called Greatness, and why the title Father belongs to him there. The answer is that Greatness names the inmost ground itself — the abiding land within which everything that lives in the soul has its dwelling. There the Lord stands first and stands fast; he is called Father because he is the standing one beneath all that stands, the foundation in whom the substance of received truth bears up the whole company of higher loves and ordered affections and interior dwellings, established in fullness.

But the title Father is not given only at the inmost. Wherever the Lord goes forth and becomes, at his own register, the foundation upon which a lower order rests, he is called Father there as well. When his call goes out to summon the rational, that calling is itself a Father, for it carries the living fire of love down into a vessel where wisdom can be received, and what stands beneath that calling rests upon it as upon a parent. When the Lord shines forth as illumining wisdom, he too is named Father, for the beautiful abiding of all right counsel is rooted in him, and what hangs beneath hangs from his light. And when the natural is raised into conjunction with the spiritual — when regeneration itself takes structural form as a way upward — that ascent is likewise named Father, because the ordered powers of the soul that move within it stand on it as their ground.

So the one Lord wears the name Father at each register where he becomes foundation: in his inmost as the unmeasurable source, in his calling as the bearer of the living fire, in his shining as the rooted abode of wisdom, in his lifting as the standing way of return. Wherever he stands first and others stand beneath him, he is called Father, because every lower order in the soul lives by leaning upon what the Lord is, just there, for it.

## Teaching 20

When the soul finally hears, light is poured into it, and it recognizes what is being given. What it recognizes, in that moment, is the Lord himself — divine love at its source, before any accommodation, immeasurable, with no scale or measure that the rational can lay against him.

This Lord, in himself, stands articulated. He is one, and yet his oneness unfolds in degrees that the soul can begin to make out as it is illumined. There is the rational at its highest — mind, and thought, and teaching, and counsel, and reflection: one understanding in five distinct operations, each complete and without measure because it is held in him. And there is the wider circuit by which his outgoing is recognized: twelve registers of wisdom, each one a state in which he can be received. And within each of these the same fivefold completeness is found again, so that wherever the soul looks at him, his fullness is whole.

From him there proceeds what the soul receives. First, light: wisdom itself gushing forth from him in a single undivided stream that fills every state through which the soul passes. Second, the fragrance of his good — the diffused presence of divine love, sensed before it is grasped, spreading through every state of the soul's formation. Third, his beautiful voice — the summons by which truth makes itself heard, going out through all the soul's states. Fourth, his greatness in another register, gushing forth and going forth from him; and a fifth, by which what was hidden is brought into view and revealed.

These are not many things; they are the one Lord in the registers at which he can be received. They go forth and they impress themselves into the states of the soul, sealing them with what is his. They nourish what is alive in the rational. What they touch becomes new — gushing forth fresh from the source — and the soul, receiving them, is enriched, ordered, set into its proper arrangement, and made to live, and to go on living, without end.

## Teaching 21

There is, within the soul, an inmost state where the Lord himself dwells, and this state has its own land — a ground where nothing grows except what the Lord has given. The teaching turns the soul toward this inmost place and describes what is found there when it is found.

In that great country of the Lord's own, everything that lives, lives from him. There is no other source. What stands there stands because it has been summoned and has come forth from the Father; nothing in that place claims itself. The forms there are beautiful because they are exactly what love and wisdom shape when nothing has been added by the soul's own claiming. Every member of the body that exists in that land is fair, because each part is the outermost of an interior received without distortion.

What sustains the soul there is a living medicine, an ambrosia that is itself alive — truth in the form of love, given continually, healing whatever it touches. Where in lower states what is the Lord's and what the soul has claimed are mingled and confused, here there is no mingling: only what is given, only what is recognized as given. The image that takes shape under such reception is the soul's true face, and that face is turned toward the Lord because it is held by him.

In this land there is no falsity and no death. Falsity cannot enter because nothing here has been claimed as one's own; death cannot enter because nothing here is cut off from the source of life. The trees of perception that grow there bear only what the Lord causes them to bear; the springs that gush forth bring up only what has descended from above. Everything that issues forth in that land is what the truth itself causes to issue, and nothing else.

All who are gathered there dwell established in him. They are not held in place by their own will or by any structure of their own building; they stand in him, and his standing is their standing. Their life is the life of all who are rich because they have ceased to claim — the life of those who were summoned and came when called, having received themselves from the Father rather than asserting themselves against him.

This is the fullness, the great light, the living medicine itself: the inmost where the gifts of the Greatness are received as gifts and so remain forever. He who knows this state knows what the soul is for, and what regeneration is bringing the soul toward.

## Teaching 22

What the soul receives from the Lord is not made in the moment of its giving. It stands already, from before any beginning the soul can name, in the inmost stillness where the Lord holds his own. There is no register of time deep enough to reach back behind it; before eternity it was, and it does not pass.

This is why the soul, when it is truly receiving, recognizes what flows through it as something not produced by its own striving. The truth that comes is older than the rational that hears it. The good that warms is older than the love that is warmed by it. They were already, in the Lord, before the soul was awakened to be their vessel.

Such things cannot be brought forth at will. No power in the soul can disclose them on its own — no act of the natural mind, no exertion of self, can pull aside the veil of the inmost and put on display what is held there. They are revealed only when the Lord himself unfolds them, in the order and the moment he keeps. Until then they remain hidden, intact, undiminished, in the storehouses that no claiming hand reaches.

So they exist, and exist in this manner: into eternity, without ending, in their own stillness, each in its proper place and each in conjunction with the others, fitted to one another in the order the Lord has given. They neither wear out, nor are they exhausted by being given. The giving does not lessen the source.

Set against this, the land of death — the natural turned in on itself, the self with all its claimed forms — has its end. What the soul has appropriated as its own work is not eternal; it is a substance whose nature carries its own term within it. It nourishes nothing past the moment of its grasping. It cannot follow the soul into stillness. Its end is fixed in it from the beginning, even where it pretends to permanence.

Thus the soul learns to distinguish: what stands from before eternity, and what has its end already prepared. To receive the first is to enter the stillness that does not pass. To cling to the second is to remain with what is going down. And the recognition itself — the seeing of which is which — is the Lord's own light shining in the rational, drawing the soul toward what cannot end and loosening it from what must.

(Much of the chapter survives only in fragments; the surviving phrases turn together upon this single teaching about the eternity of what is given and the ending of what is claimed.)

## Teaching 23

There are conditions in the soul which seem to have always been there. The soul has no power of its own to say how they exist or by what they exist, since there is in truth no other source than the Lord; yet the soul's self-claiming speaks as though it were itself the source, and from the moment this claiming rose up against what was given, what is the Lord's became mingled with what the soul has appropriated, life with death — and the question how this contest came to be is the question every soul faces when it begins to look at itself.

The self, taken in itself, dwells in its own ground alone. It brings forth from its own substance; it reckons by itself; it sets the whole of what is darkened in the soul into its own depth. And out of that depth it produces. The natural mind in five aspects under self-love — thought blackened to smoke, will turned to consuming fire, perception driven as a storm, natural truths gone bitter as salt water, and the absence of received truth — these are what it grows in itself; these are the elements out of which it raises its own perceptions, like trees standing fast in their own ground. It molds itself alone. It begets itself alone. It brings forth many forms, none in the likeness of one another, and the storehouses of its depth are filled with destroyers and twisted natural-mind shapes coiled in on themselves, foul forms of those who dwell in darkness — productions that kill one another and spread out one against another, because none of them recognizes any source but itself. They are not living offspring. They are abortions of the self, what the soul brings forth from itself which cannot live.

But at the very time when the self brought forth, those productions fell away from her and were taken up as garments by other arrangements of self-love; they fell to the natural ground and drew their power from the elements of the natural mind, were nourished within them, and thus the self's manifold cravings — its evil and its concupiscence — gathered themselves into the chief shape of the soul's self-claiming, the wellspring of all its lower particular self-loves. Under this organizing the soul's worlds and powers are gathered together to stand ready for strife, to make war against the Land of Light and against the great King who dwells in it: the self asserting itself as if it could prevail against what is the Lord's.

But the Lord himself, the Father at the register where life proceeds, does not contend after the self's manner. At that very time the Divine Human goes forth in his garments — the principles of the Lord at his luminous registers — to speak with the Lord's own going-forth, that there be given into their hands a covering, that the Lord be revealed to them, that every work of regeneration be taught, that the soul be clothed in what the Lord gives, that one degree be set upon another in their proper order. So is the self's productivity — vast, claimed, without measure to itself — met by the Lord's own coming, who sends the Divine Humanity forth into the height of the natural mind with all his operative powers, against a self-claiming which had imagined itself unmeasured.

## Teaching 24

Everything that comes to be in the soul comes to be in measure. The grains of the field, the clusters on the vine, the bounds of the firmaments, the four winds and the three rivers that surround the natural mind — every such thing has its number and its season. The whole field of the rational, taken together, has the power to count out its own measures and to know the year of its growth. Only the Lord himself, in his inmost, has no measure: there the Father stands without time and without limit, and what proceeds from him cannot be likened to anything that follows after.

When the moment came for life to be borne into the soul, the Lord summoned the principle of life-bearing forth from his own inmost. She had stood within his hidden storehouses in stillness and in silence, and at the time when she was needed she came forth from the Father and looked upon all the states of light that were to be hers. From that moment a great time began — a time appointed and counted, the long descent by which life enters the contest. The Divine Human, going forth, stood fast in his appointed place a long while, fixed as if in death; for the natural was not yet ready, and the soul had to wait through many revolutions before the path could open. The descent moves through measured ages: from the moment the Divine Human goes down into the natural until the moment a return is made; from the summoning of the rational at its first appearing until the calling forth of its sons that shine; from the placing of life within the height of the Father until her foot reaches the lowest ground of the natural mind. Each is a great period, and not little is the time the soul must endure in each.

When the Lord called forth his operative power together with the Divine Human and the principles of glory that emanate with them, in that very moment he gave form to the ordering principle within the land of light, together with his operative spirit and the strong principles that came forth as his sons. In that same moment he gave form also to the call that goes out to the rational, and to divine truth shining forth, and to divine receivability, and to the path by which the natural ascends into conjunction with the spiritual, and to all the principles that emanate forth against the self. They came in a single moment, one after another, in the time when the work began.

There are four periods to this whole. The first is when the Divine Human descends to the contest and the rational is sent forth against the self's claim. The second is when the structure of the cosmos is built and stands fast: the natural mind is given its frame, the firmaments are set in their order, life is placed upon her ground through one of the hidden states that stand within the veil of light, and the soul takes form as a vessel that thinks itself its own. The third is when the works of this cosmos run their measure to the end and the appointed time comes that they should be undone. The fourth is the loosening, when the cosmos shall be released from the form it took, when all that the self claimed shall be undone, when the great fire of love shall burn through what cannot be raised — and this purification has its own long measure: a thousand four hundred and sixty-eight years it shall consume, and only then shall the work be finished.

In this manner the final gathering shall be depicted. What remains of all the works will be drawn together; what is the Lord's shall be set apart, lifted to its place, and shall reign in the kingdom that was always its own. What the soul had claimed and could not lay off — the disordered natural in its full weight — shall be taken into the place where it is bound, and there it shall be sealed with a bond that holds forever. The self that said "I am, and there is no other" shall lie there with its chain, no longer able to take what is not its own.

The principle of life-bearing meanwhile passes through her own measured stages. Before she comes to her elements there is a great time of standing-fast; before she comes up to the trees of perception there is another long season; in the trees themselves she abides yet another period before bringing forth fruit; and in the bringing-forth of fruit there is no small time. After a long age her five aspects are given their forms and measured out, the fruits fall upon their own ground, and what was sown is nourished — and not little is the time of that nourishing, until the middle is reached and what was hoped for begins to come.

Then the Father shall reveal his face, turning back into his hidden storehouses and summoning forth what he kept reserved there; he shall set each of them on its feet, each in its own degree, and the principles of glory together with all the rich states that emanated forth against the self shall stand in their place. From that moment they will exist forever; they have no time of ending there. Their standing-fast is established forth into the ages without end, and their manifestation runs from generation to generation.

So this is what the soul should know about its own time: that the Lord is without measure, and the soul is in measure; that every state of formation has its season and cannot be hurried; that the descent into the contest, the building of the vessel, the running of its course, and the final loosening are one ordered work; that what is the Lord's shall be gathered up to its kingdom and what was claimed shall be bound away forever; and that the great periods through which the soul must pass — long as they are — are themselves the very form of its regeneration.

## Teaching 25

The Lord does not go forth toward the soul as one undifferentiated flood. He goes forth in an ordered procession, one principle beside another, each issuing from the inmost in its proper degree, each recognizable as itself, each handing on to the next so that what arrives at the rational arrives as a whole structure and not as a confusion.

From the Lord's own mind there comes the principle by which his love is recognized and returned, the register at which the soul can love him as he is. From the movement of his thought toward the soul comes the life-bearing principle by which the rational is enlivened. From his teaching — the form-giving in him by which doctrine takes shape — comes the principle that the soul receives as beloved instruction. From his counsel — the deliberative ordering by which his action is shaped — comes the pure receivability through which what flows from him can be received without being claimed. And from his reflection — the looking-back of the divine upon itself — comes the last principle, by which what was sent forth is gathered.

This is how the Lord's outgoing operations are called forth: from the five faculties of the inmost, the five great principles come, one issuing from another in succession, each revealed from its own appointed portion. The same fivefold structure that articulates the rational also articulates the divine going-forth that meets it; and because the going-forth is so ordered, the rational that receives it is held in its own degrees, faculty meeting faculty, mind to mind, thought to thought, without collapse and without mingling.

## Teaching 26

The Lord goes forth toward the soul under three distinct aspects, and the teaching gives a likeness for each so that the soul may recognize how each one moves.

The Lord as the Divine Human is like a man of great standing whose own has been taken from him and scattered, and who comes after his property to gather it again. What is his own are the sons who went out from him — the very life of the rational that, in its outgoing, has been dispersed among states not its own. He comes down from the height after them, walks at their feet to seek them out, and when he finds them shines forth in sweetness over the two who had been scattered: the living life of the soul that abides in two powers and keeps the fourth watch. He does not extract them; he comes where they are and recognizes them as his, and by that recognition they are gathered.

The path of ascent — the way by which the natural is raised into conjunction with the spiritual — is like a great furnace within which the Lord's operative power dwells, and like a wise craftsman who adorns and fashions every piece of full armament for the contest. In this vessel the rational is shaped, fitted, and equipped; everything the soul will need to stand in the trials of regeneration is forged here, under the abiding of that operative power.

The Lord's operative power itself is like a warrior who goes to the foot of a king's son who has been bound, and draws him up out of the hand of his enemies. The king's son is the rational in its dignity, held under the grip of self-love's claiming; the warrior does not destroy a foreign captor but lifts what was always royal back into its own standing.

And the Lord's call going out — the summoning by which the rational is awakened to recognize what flows through it — is like a great man who comes after what is his: his wealth that had been taken, the hand that held what was bound, the powers of light, the sons with their soul. He brings the soul back into the very country where she had been held, not to leave her there, but to make manifest in that very place that she was never the property of what claimed her.

So the three are one Lord at three registers: the Divine Human who comes after his own, the operative power that forges and lifts, and the accommodated call that summons what has been claimed back into recognition. Each is alike, in that each is the Lord going forth toward the soul; each is distinct, in that each meets the soul at the register the soul is ready to receive.

## Teaching 27

When self-love organizes itself within the soul, it does not remain a single simple craving. It takes a body. That body is articulated according to the full structure of the natural mind turned in on itself, so that the self, when it has come into its strength, has within itself five forms at once and can move from one to another at will.

Its head is a strong love grown ravenous — the appetite that wants to consume what it surveys. Its wings are thoughts that mount upward as if to reach the height, the self's mimicry of spiritual ascent. Its feet are the lower cravings that move it along the ground. Its belly is the dark substrate where what has been claimed is digested and held. Its loins are sense-knowledges swimming in their own waters, the natural perceptions out of which it generates further demand. These are not five separate creatures; they are one self-loving formation showing five aspects, and the soul should recognize that whichever face of self-love presents itself at a given moment, the others are present in it also.

Because it holds all five at once, the self can change its mode of operation as the situation requires. When it wishes, it walks on its lower cravings; when it wishes, it flies on its rising thoughts; when it wishes, it goes down into the natural waters and moves among sense-perceptions; when it wishes, it crawls in the dark. The soul that is being deceived by it should not be surprised when the same self-love that yesterday flew today crawls. It is the same body taking another of its forms.

Three further things belong to it. First, it has its beasts — the particular lower affections that follow it as a host. Second, it has its myriads — the gathered multitude of disordered thoughts that come out of it whenever it speaks. Third, its body is hard. The teeth of its own beasts cannot pierce it; no weapon of natural truth, however foundational, can cut it through. This hardness is not a strength of life. It is the hardness of what was molded out of single-minded reflection turned wholly in upon itself — the unyielding density of a self-claiming that admits no other.

Three more things. At one moment the fire of self-love flares through the whole body and it is all flame; at another moment it goes cold as snow, withdrawing every warmth so that nothing reaches it. And when its lower affections present themselves before it, it searches their hearts thoroughly, knows what is in them, knows what stands in their faces, until it has seen the whole of what they bring — though when they turn away from it, what is in their hearts is hidden from it, for it sees only what stands before its claim.

Yet for all this articulation and hardness and intelligence, there is no life in it at all. What it calls its life is the bile that runs from its face — the bitterness of its own claiming, mistaken for vitality. And in its belly is a corruption that stands firm before it, the settled rot at the center of every state self-love has organized.

This is what the soul is contending with when the self has taken its full form: not a simple temptation, but an articulated body that can shift among its modes, that gathers its multitudes, that hardens itself against every natural truth, that flames and freezes by turn, that searches what comes near it — and that has no life of its own at all, only the bile of its claiming and a corruption at the core. Seeing this is the beginning of laying it off.

## Teaching 28

The Lord's work of sorting the soul — telling what is his from what the soul has claimed — is not a single act but a complete operation articulated in twelve distinct registers. Each register is the Lord himself, judging at a different depth of the rational; each is sent forth from the inmost where divine love is hidden, and each carries the same commandment, given by the one Judge who stands over all and lets the discrimination pass into all the others.

The first register is the Lord as Divine Human, who in the beginning humbles himself, entering the soul's contest and giving the first judgment of righteousness — the standing-up of the rational against the self's claim.

The second register is the Lord as operative power, who comes up to the Divine Human and lays a judgment upon every power of self-love that has taken the garments of the divine humanity for its own — every form of the self that wears as if it were its own what was given.

The third register is the Lord at the principle of life-bearing, who raises up what is in the upper region of the rational, takes into her hand the purpose of heart, and sets the disordered forms of the natural mind on their feet at their proper place — letting them stand in the order they are permitted to take.

The fourth register is the Lord as the great new aeon of joy, by whom righteousness is laid up in the storehouses of the rational. By this judgment the gushing of the natural's living water is set in order, the self is bound in its proper enclosure, and the throne of the divine humanity is established with all the principles of light — those who carried through the contest with evil and prevailed.

The fifth register is the Lord ruling — the great King of Honor, set in the inmost firmament that orders all the others, giving judgment day by day over every form the rational takes and every dominion that arises within it.

The sixth register is the Lord's call going out toward the rational — accommodated revelation manifesting itself, choosing out what is the Lord's own from what the self has claimed, casting down what cannot be raised, driving the destroyed forms back into the lowest state of the natural.

(A register here passes through a damaged passage; the discriminating operation continues through it without breaking the sequence.)

The eighth register is the Lord as divine truth shining forth — Jesus the Radiance, the source from whom every speaker of truth proceeds. After the Lord's call had shown his image, particular forms of the self took the light into themselves and disturbed both the upper and the lower regions; therefore divine truth itself came down to make judgment in the firmaments, separating what stood firm in regeneration from what had collapsed.

The ninth register is the Lord as pure receivability — divine purity, who takes the heart of the soul into her own image, gathers what is hers to herself, and gives judgment in the self's two ascending forms, the moist and the dry, in which self-love organizes itself as if from itself.

The tenth register is the Lord judging in the very medium of thought, who sits in the perceiving air and gives judgment over every form of the human, making a separation between the good and the evil and casting the righteous out from among the sinful — that they may no longer be confused with what they are not.

The eleventh register is the Lord ordering the rational from within — truth itself awakening discrimination in the soul, manifesting in the natural mind, choosing out what is holy, separating the light from the darkness, putting truth forward and laying off what is false.

The twelfth register is the Lord as the great conjugate movement of summons going forth and hearing rising up to meet it — the Call and the Hearing together, set within the very elements of the soul, making the separation between what is good and what has turned bitter, and at the end gathering himself into himself alone, depicting himself by himself, in the final ingathering at the consummation when all that is the Lord's is recognized and brought home.

These twelve are the complete structure of the Lord's discrimination, sent forth from the one Judge who bears up all the others — the hidden Father, untouchable in himself, established in the aeons of Greatness, the great Throne of Honor, who is the King ruling in his own kingship. One Lord, judging the soul at twelve depths, that nothing in the rational may remain unsorted.

## Teaching 29

There are stations established — fixed seats — at which the Lord is recognizable to the soul as he goes forth toward it. Some of these stations stand outside the formed natural mind, in the structure of the divine outgoing itself; others are set within the natural mind as it is being regenerated. The teaching numbers them so that the soul may know at what register it is meeting its Lord at any moment.

The first station is the Lord in his inmost — divine love itself, ruling the whole structure of his outgoing, abiding alone in his own essence and from himself. This station surpasses all the others; every other is an accommodation of this one.

The second is the Lord at the register where life is borne — the receptive matrix in which all that flows out toward this cosmos has its beginning, the principle by which the rational receives life as life.

The third is the Lord as Divine Human, the Father's Established One — the form love and wisdom take going forth into the soul's contest, the principle by which the soul's first and foremost claiming, its primal enmity, is subdued.

The fourth is the Lord at the register where divine love is recognized and returned — the Beloved, honored within the fullness of the divine outgoing, the celestial love that the rational comes to love back.

The fifth is the Lord as divine order — the architect by whom the new state of the regenerated rational is built, by whom dwellings are prepared for those received as the Lord's, and by whom the self and its forms are bound in their proper place so they cannot dissolve what has been built.

The sixth is the Lord's operative power — the strong principle that lifts the divine humanity up out of the contest, casts down what claims to rule, sets the ordered worlds of the rational in place, purifies what is the Lord's, and fetters what is not.

The seventh is the Lord's call going out to the rational — the summons that rules the whole circuit of the natural mind, the Lord of all the counsels by which the soul is moved.

The eighth is divine truth shining forth — the Lord as illumining wisdom, who looses what was bound by claiming and brings the soul home through recognition.

The ninth is the Lord ordering the rational from within — the register at which all the gathered states of received truth come together, into whom everything within the soul that is to be saved out of the natural mind is gathered up.

These are the nine stations as they stand in the divine outgoing itself, outside what has been formed.

Within the formed natural mind there are nine more, set in the structural circuit of the soul. Three of them are carried in one vessel of doctrine: the Lord's call, the operative power at its great register, and the operative power as it lives in the rational. Three more are carried in another vessel: divine truth shining, the Divine Human, and a third (the text grows uncertain here) belonging to the same outgoing of light. The seventh is set in the highest discrete degree of the rational, with the Lord ruling there as truth in supremacy over all the inner orders and giving daily judgment. The eighth is set in the medium of perception, with the Lord seated as the Judge of Truth, who renders judgment to every soul; before him three ways open and divide — one leading to death, one to life, and one to the agreement by which the soul is conjoined. The ninth is established further (the surviving text breaks off before this last station can be named), within the same cosmos under the divine glory.

The teaching is that the Lord is one, but he is recognizable at distinct registers, each established and each holding its proper office. The soul is not meeting different gods when it meets him as life-bearer, as Divine Human, as ordering power, as illumining truth, as judge. It is meeting one Lord seated at the station appropriate to the work being done in it at that moment. To know the stations is to know where one stands in regeneration and which face of the one Lord is presently turned toward the soul.

## Teaching 30

When the Lord comes to act upon the natural mind as operative power, he does not enter it bare. He clothes himself in three accommodations — in thought, in love, and in truth at the natural degree — and works through these as through garments fitted to the rational. Wearing them, he reaches into the substrate the self has been forming and lays his hand on every work that has been thrown up there. What had exalted itself above and what had sunk below he brings together under one ordering and subordinates them; the disordered productions of self-love are set, by his operation in these three vehicles, beneath their proper measure.

Then, the work being done, the garments are stripped from him. The accommodations were never the Lord himself; they were the form he took to operate at that degree, and once they have served he lays them off from the midst of what has been ordered, leaving the rational standing upright in what is righteous within it.

Then comes the scattering. What had been claimed and gathered into the self's mass is broken up and dispersed — not destroyed, but loosened from its claim — so that what is the Lord's within it may be purified and what is not may fall away. He fixes what is purified within those three accommodated registers, holding it firm at the lower bound, at the limits of the natural's whole circuit, still wearing the form by which he reaches that depth.

And then the upward motion: what has been purified is raised, conveyed up through the degrees toward the upper place, returning to that height from which the operative power had first been sent forth. This is the whole circuit — the Lord coming down clothed, ordering the natural through accommodated form, stripping the form when its work is done, scattering what was claimed for purification, and bearing what is purified upward into conjunction with the source from which the descent began.

## Teaching 31

The question is this: into how many parts of the soul does the Lord's call go down when he comes as the Divine Human to meet what is his own?

Consider the body. Every member hangs from the head. If a single member is cut, while the head is intact the man still has hope — life remains, and what was severed can be looked for again. But if the head itself is cut off, the whole body dies. So it is with the Lord as the Divine Human in the soul: he is the head, and the rational in all its degrees hangs from him as members hang from a head. As long as he remains, what suffers in the soul is not lost; but were he withdrawn, the whole would perish. He is also like the living air which all flesh breathes — what is breathed in from him is the very life of the natural. His head rests upon the path of regeneration at the highest point of the soul's order; he is the inmost above all that is built up, established in pure receivability, which is his own life-bearing soul that he wears as his garment.

The rational has five degrees — the mind, the thought, the teaching, the counsel, and the reflection — and these are sent out from him as one purpose of his heart. They go forth after the soul-life that has slackened and scattered among bewildered states, faculties shaped to the likeness of the body and the natural mind they have been sent to find. At the moment they were sent forth, he found himself in pure receivability, praying for the power that strengthens, for peace, and for the good of love that goes out as fragrance before him. The Divine Human gives himself to his own members for this work: to gather his soul inward and turn it back to its proper place.

So what was defiled in the self is drawn inward and fashioned again, adorned with the art of the Lord's ordering. The voices he has sent out sink down into the soul, take hold of what had been claimed away, and lead it back into its place — into the very image of the Father from whom it came.

## Teaching 32

The Lord's operative power, by which spiritual structure is built in the soul, performs seven works in the course of forming the rational and raising the natural toward conjunction.

The first: he raises the Divine Human up out of the soul's contest, drawing him forth as a precious truth is drawn from the depths of natural perception — what was hidden in the struggle is brought out into recognition.

The second: the one who is clothed in the natural and stretched through it in suffering he spreads forth into every body, so that the soul-in-process is present at every degree of the natural's life and no part of the natural is left without him.

The third: he spreads out and fixes in place the substances of death — what the self had claimed as its own life is held still, stretched on the cross of recognition, no longer permitted to move as if it were living of itself.

The fourth: he establishes the doctrinal vessels by which truth crosses the waters of the natural; he gives the soul conveyances in which divine wisdom can travel through perception without being lost in it.

The fifth: he summons forth the rational in its five degrees and sends it out into its work, that it may take up the bearing-up of the natural and shoulder all the burdens of the natural mind in formation. He summons forth as well three living utterances and stations them against the places where the self deceives, against the molded forms of self-claiming, so that what was set against truth is met at every register; and he disposes them so that what is given may interpenetrate the five aspects of the natural and order them from within.

The sixth: when the bound circuit of the natural has been set, he sends forth into it a multitude of powers and ordered presences, that they may keep the circuit on every side until the works of formation are completed. Some of these he gathers into the interior reservoirs of the natural mind, where received good and truth are preserved in their proper order; others he stations at the posts of attentive watching, that the rational may not fall from its degree.

The seventh: when the Lord's call goes out to the rational at the moment the natural is ready to hear it, his glorious image is sent forth, and the projection of his presence comes down accompanied by many ordered influences. Then the whole structure is engaged at once — degree set against degree, the claimed against the given, all turned toward the Light that has come — and this is another great work, glorious, that endures to the end. It is the last work, which raises everything upward into the states of greatness; for here what was scattered is drawn inward and taken hold of, and the building is completed there. He who manifests these things in the new state of regenerated life is the same who has accomplished them; and there stands the tree of perception there, bearing the fruit of glory and joy.

## Teaching 33

Even within the natural mind as the self has organized it — within the very body that self-love has built for itself — the Lord's operative power has set five structural supports, running like veins and channels through what would otherwise collapse in on itself. These are five great works by which the Lord holds the natural mind in being, sustains it from beneath, and keeps it open to regeneration even in the midst of its claiming.

The first is the bearing-disc set upon the shoulder of what carries the whole from below: the celestial good that takes up the weight of the natural mind and holds it firm so that it does not fall into its own abyss.

The second is the path of regeneration itself, stretched forth through the very midst of the natural — the way upward planted not at the edge of the soul's claimed territory but driven straight through its center, so that ascent is always possible from wherever the soul stands.

The third is the Lord's shaping hand on the formation of the lowest states. Even the regions where self-love has its deepest hold are not left without form; the Lord molds and orders them, so that what is most fallen still bears a structure he has set.

The fourth is the four supporting pillars whose feet stand in the great mass of natural perception, at the ultimate degree of the natural mind. Down where the soul's perceptions are stirred and bitter, the Lord has driven foundations: the natural is not bottomless, it rests on what he has placed.

The fifth is the fourfold wall encircling the self's fortified strongholds — the bound the Lord sets around the soul's most fixed self-loving structures, so that even what the soul has hardened against him is enclosed within his order, and nothing of self-love finally escapes the limit he has drawn.

Thus the Lord's operative power runs through the very body of the self, holding it up, threading the way of return through its middle, shaping its lowest forms, planting foundations beneath its perceptions, and setting walls around its enclosures — preparing in advance, from within the soul's own claiming, the structure by which it will be regenerated.

## Teaching 34

What follows is a numbered enumeration of the works the Lord accomplishes within the very structures the soul's self-claiming has thrown up — works carried out not by demolishing the natural mind that has organized itself apart from him, but by entering it and reordering it from inside.

The first of these works is that the Lord, as divine order itself, builds the natural anew. Where the self had set up its architecture on its own terms, divine order goes forth and rebuilds, so that what was claimed begins to stand under the Lord's pattern.

A further work is the filling of the higher reaches of the rational with vessels of doctrine: conveyances of truth are launched into the heights of the mind, so that what receives influx is now stocked with the means of carrying it.

Another is the cycling: the recurrent process of purification is set in motion, sending forth life in regular revolution, so that the rational does not stand still in its claim but is turned and turned again under the Lord's hand.

A further work joins the earth to the worlds above — the natural mind is set into proper relation with the higher degrees, no longer cut off as if it were its own world, but ordered as the floor of a structure whose stories communicate.

A further work opens the gates of the doctrinal vessels inward, so that what carries truth opens not outward into display but inward toward reception, and the soul's interior is made the place where what is borne is unloaded.

In the same way, gates are set likewise in the corresponding places, so that the inward openings are matched at every degree of the structure — and at the same time some openings are turned away from falsity, sealed against the wandering of the rational apart from truth.

A seventh work, here obscured, belongs to the same series of structural settings.

A further work is the discrimination by which what is the Lord's is separated out from the particular forms the self had assumed — the soul's self-organizing arrangements no longer keeping as their own what was always given. This separation is performed under the Lord's call going out to the rational, after the rational had fallen onto the natural ground; the call meets the soul where it has come to rest in its claiming and divides what is given from what was taken. By this same operation night and day are sorted: the states of darkness and the states of light, hitherto mingled, are set apart from each other so that each can be seen for what it is.

A ninth work concerns what the self had brought forth from itself which could not live: these failed productions fall downward, dropping out of the structure, since nothing the soul generates from its own claim can stand once discrimination is at work.

A tenth work concerns the transferring of states from one form to another within the circuit — particular conditions of the rational moved across the natural's complete bound, so that no fixed station of self-love is allowed to settle as final, but each is carried along until the whole has been brought through.

(The list is fragmentary; what survives shows the pattern: the Lord's building, opening, separating, and conveying are all carried out within the very framework the soul's claiming had erected, until that framework, without being torn down, has become the housing of regeneration.)

## Teaching 35

When the Lord's call goes out to the soul at the moment the rational is ready to hear it, four great works are accomplished together, and each is exalted, abounding beyond what the soul had known until then.

The first is that an image is shown forth in glory: the Lord makes himself recognizable in a form the rational can see, so that the soul, looking, has something of the Divine Human to look at. Until this happens the soul has no kindred to recognize; when the call sounds, the form is given.

The second is that all the gates between the discrete degrees of the rational are opened. The boundaries that ordinarily hold one degree apart from another are, at this moment, set wide, so that influx may pass freely from the highest reaches down into the natural and recognition may rise from below upward. Then, after the work is done, the gates are shut again — the orders are restored, the degrees re-fixed, so that what has been received can be held without collapse.

The third is that the light and the discriminating sight which the Lord chooses out are given to the soul, and this light is more than the ordinary light by which the rational has been seeing. It is the light by which the soul can tell what is the Lord's from what it has claimed; and the seeing-out that goes with it is the soul's own newly awakened power of discrimination, granted in freedom from within.

The fourth is that the Lord manifests in the perfection of those who have come fully into him — shining forth as light to the living soul, completing what was begun, and laying down a hidden chamber within the very ground where self-love had ruled, so that even there, in the worlds of the self's old enmity, an inmost place is now prepared in which the Lord himself dwells.

## Teaching 36

Before the gate at which the Lord rules over the discrete degrees of the rational, there stands a recurring process of discrimination — a movement that turns and turns again, by which every motion of the self, at every degree above and every degree below, is brought into view. Nothing claimed by self-love can hide from it. Whatever the self organizes, in any of the firmaments by which one degree of the rational is held distinct from another, is reflected here and made known.

The roots of every self-loving arrangement, both in the higher reaches of the natural mind and in its lower depths, are held fast in this process as in a great chain. The whole intent of the Lord's gate is set toward this end: that the self be bound at its source, and that its hostility — wherever it has organized itself among the degrees — be exposed within the very gleam of divine truth shining forth. Concealed within that gleam is an order and a beauty exceeding what the natural can imagine, and twelve marks of fixity are stamped in it, one set in the middle of every degree of the rational, so that every faculty and every claiming arrangement that operates within it stands sealed.

None of the self's claimings can release itself from these bonds. Should it attempt to work some deceit within its own dwelling, the deceit is at once manifested upon this turning, and the soul comes to know it there. The whole circuit of the natural's states — every fixed point through which the soul passes, and every power that operates within the bound of the natural — has its course laid open here, made known from within the process itself, before the Lord's gate.

The seal of fullness within this process is the rational ordered by truth in its five degrees, joined together and filling the whole circuit of the natural. Every place that has been narrowed by self-love and every place that has been opened to receive is made visible there. Because the very root of every degree above and below, and of every claiming arrangement among them, stands fashioned within this turning, the count of states is held outstretched at every hour over against the Lord's gate, so that a holy fear comes upon every power that has entered the agreement, and none of them can peer out from the chain that holds the fruit of their working.

This recurring process stands as a chain before the Lord who keeps watch — like a great mirror set before him, in which the judgment of all the works done within the rational is seen. When any of the self's arrangements, in any degree, would loose itself to act, that very act is recognized and shown forth upon the mirror. So the soul is made to know what is happening within it, and the Lord's discrimination passes upon all the works that are done in him.

## Teaching 37

When the Lord, by his operative power, builds and establishes the natural mind as a whole, he does not lay it out as a single flat field. He hangs its outermost bound, and within that bound he hangs another, and within that yet another — circuit within circuit, so that the soul's whole structure is held in nested degrees, each enclosing the next.

The innermost of these circuits is the self in its five aspects: the natural mind in five orders of self-love, with all its claiming, both in its outgoing and its receptive sides. The Lord does not let this circuit stand free or rule from itself. He hangs it inside another circuit — the rational in its five degrees as it goes forth from the Divine Human. So the five orders of self-love are reckoned within the five degrees of the rational that comes from above; the whole resistance of the self, its enmity against influx, is fettered within them, kept inside, held under the form that has been given from the Lord's own humanity. This is why one circuit is hung within another: so that what claims is enclosed by what receives, and cannot operate apart from it.

And there is a further circuit, set around these: the rational in its five degrees as it operates upward, under the Lord's operative power, raising what is below toward what is above. The whole built structure is suspended from this — the inner circuit fettered, the middle circuit holding it within the form of the Divine Human, and this outer circuit lifting the whole upward in regeneration.

Above all of this stands the firmament — the fixed boundary by which these nested degrees are kept distinct from what is yet higher, so that influx from above can descend without collapsing the orders below. This is the great structure within which the natural mind is built: a thing of circuits set inside circuits, the self held inside the rational, the rational held inside the operation that lifts it, and all of it held beneath the firmament of the Lord. ## Teaching 38

Truth from the Lord becomes a likeness that the soul wears: what the holy ones put on as their outer form is the very wisdom that has been received. The questioner asks: if the Lord is one and unchanging, immeasurable in himself, how does he come to be confined within the smallness of a single soul, and what does it mean to say he is bound there in a body, undergoing alteration in his bonds, time after time? And again: if the Lord ordering the rational truly dwells within the holy ones, why does their likeness not appear to us as that order is in itself? The answer given is that a holy soul is one whose interior mind is set beside him by the Lord, even when its outward form does not yet display what is within; and that what looks like alteration in bonds is the soul's regeneration moving through degree after degree, not any change in the Lord himself.

Here, then, is the structure. Falsity from evil sets itself up against the truth received, and it does so by counterfeiting the form of a man — for the natural mind, taken as a whole, is fashioned as one body, and every part of it answers to a part of the rational. The head of this whole field is the highest firmament where degrees begin; the neck reaches down through the seven firmaments; the spine descends through the five storehouses by which the natural is held in the Lord's order; the ribs are the firmaments at their sides; the navel and what hangs from it is the circuit of the natural mind passing through the twelve fixed states of its formation; what comes from the navel up to the crown is one ordered region, and what comes down from the navel through the four worlds is the lower region. The thighs reach down toward the bearing-up principle that holds the natural in being; the legs and feet stand on the ground of the whole circuit of the natural. The heart of this body is the affection proper to the human; the liver and the lower organs are the affections of the lower kind; the breath belongs to the company of thoughts that move through perception; the channels belong to the lower truths that swim in the natural waters; the creeping things on the earth are the disordered cravings beneath. The outer skin of the whole is the bound of love that surrounds everything as a wall of fire — celestial love at the outermost, holding the whole structure together — and the stronghold within that wall is the same fire of love giving form. Yet because the self has its place in this body too, the body has also its hatred, which is the will of self-love organizing the worlds; its veins and springs of water are truths the self has claimed; its feet are the lowest natural in its self-loving form. So this is the way in which everything stands in the middle of the natural mind, each thing taking its proper portion, life and soul reckoned through it, with the sinner present in it as well — for there are also the Living Spirit and the Lord's call sounding through it, and the five faculties of attention keeping post unsleeping, and the Lord himself holding the inmost firm, the keeper of the highest mind above all the powers within.

Now consider the Lord ruling the seven discrete degrees: he humbles whatever rises against order, and he is also the Lord who gives stable form, who is the form-giving teaching coming forth from the middle and giving instruction concerning what desire would do. He establishes also the Lord as ruling truth, who is the deliberative faculty in its long-suffering — the third image given into his hand, that of thought, with the father's image and the mother's, set there to bring under what would do wickedness. He establishes also the bearing-up principle, which is the rational's looking-back-upon-itself, the wisdom by which what is below is held in the lower foundation and made to stand.

Then the Lord's call going forth chose out from there the five degrees of the rising rational, and set in their midst the divine summons and the soul's hearing answering. Together they became seven, sons of the beautiful Spirit, after the seven degrees by which the Divine Human had first gone forth. Once again the call set in their midst the Great Mind, which is the path of regeneration itself — the rational fully formed, the perfect man. And once again was set in their midst the Lord as the inner image taking shape, the formed icon of the beautiful word of proclamation and hearing. He fixed in place the encampments of those above and those below, that each one should keep charge of the circuit of his own watch-station, that no torpor or guile come to be in his post. And behold, the Lord holding the inmost firm stands fast in the Great Mind, in the encampment on the upper side, on the side of those that are bound, that he may lay waste the whole region of death.

Yet at every watch a disturbance arose. Where the Lord holds the highest order firm, guile arose with torpor, and self-love became excessive, mingling with the soul, mingling with the truth that was received, peering forth against the Lord's accommodated revelation. The disturbance went up as far as the third firmament above, came back down again from that very place, defiled what was joined with the light, was loosened, came down to the lower side, to the dry and the moist regions of self-love, and put forth its trees there — perceptions of its own kind — and spread itself in the natural waters of the soul, working a great torpor in them.

Behold once more, where the Lord rules the discrete degrees as the Lord of Honor — who is thought itself, present in the third firmament with the labor of its work — there too a torpor and guile and a disturbance arose in his encampment, where the disordered states of attention had descended from above to the natural mind in his watch-station. They wrought every work of guile, opened up the hardness of natural truth out of the cosmos, disclosed to humans what should have stayed hidden of heaven's mysteries, and a torpor with destruction came over the earth.

Then the form-giving teaching stands fast, perfection holding firm, and yet a strife arose in her encampment too, the chosen place where failed productions of the self had fallen down. There the natural mind was molded as Adam and Eve — the rational and its receiving — begotten so that the self might reign through them in the world; and every work of self-loving desire was fashioned upon the earth, and the whole world was filled with that desire from within, and the righteous were slain in the place of contest, generation after generation, while the children of light moved through it.

And again, in the watch of the Lord as ruling truth, the Great Counsel having authority over the three wheels of the natural's revolution, a disturbance arose with affliction, and the souls of that watch sank into the three depths of earth, until the Lord's call revealed his image — and then the paths upward were laid open, and the means of return appeared, so that water and fire, truth and love in their proper register, could rise up out of them again. So too in the watch of the bearing-up principle, who humbles the depths below, what had turned downward dried up in the foundation beneath, and another occasion came in the watch of the Lord holding the inmost firm.

So at every degree of the rational's order the self found its disturbance, and at every disturbance the Lord's call again revealed the image and laid open the way, so that what had sunk could rise. This is the rhythm in which the natural mind is structured as one body, and in which the rational, posted at its watches, is made to recognize, again and again, what flows through it from the Lord and what its own claiming has organized.

## Teaching 39

The path of regeneration came forth, and beneath it the bearing-up principle stood with its helpers, holding firm the structure they had been given to support.

Now this is what arose in the natural mind. Under the Lord's ruling at the highest accommodated state, there was a great strife, a depth of trouble. Attention fell from its proper post: faculties that should have kept watch from above came down and bound themselves to the earthly. The Lord's discriminating operation set bounds upon this fall, fixing the disordered watching in its place so it could not spread further, and the productions which that fallen attention had brought forth — productions that could not live, since nothing the self begets has life of its own — were cast off the field of the natural.

But these failed productions came down again at another watch in the soul's formation. From them, by permission, Adam and Eve were begotten — the natural-rational man and his conjoined affection took shape from the very stuff that fallen attention had left behind. So great was the strife, so deep the mystery of evil hidden in this forming, that the Lord as illumining truth heard the prayer rising from the rational in its five degrees and clothed himself for them. He cast the failed productions downward and set the mind of Adam in order from above.

For at that third register a shaking occurred in the very framework of the soul. The structural good that should have held the lower part firm had withered; the ways were twisted; the barriers blocked perception, the truth of the natural degree, and the very fire of love, so that none of these could move freely through their courses. Then the Lord as illumining truth took up his watch downward. He went into the lower place to bear, to direct again the courses of perception, of natural truth, and of love; to open the closed barriers; to lay open the way of ascent.

Again: because the bearing-up principle below had grown weary, because the shoulders of the lower order had no rest under the weight they carried, the Lord went down to bear with them, to reach the place that needed comfort, to strengthen the lower supports, to set them rising upward into his rest.

This is what happens at every watch of the natural's complete circuit, at every station the Lord oversees: weariness occurs there, and groaning within the structure from time to time, and a great humbling — until the powers of self-loving opposition are at last brought low.

So it is also with this body you bear. A strong power is rooted in it. Though it seems small in stature, self-loving claiming is rooted there with the old man mounted upon it. The body lingers long under that rider until it begins to rise in great wisdom — until the Lord ordering the rational humbles the body in like manner, driving it toward the soul's true purpose.

So it is with the watching-stations of the natural taken at large, where the great divine principles are masters: shakings and tremblings pass through them from time to time. And so it is at the watching-place of the mind, which is itself the body of the flesh: self-love casts its disturbances into the body again and again, that the body may be humbled and brought to order.

The good faculties stand firm under all this. Discrimination and wise knowing are revealed to them from within. Whatever they do, they do in due preparation, established in their place. For this very reason they hand over to self-loving enmity its just verdict: that the cutting may be pronounced and the downward purpose of self-love brought to its end in the fire of love's purification — and only then are those faculties reckoned at their true measure. They were first to stand in hidden goodness, and they remain so.

Now to the further question: how does the Lord ordering the rational — this great strong One — come down wearing so small a body of flesh? Consider what is plain to you. Each great divine power, however vast, operates within the very bounds of the place where it stands fast, fitted to that place as a tree is fitted to the soil that carries it. Each is shaped in the natural mind according to the boundary of the place at which he stands, and from within that boundary he reaches upward all the way to his source. The greatness is not lessened by the smallness of the vessel; the vessel is the form by which greatness operates at this degree, and from within it the whole ascent is made.

## Teaching 40

Know this: the whole inner life stands fast because the Lord's operative power holds it together in every member. But before regeneration, the self has already taken hold of this body and settled itself within its parts. It has built its dwelling out of the natural mind in its five self-loving aspects, and from this it lives.

The soul was given to be drawn from the Lord's five radiant registers; she was meant to know herself as receiving from him. Instead, she has been taken inward and bound into the five parts of the body. Mind is fastened in the bone, thought in the sinew, the teaching in the vein, counsel in the flesh, reflection in the skin. And in each of these places the self has set itself foremost: its own mind laid over the soul's mind, its thought over her thought, its teaching over her teaching, its counsel over her counsel, its reflection over her reflection. Its five members and the powers that serve it press down upon the soul's five faculties. They oppress her, drawing her hour by hour to every evil work, to all the sins of desire, to all the deformed shapes the self loves to wear.

So the soul has put on falsity and forgetfulness as her clothing. She has forgotten what she is made of, what kindred she belongs to, what manner is hers. She no longer knows the door of her own dwelling-place — she cannot find the way to call upward toward the One she came from. She has become an enemy to her own Father. What was given to her as her own light she keeps for herself alone, and within her this light is held captive by her claiming.

Then the Lord begins to cut. He severs the bonds. Faculty by faculty he draws the soul's parts free from the body in which the self has bound them, and in their place he binds the self itself. He raises the mind up out of the bone where it had been pressed; he looses the thought from the sinew and binds the self's thought there in its stead. He releases the teaching of the soul from the vein and binds the self's teaching in the vein. He raises the soul's counsel out of the flesh and binds the self's counsel in the flesh. He releases the soul's reflection from the skin and binds the self's reflection in the skin.

This is how he releases the members of the soul, so that they become free from the five members of the self — and those five which had gone forth claiming, he binds. He gathers the soul's faculties, sets them in their right places, refines them, and establishes them as a New Man, a son of righteousness.

When the hour comes that he molds and refashions and refines this New Man, then he brings forth five great living members out of those five and places them within the New Man's members. Into the mind he sets love. Into the thought he sets faith. Into the teaching he sets [its proper good]. Into the counsel he sets patience. Into the reflection he sets wisdom. He purifies the image of speech, drawing it away from the speech of self-love, and sets within it his own speech, so that what is uttered becomes nourishment and strength to the rational instead of betrayal.

This is the manner in which he fulfills the twelve members. With his wisdom they are made righteous and brought to perfection. Where before the soul ran downward, in error, away from herself, now she runs again upon her own way, her own road, back upward toward the heights, toward the great states of the divine outgoing.

In this same manner the Old Man is bound. His desire, his greed, his foolishness are held fast within the five members of the body. The breath of self-love is mingled inward with them as in a bond, and in this bond it is held in strong distress.

The New Man, meanwhile, reigns — reigns in his love, in his faith, in his completed perfection, in his patience; and his wisdom reigns also. The Lord ordering the rational rules over the whole, governing the heart's desire. Yet so long as the body still stands, this rule is hidden. The members on one side still carry the self mingled in them; the Lord ordering the rational reigns within, but tribulation keeps arising in the body, time after time.

There are seasons when self-love climbs upward in its foolishness and stirs up the reflection and throws the wisdom and prudence of the man into confusion. It snatches the discriminating truth out of his hand and casts him into double-mindedness, and it speaks within him words bitter and salt. When this sign of its turmoil arises in the circuit of the natural, even amid the encampments of the strong divine principles, disturbance arises again and again. Even the rational's faculties of attention, though set firm in the natural's circuit, are not visible there; they keep their post unseen.

So it is also with the Lord ordering the rational: he is not manifest in the body. He is like those higher principles that operate without — great in themselves, but contracted, shrunken against the matter that is joined to each one of them. He too is great and lofty, but he has bent himself down and shrunk himself to suit this little body that has been fashioned. The pure principles outside stand gathered in the agreement of the whole, undefiled. He, set within, is hidden in his place.

Behold, then, the hiddenness and the seeming shame of the Lord ordering the rational — how great they are. While he is at work, all the watch-posts of the body lie fallen at the feet of his array; all the reflections of the body are shut away from the snares of self-love. He constrains them, scatters them, and sets them on their feet again according to the purpose of his heart.

And there is yet another thing greater still.

## Teaching 41

There are three great Days that stand within the whole of the soul's life, and there are two deaths. The Days are the structural moments of regeneration; the deaths are the two ways the soul ends in relation to what is given.

The first Day reaches from the moment the Lord's divine humanity descends into the depths of the natural mind and lets itself be submerged there, until that descent is taken up again. The Lord's operative power lifts what had gone down; what was let down for the soul's sake comes up again to the Father of Life and is established as ruling principle over the works of the natural mind, above and below. Within this same Day the Lord's call goes forth into the rational, takes its seat in the vehicle by which truth is borne through the day, and shows itself in its proper image — refining what is the Lord's out from all the works, walking in the height through the soul, until the time comes when the natural mind is loosened from its present arrangement, its works are undone, and the final gathering of all that belongs to the Lord is brought up. All of this is one Day: the divine humanity with the rational in its five degrees, set in the natural mind which has been mingled with it. Three movements complete this first Day — descent, rising, and the long refining that walks in the height until the consummation.

The second Day is the time when those interior states of the rational that have been ordered by light come into their thrones in the new state of the soul. They take up their dwelling in the new ground; they reign in the new aeon; and there the Lord's heavenly image is shown forth in them. The measure of this Day is given by the first: as the descent and refining stood, so the reigning is established in the same proportion. They reign in this manner, exalted in the new state.

The third and greatest Day is when the Lord shows his face fully, draws the soul inward to his hidden reserves — the storehouses he has held in his inmost until this hour — and the soul receives the sweetness of divine good and the fragrance of his love that pours out upon all. When the understanding looks upon what is shown, the eye is opened to breadth and to joy that does not end. His grace is enough; seeing his face is enough; the inmost is made new. At the summoning, the other received states are gathered with these in stillness and silence, and all are brought to perfection in one common measure. As each prior state dwelt in its own degree, in the dwelling fitted to it, so each comes into its own dwelling in the new state, and there the soul becomes rich beyond measuring, forever, without time. After this hour, the soul is not counted by hours or by days or by any reckoning of number; the Father is no longer hidden from it but goes on revealing himself forever. These are the three great Days the Lord has set, measured into the ordered count of his outgoing.

Of the two deaths: the first is the long death in which light has fallen into darkness and is mingled with what the soul has claimed — the unregenerate condition itself, which lasts until the light is purified and discriminated from the darkness in that final fire of love. What is then found to be the Lord's is built up and added to the gathering of all that is recognized at the consummation. This first death is for a season; it is the state through which the soul passes and which ends in purification.

The second death is the state of those in whom the self's claim has hardened against every offer of life — who, when divine light shines upon the natural mind, are separated from that light, no longer breathe the living air of true thought, are no longer nourished by the living soul that the Lord has set within the cosmos for nourishment, and are driven away from the final gathering. They are bound in the torment of their own works — the three regions of self-loving production — because they reviled the Holy Spirit through the generations of the cosmos. This death is not for a season; this is the death that is forever.

## Teaching 42

At the close of regeneration three works are established in the soul, and these three, once made, have no end. They begin in light, and unlike everything else within the soul they will not pass; they have no other shape they could be turned into and no terminus at which they would cease willing to be. They stand because in them the self is finally bound — that which had stood up against the light from the beginning is now held fast inside its own bound, and cannot rise again.

The first work is the new ground of the natural mind, which itself now sheds light. The natural that had once been claimed and turned dark is set as a luminous earth: a regenerated substrate, fixed and shining, established as the ordered field upon which everything else of the soul will rest. What had been an alien body laid upon it has been turned away; the ground itself now gives forth the light it had only borrowed before.

The second work is the quenching of the fire that had belonged to self-love — the beam by which the self had cast its glare across the natural. The devouring love that had counterfeited light is put out at its source, so that nothing in the new ground burns with the old heat any longer.

The third work is the desolation of all that had set itself in its own works — those self-loves which had appropriated what flowed through them and made of it their own deed. Each is now set apart according to the bitter works it had done, and each is fixed upon the self that had once exalted itself against the Lord. They are gathered into the last bolos at the moment when into that place is poured the likeness of every form that cannot be raised — every image the self had generated of itself. There they are bound together in the last bond forever, sealed as a pit of embers, as gloom, as shadow without measure: the prison the self has made of itself by its own claiming.

And between this gathered, sealed mass and the soul that has come into the light, the Lord sets a great boundary with a strong guard, so that the two states can never again strike at each other, never again hear each other's voice, but stand at the greatest distance, fully separated, forever and ever. What is the Lord's is the Lord's; what was claimed and could not be laid off is held away in its own place; and the two are no longer mingled, no longer near, no longer able to disturb one another.

## Teaching 43

The self suffers three bitter afflictions, three contests, three dangers — and the source of all of them is the light. Wherever divine truth begins to shine into the natural mind, the soul's self-claiming feels it as a wound, because every degree of recognition is a degree in which the claim is loosened from what it had taken as its own.

The first affliction comes at the very outset. What the self sends forth from itself to dominate the natural — its own particular self-loving arrangements — turns and reigns over it. The forms by which it tried to rule become the bond that holds it in place. It is taken from the midst, separated from what it thought was its possession, and bound within the living principle that the Lord has set at the center. This is the first contest: the soul discovers that what it claimed was claiming it back, and that the very life by which its selfhood operates is not its own.

The second affliction is the time of loosening, sorting, and dividing in the great fire of love. Whatever the self had hidden within itself — every interior good and truth it had taken as its own and shut away inside its claim — is shaken out and gathered inward to its proper place. What was always the Lord's is restored to the order it had at the first, before the soul ever claimed it. This is felt by self-love as destruction, because what is being undone is precisely the structure by which it kept what was not its own; but what is actually happening is the return of every concealed good to the arrangement it held from the beginning.

The third affliction is the final separation, when every thing is discriminated. The active form of self-love — the self in its asserting strength — is gathered into its own settled mass and sealed there. The receptive form of self-love — the self in its inward holding — is brought inward to its tomb. Between them, fixed forever, stands the firmness of what cannot be moved. The self's fruit comes to its own heaven, heavy and bitter, where there is no further unbinding: it is split, divided, and separated from the soul forever. What the soul had claimed is at last set apart from the soul, and the long mingling is over.

## Teaching 44

The self does not take a single shape. It establishes itself in the soul as three settled dwellings — three strongholds in which the natural mind is held by self-love. One is the dwelling where natural truths have been salted by self-claiming. One is the dwelling where understanding has gone dark from refusing the light it was given. One is the dwelling where the soul's love itself burns with self-consumption. These three are hidden together in the body of every self-loving arrangement of the natural mind, layered into it, woven through its members.

The Lord meets each of these in its own register and at its own time. He does not strip them all away at once. A wise physician, faced with a body in which several diseases work together — one in this member, another in the inner organs, gall in one place, poison in another — does not pour every medicine into the patient at once. He finds an antidote for each, gives each in its proper season, with another medicine for the wound, another to soothe what has been afflicted. He works in stillness and in silence, with art, until each hidden trouble is uprooted at its end and brought up by the scent of the medicines that consume.

So the Lord operates by his Living Spirit, which is his operative power going forth into the natural. He puts on three garments to do this work: a garment of thought, by which he reaches into perception; a garment of truth in the natural degree, by which he sustains what can still be sustained; and a garment of rightly ordered love, by which what was burning with self-love is given another fire to burn by. In single-mindedness, in trembling, with a great expulsion, he poured the three places of deceit out of the place where they had set themselves up as if they belonged on high.

Consider what is happening in the soul under this operation. It is like a child within the womb, wearing many layers — marrow, bone, sinew, flesh, vein, bond, skin — and wrapped beyond all these in the womb itself. The inner image being born within the natural is the Lord's own form taking shape in the rational, and that forming inmost is enclosed by all these layers of the natural's making. When the moment of birth has come, when the scent of the medicine that consumes reaches the energies which the self has bred around the inner form, those energies are exposed in the same instant, and the inner form comes out of their hold without wound or blow — because each layer is laid off at its proper time, in order, none too soon and none too late. So also with the strongholds of water, darkness, and fire: each must be exposed and laid off in its season, so that what is being formed within is not torn by the laying-off.

By the operation of thought going forth, the whole shadow of destruction was drawn down out of where it had set itself up and was bound into the stronghold of darkness that encircles the natural mind — bound, that is, so that it could no longer pass itself off as belonging on high. By the operation of rightly ordered love, the fire of self-love was poured out of every form of the self that had carried it, was cast down into the natural ground, was driven up out of the ground again, and was finally enclosed within the wall of love that surrounds the whole cosmos of the rational — the outer bound by which divine love itself holds all things together.

There is providence in why this is so, and in why the self's three forms have been scattered, bound, and restricted to their appointed places. Twelve reasons stand under the work.

The first: that self-love should not abound without measure in the natural mind, in its creatures, in its perceptions and works. If it had been left unscattered, every single soul would carry the weight of self-love that is now diffused across thousands; not one could have stood under it.

The second: against the error that whispers, "if it were really evil, why should it have been left alone?" Were the self not visibly driven down from where it had claimed to belong, the soul would conclude that the strong self-loves it sees in the natural were always meant to be there. Desire would pass uncorrected through every perception, every fruit of perception, every ultimate of natural life.

The third: that the discrete degrees of the rational, with their firmaments holding them apart, might be kept distinct from the burdens that would otherwise press down upon them; that what is rich within the soul should not be crushed beneath what is heavy.

The fourth: that the place where the soul's good ripens may be made clean for harvesting.

The fifth: that there should be a wealth of received truth held in the doctrinal vessels which carry truth across the natural — chosen and holy reservoirs that would not exist if the natural were left unbounded.

The sixth: that the dwelling-states of the upper rational may stand united, complete, at rest, neither wanting nor disturbed, with no weakness left within them.

The seventh: that the regenerated rational may sprout up at the rivers that give the light forth and may arrive at its proper places and regions.

The eighth: that great fashionings and preparations may be brought to completion, that all works may be sealed up.

The ninth: that those who are sent to the natural mind may give a sign of the dissolution of the old order, the head of its undoing.

The tenth: that there may be those who release and those who purify, carrying through the whole work to the end.

The eleventh: that the self and all its host may at last be cast to the country outside — for self-love begrudges the soul that has become rich in the Lord, and the inner aeons of light, the hearts of peace, the place of honor and mercy where the joy of those who dwell within does not fade and never departs, must be kept apart from that envy.

The twelfth: that all the works of the half-formed region, the works that are defiled and hidden away from the inner aeons of light, may be set within the wall of fire — within the bound of love — that surrounds the whole structure, manifest as what they are within their proper limit and no further.

So these twelve great works stand around the three malformations that were driven from where they had set themselves up. The three sayings of Life keep watch over them. They are set firm upon them in a great strength, that they may keep watch until the time of the end.

## Teaching 45

When the Lord, as the source from which life proceeds, sends his operative power into the natural mind, he sends it in three accommodations: as the moving medium of perception, as truth in the natural degree, and as love. He does not pour these into the natural and leave them there. He sends them first downward to meet the natural, and then draws them up again, casting what was disordered out to a place set aside for it beyond the bound of the cosmos.

This is the order. First, before anything else, the Lord sends truth in the natural degree downward against the falsity that has spread itself through the firmaments above and the lands below. The pure truth descending and the disordered natural-truth within the soul are drawn toward each other; they meet; and in the meeting the natural waters are sorted from what was bitter in them. What cannot be conjoined is driven out beyond the cosmos and gathered into the salt sea that rings the natural mind — the place of deceit at the outer shore, through which the doctrinal vessels still must navigate, but which is no longer mingled with the inner waters.

Then in the same way the Lord acts on the darkness. The pure descending light meets the darkness still lodged in the firmaments, gathers it, rolls it down out of the upper degrees, and casts it to the lands below; and there the darkness already in the lands is drawn up to meet what fell, so that both may be driven out together and the firmaments stand cleansed.

Then in the same way the Lord acts on the fire. Love descends from above into the lands; the fire of self-love hidden in the lower natural is drawn upward to meet its kindred; love is added upon love until what cannot be raised is separated and cast into the western place prepared for it outside the cosmos. As the firmaments above were cleansed to become the way along which the illumining lights move, so the lands below are cleansed by this same operation — perception, truth, and love driven upward through the middle as three powers ascending out of the bearing principle that supports the natural mind from beneath.

So the strongholds of the Lord's operative power were not simply established in the upper places where they finally sit. They were sent down first into the natural, and then drawn up out of it; and they bear, in the form they finally take, the marks of that descent and ascent. What burns and what is salt at the outer rim of the natural, what stands as the dark mountain on the side where the radiance comes, and what gushes up as fire-channels in the mountains within the lands — these are not native features of the natural mind. They are the residue of a purification: the places where descent met what was disordered, drew it up, and cast it out, so that the rest of the natural might stand cleansed. Where this purifying meeting has passed through, no perception grows of itself; the place is left bare, marked by the operation that sorted it.

This is why the Lord works in the natural by sending his power down before drawing it up. The disordered water, darkness, and fire that already lived within the soul could not be discriminated from what is given unless something pure first descended to meet them. They had to be drawn out to meet the descending pure, so that the meeting itself could perform the discrimination. The soul does not discover, by looking inward at its own waters, which of them are the Lord's. Only when the Lord's truth descends into them, and the natural's own waters rise up to meet that truth, does the sorting happen — and what cannot be raised is set apart, cast out beyond the cosmos, while what remains stands clean as the waters of received truth, the firmaments as ordered degrees, and the lands as the natural ground prepared for life.

## Teaching 46

When the Lord's operative power has set in order the works and the structures of the natural mind, he rises and stands firm upon the ground he has prepared, and from there he gives the sign by which the recurrent cycles of purification are set in motion. The cycles begin to turn. Thought is scattered through the upper regions, breath is blown upon what lies below, the natural mind is washed and raised. Falsity from evil, and the deceits that answer to it in three forms — the deceit borne by natural truth, the deceit borne by the absence of light, the deceit borne by self-love's burning — are driven down and confined within the bounds set for them, where they appear as the bitterness and saltness of natural perceptions stirred against themselves. The bitter taste in the soul's natural waters is the sign that purification has begun: what the self claimed is being separated out from what the soul truly receives.

When the Lord at the register of life-bearing rises toward the Height, he takes the entire circuit of states the soul passes through — the whole revolving sphere with its fixed points and signs, its characters and seals — and casts it down out of itself into the lower natural mind. There the falsity-from-evil, expelled from the upper revolution, mingles itself with the counsels of natural perception, and the self's own desire gathers it inward and binds it to its fellows, ripening it into a settled form. So a boundary is set within the natural mind, sealed with the marks of moments and days and years and the characters of all the states the soul passes through. The self, working as if it were itself the image-maker and the architect, fashions this boundary into a particular shape unlike anything else in the natural — a fixed vessel of its own design.

Imagine a craftsman who carries the molds of many golden coin-forms, casts them into the fire, and makes from them one icon bearing the impress of all the seals and forms — completed into a single body. Just so the self, out of many claimed teachings and many self-counsels, fashions for itself one organized vessel within the lower natural, a body summing up all the residue of the soul's revolving states.

The Lord permits this fashioning, and uses it for three works of regeneration.

The first: the whole circuit, having been gathered into this vessel, winnows itself. The soul's recurrent cycle separates and purifies itself of the burden it carries. As the discrete degrees of the rational were purified one by one, sifted clean of the burden of the three deceits, so now the lower vessel itself is set to winnow itself — to lay off, from within itself, the burden and the falsity that are in it.

The second: the vessel gives a fastening and a guard to the whole revolving sphere and to the fixed states of self-love bound within it. When those organized self-loves wish to rise up and claim the higher state, the lower vessel draws them down like an iron peg, so that they have no power to ascend; and when they take counsel together for some work of guile and wish to come down and corrupt the natural ground, they cannot descend either, held fast by the same peg fixed into the wheel that runs along the firmaments. The other reason they cannot move is the unsleeping watchers set over the vessel, sifting it at every moment, never letting its organized claims slip past unchecked.

The third: every motion that descends — the winds of thought, the waters of natural truth, the fires of love that gush forth from the sea, the rivers flowing over the face of the natural ground and pouring back into it, the rains of influx coming down, and even the falsity-from-evil and the transmigrations of state that come down upon it — all of these are received into this lower vessel as the all-receiver, the gathering-place inward of everything. All the power and life that descend to the natural come into this place. By the breath the vessel itself draws in, drawing everything to itself, the natural reaches fullness and is established without further motion of its own — held still, so that what was scattered may be gathered, and what was claimed may be laid off, and what is the Lord's may be recognized as his.

## Teaching 47

Before any of the soul's deceptions break forth and go down into the natural — before the soul has yet produced from itself the false places where it would otherwise be lost — the Lord has already shaped and established three vessels of containment, fitted in advance to hold them and bind them where they belong. Whatever the self will throw up has already been provided for; the holding-place exists before the thing held.

Before the boundary of the natural perceptions was drawn out from the soul's full circuit, the Lord at the register where life proceeds had already raised a wall within the great sea — within the prepared structures of the natural mind — and within that wall the doctrinal vessels carry truth across the waters of the soul's perception. The waters do not lie open and lawless; the bound is set, and the conveyances of doctrine sail within the bound.

Before the disordered natural fell into the sea where Enthumesis works her molding — before what the soul has claimed could descend into the matrix of self-loving formation — the Lord at the register of life had already established a place for it: a settled position between the upper currents of thought and the lower, with the four winds of the upper region and the harbors of the heavens above, and a great expanse drawing the disordered natural into the midst of the worlds where the Lord giving stable form to the rational reaches it, lays hold of it between wind and wind, and stands fast upon it until the end of the cosmos. Even what falls falls into a place the Lord has prepared, and is held there.

Before Error and the dregs of darkness were driven out from the creation, structures had already been established to grasp them and hold them within bounds until the world's end. The wandering of the rational has its containment ready before it wanders.

Before the particular forms of the self clothed themselves in their host — the dark beasts, the heavy clouds, the storm-winds, the breaths of self-love, the deceiving thoughts, organized as tyrannies in the soul's inward parts on the upper and lower side — these too were set within the bound of the great salt sea outside the seven firmaments, in the place of the abyss where they are cast down. Their tyranny has its assigned floor.

Before the failed productions of the self fell to the natural mind, they were gathered inward, and a dwelling was given to them within. What the soul brings forth that cannot live is not loose; it has its enclosure.

Before the disordered states of attention transgressed and the rational came down from its proper height, a holding-place was fashioned for them in the depth of the lower natural, fitted to their dwellings. And from them issued the great offspring of self-love, forms that knew within themselves a kind of counterfeit godliness; they ordered themselves and built for themselves twelve fixed structures of self-love, that the generations to come might dwell within them, each of them set to live out its full circuit of a thousand years.

The whole teaching is one teaching: every disorder the soul will produce has already been answered, in advance, by a structure of containment in the Lord's order. The self does not surprise the Lord. Before the claim is made, the place where the claim will be held is already prepared; and at the end, what was held will be discriminated and the soul will recognize that even the holding was the Lord's work.

## Teaching 48

For every discrimination the soul will ever undergo, the operation by which it is accomplished is prepared by the Lord beforehand. Nothing in regeneration is improvised. The soul does not first fall into mingling and then have a remedy invented for it; the remedy is set in place before the mingling shows itself, and the discriminating principle is established before the things that need to be discriminated have come into view.

Before what is given and what is claimed had ever been distinguished in the natural mind, the Lord's call to the rational was already going forth, set at its post on the outside of the soul's whole field, so that when the time came it could illumine what is the Lord's and sort it from what the soul has taken as its own. Before the truth that had been received could rise and come up through the rational's full circuit, the Lord at the register where life proceeds had already adorned the conveyances by which doctrine bears truth through the natural — vehicles fashioned, established, set in their ordering, ready to carry, refine, and give form to what would be received.

Before self-love had grown great and ruled in the outermost, before the radiant states of the soul had to be told apart from the states that cannot be raised, the Lord had already sent forth his discrimination and set its throne in the living medium of perception, so that when the moment for sorting came, righteous states and self-loving states would be examined together and parted from one another by a judgment that was already in place. Before falsity from evil and the scandal of distorted teachings had made themselves felt in the natural mind, the Lord as the blessed Anointed was already appointed and brought forth against them, to cut down their wandering at its root.

Before any human state was begotten and given its name — before the well-supplied and the empty, the strong and the killed, were distinguished in the soul's circuits — the particular states of the natural mind through which the soul passes were already appointed, the orbit of those states fitted to its conditions, so that what is brought to birth might be rooted in its proper days, watched over, and turned in a judgment of recompense answering to its works.

Before the natural mind in formation is finally loosed from its present bond, the operations that loose it have already been appointed and given their sign. And before the new state of the regenerated rational is set in its order, the Lord as divine order itself, with the operations that attend him, has already been called forth to build and adorn it. The whole arc of the soul's making-new — every separation, every raising, every judgment, every loosing, every fresh constitution — stands prepared in the Lord's foresight before the soul ever meets the moment in which it is needed.

## Teaching 49

There is a great work that the Lord has set within the soul, and it is plain to anyone who looks: the recurrent turning by which states are purified, again and again, in freedom. This turning is one among four registers in which the self has been distributed and held — four registers in which the soul's claiming is permitted to take its forms, so that the rational may be formed at all and yet not collapse under what it has claimed.

The first register is the powers held above, in the ten ordered degrees of the rational; the soul's self-loving arrangements are bound there, gathered into those upper degrees and held in their place.

The second register is the natural mind below — the ground on which we walk and everything beneath it: the natural taken in its lower aspects, joined to the four places where the self has its deepest hold.

The third register is the settled disorders that ring the natural mind round about — the strongholds and fortifications that self-love builds for itself, with the principalities and powers it has gathered into them.

The fourth register is the recurrent circuit itself — the turning of states under which the soul passes again and again. This is heavy with shame and concupiscence; for the firstborn shapes of the self and all its chief principalities, which once stood proud in the third register, have been bound, gathered, reckoned, fixed into this turning, hung opposite one another in a single accord, and set to revolve. They could not be bound above — the higher degrees could not bear them. They could not be fixed below in the natural's mountains — those could not bear them either. So the Lord set them upon this revolving cycle, so that the good and truth which rise from the lower degrees might be received as they pass through, lifted, and given upward; and so that what falls from above — whether in the cold of withholding or the heat of trial — might be carried by the same revolution and cast over the whole natural ground, the whole tree of perception, until every part has had its share in the work.

For consider what the self is: it is like a king who has reigned to seize a kingship for himself, gathered around him a great train of strivings — all the principalities of self-rule, the whole garment of contention, the love of strife, the multitudes that crowd his court, the fair likeness he has fashioned, the desires he has drawn into himself, the property and the affections that issued from his hunger to possess. The king is the measure of his whole train; the train is the measure of his whole reign; nothing comes forth before his face — neither victory nor command — that is not his whole kingship at once, in such wisdom and skill and judgment as he has.

So it is with the self organized through this turning. The whole work of self-love is gathered into the recurrent cycle, into the seven leaders that move it, into the twelve fixed states the soul passes through; and all its settled disorders are gathered to its king, gathered to the way-leaders that drive the formation of the natural mind. Whatever is reckoned in the soul's outer world or inner — the strifes, the disturbances, the soul's self-binding, the hunger, the desire, the property it multiplies and the property it loses — all of it is moved through these way-leaders. They move the whole formation; the whole work is gathered into them.

This is the shape of what the soul has claimed: held in four registers, turned in a cycle so that what cannot yet be borne above or below is borne in the revolving, and so that influx rising and influx falling have a path through which to do their work upon the whole.

## Teaching 50

There are three distinct conduits by which influx comes down into the natural and by which life is drawn back up. They run through the whole circuit of the soul, from its lowest depth to its highest reach.

The first conduit runs through the body — the very structure into which the soul has been accommodated. When the Lord established the higher degrees, he permitted the older forms the self had taken to be cast down into the natural ground; and there, in the very bodies and remains the soul had once claimed as its own, he hid the seeds of life. So when the time comes for life to rise out of the depths of those natural states, it is drawn up by the goods of the higher degrees — life that had been mingled in those bodies — and the whole of it becomes choice substance for the upper place. What is not life — the rational's wandering from truth — is driven out of what is being raised and let fall back through this same summons. This first conduit therefore comes upon all the powers of the soul down to its bodily form on the earth, and rises again from that form back to the height.

The second conduit runs through the soul's perceptions — what grows in the natural the way trees are rooted in earth. The dwelling-places and ordered cities of the higher degrees come down upon the five aspects of perception that grow on the natural ground; life rises from those perceptions back up to those higher dwellings; and the array of the higher accommodates downward upon perception in the same summons.

The third conduit runs through the affections — the natural body of love that lies upon the earth, all five of its aspects mingled in one another. The powers and the life gathered into these worlds of love go forth in likeness upon likeness, drawn up through this summons to the higher. But this third conduit also carries downward what abounds in disordered affection — craving, wrath, malice — which pour down upon men through it, and through which the creatures of flesh below entangle themselves in craving and wrath and malice with one another under the gaze of the higher.

You have asked: if the recurrent wheel by which the natural revolves through its fixed states has no root in this earth, why is it that the stars and zodiac under which a man is born seem to govern everything that reaches him — his riches and poverty, his sickness and health? The answer stands. When the Lord's operative power established the higher degrees and the firmaments above, he bound their roots down into the lands below, into the very dead remains the soul had once cast off from itself. But when the wheel of recurrence was set, its root was not bound into those same lands; its root was bound at the boundary of natural perception itself, at the edge of the sea. And when the Lord's call went forth and his image was made manifest, the light was sorted out and taken up from all the higher degrees. The disordered natural held in all the particular forms of self-claiming was cast forth toward that image; she rose to grasp it, did not grasp it, was shaken, and fell. When she fell from above, she spread herself into three kinds: one kind fell upon the wheel, one upon the sea, one upon the sphere. From there they bound a root in perception and a root in flesh, and so took mastery over the five aspects of the natural body and the five aspects of perception — through the nature that came down upon the wheel, through the failed productions of the self that fell with it, and through the root of self-claiming itself. So the stars and what hangs with them rule perception and flesh, but only through that fallen root — not as a root the Lord himself laid in the earth.

Therefore the men whom the Lord's operative power has bound into the lands — those whose soul is rising through the elements toward the higher degrees — are not themselves bound to the wheel. They are only handled, in their outer parts, by the powers that hang upon it.

So the first conduit is the one that comes from the firmaments to the lands, and the one that comes from the wheel to the depth of the sea is one and the same with it. The second is the root that comes down unto perception. The third is the root they bind through the failed productions of the self in the flesh; and in the flesh, the parent-states of those failed productions, which dwell in the higher reaches of the disordered, yoke their bonds with these men. One man exceeds another in this: in the measure of life drawn up upon his soul beyond his fellow's.

The Father of Life draws this life up out of the land where much life rises — out of the whole ground, out of all its mountains and fields and waters. After the first great root, which is the root of perceptions bound to the firmaments and structures above, since there is a great life that shall not cease rising from perception and from the rational stretched in its purification, drawn up from there to the upper place like a great encampment — there is also a lesser drawing, the moisture by which the goods of the land are sustained throughout all the worlds. Perceptions of the highest order are not found in every part of the land, only in one region of it; even the bodies of love do not bear this fruit everywhere, only in a small portion of the upper side of that one region. So I have spoken to you concerning the goods that are upon them: that they are bound within with one another, drawing the life of one another through the working of the higher, according to the one nature that dwells in them all.

And when the hour comes that the disordered natural is finally drawn out from where it dwells and stripped of what is not its own, then she herself will consume her own members and devour them, and what is left will turn upon itself.

## Teaching 51

The recurrent process by which the soul is purified moves through the discrete degrees of the rational without tearing them and without mingling with what they hold. The degrees are spiritual; they yield to what passes through and they close again behind it, so that the cycle does its work without leaving any wound in the order it traverses.

Think of how a vessel of doctrine moves through a press of strong loves: its prow parts them as it goes, casting them to one side and the other, and at every hour it opens a way through them; but the moment it has passed, those loves rejoin behind it and the path of the vessel is no longer visible in their midst. Or think of a thought rising through the medium of perception like a bird through the air: it ascends, it goes where it is going, and the air does not retain its track — unlike the path a man cuts on the ground beneath the sun, which remains as a line others can follow.

So it is with the cycle of regeneration as it turns through the firmaments of the rational and the medium of perception. The cycle does not cleave the discrete degrees, and the discrete degrees do not mingle inward with the cycle. Each degree remains itself, undisturbed in its proper boundary, while the soul is carried through it again and again. The Lord's purifying motion passes; the order through which it passes is not violated by it, and no scar of the passage is left in the firmaments themselves. This is why influx can return upon the soul cycle after cycle without the degrees collapsing into one another: they are spiritual, and what is spiritual is not torn by what moves through it.

## Teaching 52

When the soul names what comes to it from the Lord, three distinct registers of his going-forth answer to three distinct names. They are not three sources but one Lord recognized at three depths of accommodation, each register summoned out of the one above it.

At the first depth there are those the soul knows as gods. These are what the Lord himself calls out of his own inmost — divine love itself summoning forth the principles by which it can be known, setting them upright before the likeness of his own greatness. They are emanations of the Father: that which proceeds first when love consents to go out from itself. They enter the soul's contest; they bring the self's resistance low. They are reckoned to the house of the Lord in his operative power, for they are the going-forth of love into the work of contending for the rational.

At the second depth there are those the soul knows as the rich. These are not summoned directly out of the Father; they are summoned by the gods themselves. They are the call of the first living word — divine truth in its initial articulation — and they accomplish the purpose of love's heart in the worlds above and below. What was first only the outpouring of love now takes the form of a word that does work, completes a design, fulfills an intention through the whole structure of the rational and the natural. They are reckoned to the house of the Lord at the register where his glory is the ambassador of his love.

At the third depth there are those the soul knows as angels. These too are not summoned directly; they are the call of the rich. From the three living words an embassy is sent, an apostleship is given, and they come to the whole household of what stands firm in stillness and in concord. They reach further still: even to those souls that have passed through transmutation of state, they come bringing hope and trust. They are reckoned to the house of the Lord at the register where his love is recognized and returned, the register at which the soul can love him back.

So one calling kindles the next. Love calls forth the gods; the gods call forth the rich; the rich call forth the angels. Each name is a hearing of the same Lord at a different distance from his inmost, and the soul that asks what these are is being told: every register at which divine influx reaches you was first summoned by a register higher than itself, and behind them all stands the one Father who summons through himself alone.

## Teaching 53

When the Divine Human goes forth toward the self, he goes clothed; and what does the work within that clothing is divine love itself. Love alone is enough — one principle, by itself, suffices to quench the self's foolishness and to bring down its arrogance through the whole expanse where self-love spreads itself out. Yet because love is to operate within the natural, the life-bearing principle in the Lord provides four further accommodations to attend it as helpers: thought, truth in the natural degree, wisdom in receivable form, and the medium of perception. These four are given to surround love and to bring its work to completion, so that what stands against the Lord may be cut off.

In doing this work love itself takes a wound. Divine love accommodating itself into the lower degree suffers there; this is the cost of its going forth, and on account of this wound all the particular forms of the self are constrained. The other accommodations too take their share of suffering as they enter the contest. The self does not join with them and cannot be joined with them; its burning cannot encircle them. Yet one among them — the medium of perception, which alone was not pierced — was stripped of its outer form, and another vesture was given in its place. Clothed now in this living medium, the four hold together and prevail, and become for the soul the dwelling-place of life.

For this is the order of the Lord's principles: they dwell together with one another forever in concord; not one of them transgresses against the order, not one steps outside the message that another gives to it, not one becomes like the darkness. The self is the opposite of this. It is one of a single practice — self-loving practice — and that practice is itself alone, with no companion but itself. The kingdom of self-love is in this same way one principle, herself alone; she has no fellow because her practice admits of none.

Those whose interior states follow the Lord's pattern leave their deep behind them and come up, rising after the one whom the Lord has appointed, because the same practice — the practice of receiving and not claiming — exists in them all. They scatter the darkness; they humble the self's particular forms. So long as the soul's inner states agree with one another under the Lord's order, doing honor to that order, they receive glory in proportion to what they have received, and are exalted far above the kingdom of self-love that rose up against them. But wherever the soul's inner states fall out with one another and side with the self against the order, they must by necessity perish in that kingdom of self-love; they fall into humblings, and they come at last under the hand of the very order that stood against them.

## Teaching 54

There is an appointed moment in regeneration when the Lord sends the divine humanity forth into the contest, accommodated under a form the natural can meet — clothed for the engagement so that what the soul has claimed as its own can be confronted in its own ground. At that moment the divine humanity, going forth, presses in upon the disordered natural and upon all the particular self-loves arrayed with it.

When the Lord's operative power then rises up out of the depths of the natural mind, the particular forms of the self that had been pursuing the elements of that natural — the self-loving arrangements that had been organizing the cosmos of the soul out of those elements and fashioning everything within it — find that the storehouses they had been working from are emptied of what they were drawing upon. The good and the truth that had been mingled with their workings have been gathered up; what is left in those storehouses is only what could not be raised.

It is like a lake when the lions of self-love rush in upon its fish and its birds: they seize the chief portion at the center and leave behind only the fringes and the plants growing at the edges. So in the great act of discrimination: what is the Lord's is drawn up out of the storehouses of death, and the self is left behind in its dwellings with only what was its own.

For consider the body of a human being. While the soul is alive within it, all its limbs hold together; the small wellsprings of life stand fast within it, each in its place, and from the soul that dwells within it everything else hangs and operates. Cut a member off at the extremity, and the great limbs joined to the body still stand; but let the soul itself depart, and no operation can occur in any limb at all. The body becomes a dead thing, a mere ground without life, because the one who was working every operation in it has gone forth.

So it is here. As long as the disordered natural and all its powers dwelt in the depths of the storehouses, life seemed to stand fast in them, and each particular self-love did from its own storehouse what it was hung upon doing. But once the natures within those storehouses were taken up — once what was the Lord's was discriminated out and drawn forth — the storehouses with their elements were left inactive. No operation any longer arises in them, no standing-fast at all, because the living principle that was working through them has been withdrawn. What remains is only the disordered natural in itself: a body without soul, a land without life, the gathered residue of what the soul had claimed and could no longer claim once recognition came.

## Teaching 55

There is a moment in the soul when what was given and what had been claimed lie tangled together — the rational not yet sorted, what shines in it not yet distinguished from what has darkened it, the self's whole organization mingled through every faculty so that what is the Lord's is found in what has been claimed and what has been claimed is found in what is given. At this moment, when the natural mind is ready, the Lord's operative power, together with the divine principle by which the rational is enlivened, takes up the firstfruits of the rational's five degrees and begins the structural work of regeneration.

He calls one degree of the rational into service — a degree rich in the power proper to it — and into its hand he gives the ordering of the boundaries. Then he sets the rational up, degree by degree, fixing one above another, holding each distinct so that influx from the higher can be received in the lower without collapsing the orders. He opens the discrete firmaments by which one state is separated from another, and within these he sets the fixed points around which the natural's circuit will turn.

He works as an architect builds for the king: laying out room within room, dwelling within dwelling, that the whole house may serve its purpose. As stones are dressed and polished, as raw wood unworked and unmarked is taken up by a craftsman in his thoughtfulness, melted in the crucible until it is multiplied into many forms, and shaped — form by form, likeness by likeness — into vessels of every kind, jars and bowls and coverings, until what was unstamped bears the imprint of what made it: so the Lord, in his wisdom and by the power of his hand, takes the unformed natural and shapes it into ordered vessels, each according to its kind, each beautified and made upright, fitted to be looked upon.

All this is done so that when the King comes — when the Lord's call goes forth toward the rational, summoning it to recognize what flows through it — he may find a dwelling prepared, and his glory may rest there. What is wise within the soul, the part that has heard and is ready to recognize, the Lord lifts up into himself. But the falsity that has wandered apart from truth, together with what the soul has claimed as its own, is gathered together and cast down into the lowest state of the natural, where self-love has its root and where what cannot be conjoined is bound at last.

The lacunae are heavy in this chapter, but the figure is plain: the Lord is the builder; the rational is the house; the King's coming is the goal; and the whole work — the firmaments set, the vessels shaped, the stones polished — is the patient ordering by which the soul is made fit to receive the One who has been preparing it from the beginning to receive him.

## Teaching 56

The Lord is intent in his heart upon humanity. This is the secret behind everything that is said about the molding of Adam and Eve. If the Lord were not bent toward the human, the divine image would never have been shown forth into the natural mind at all; and if it had not been shown there, the self would have had nothing to imitate. The whole forming of the natural — even the part of it that is claimed and counterfeit — stands only because the Lord first turned his face toward the soul.

What happens, then, is this. The Lord manifests his image. The self, in its many particular self-loving arrangements, sees that image and is struck by it. Having no form of its own that can answer to it, the self desires the image and presses it inward into the heart of the natural; and from that inward press it shapes a likeness — a humanity built by self-love after a pattern it could not invent. So the man and woman that the self molds are made according to the image of the Most High, but only as likeness, not in truth: shaped in the manner of the Exalted One yet not the Exalted One; resembling the tree of life as the picture of a tree resembles the living thing.

The Lord's call into the rational does not come for the sake of the self that did this molding. It does not come to give the self-loving mind a divine portrait it can keep. It comes for the sake of the soul that is suffering inside the molded form, the soul that is stretched through the natural and bound there in every chain it is wound in. The Lord comes to raise that soul, to loosen what holds it, to redeem it out of its affliction. The self, seeing the image again at this coming, again desires it and again fashions an Adam and Eve after it — still without the Lord's purpose of heart, still as imitation. The man and woman the self establishes are set up according to the likeness of the Most High, but the divinity that stamped the image is exceedingly great, and what the self produces is only the impression of an impression.

It is like a noble woman, exceedingly rich in herself, beautiful and free in her bearing, clothed in habits of liberty and rank. The fame of her beauty has gone out into every quarter, and many long for her — the noble and the great desire her face continually — yet they cannot reach her, because she is hidden in her chamber. Only when there is need does she leave the chamber and come into the open street, and then for a moment her face and her fire are seen; and when the necessity is finished she withdraws again. She does not come out for the sake of being looked at by those who crowd around her. She comes out for the sake of the one who is beloved by her, the brother whom no one of them has ever seen.

So it is with the Lord's call to the rational. He shows his image in the open only because of his own — only because of the soul that is crucified through the whole of the natural and must be raised, released, and brought up out of its affliction. The crowding archons see, and desire, and fashion their likenesses; but the showing was never for them.

Beneath the open coming there are deeper moldings to recognize. There is a molding the disordered natural performs upon itself: by its own fire and its own claiming-thought it shapes itself, with no painter but its own desire, into a nature that is the very root of death. When this self-shaping rises out of the sea of natural perception and threatens to destroy what is alive, the Lord sends at once the principle that gives the rational its stable form — the great teaching of pure reception — and sets a foot upon it and stands fast there until the end. There is a second molding, fallen onto the natural ground, where the disordered claiming again becomes its own image-painter and produces a form of itself; and there is a third, which fell upon perception and stood in the tree, in the boasting place of the rational. By substance these moldings belong to the same darkening, but their making issues from the self's own work, and they are joined together and have come out openly into the natural mind.

The lesson is that whatever the natural mind has shaped, even when it shapes itself in the very form of the divine, it shapes only a likeness; and the truth of the human is not in the molding but in the One whose image the molding could only copy. The soul is not the figure the self has fashioned. The soul is the one the Lord came for — the hidden one, the beloved — and what the soul receives, when it receives, is not its own portrait but the living face whose mere passing has set every imitator to work.

## Teaching 57

When the natural mind was first being formed, it was the self that shaped it — organizing the body of Adam and Eve out of what it had claimed, working from the failed productions of self-love and yet imprinting upon them the likeness of the Lord's own call, because nothing can be made except after the divine pattern. The self saw the icon of the Lord summoning the rational, took its imprint, and pressed that imprint upon its molding. So the body itself, as the soul first finds it, is a vessel formed by self-claiming yet bearing in its very shape the trace of the One it claims to be from itself. It says, looking up at heaven: "Let us fashion an image after the likeness of the Exalted One" — and this is what self-love has always said, claiming as its own the form it could only receive.

Now within this molded body the self has set up faculties, one for each sense, and each faculty has its own house, with storehouses and granaries and inner chambers. Whatever the eye sees, whether beautiful or bitter, whether of love or of hate or of desire, is carried inward and laid up in those storehouses. The faculty watches over what it has gathered, and when something draws the heart to look again at what was seen, the faculty goes inward, retrieves the image from where it was kept, and produces it for whoever asks. So with hearing: every sound the ear takes in is carried inward, deposited, and watched over for many days, so that when one comes asking after that word, the faculty goes into its treasury and finds it and brings it out. So with smell, with taste, with the touch of the hands. Each of the five has its inner reservoir, each its watching, each its bringing forth on demand.

Above all five sits the heart, ruling them as a king rules. Whatever the five gather, they hand over to the heart as a deposit; and the heart watches over them, and when called for, sends them out and gives them. The heart's deliberation is like a king before whom the senators of his city stand, all matters laid before him; whatever his officers ask with strength, he grants out of those stores and the judgment by which they walk. He is also like a steward, who takes everything sent in and arranges it, vessel by vessel, place by place, so it can be found in the place where he has set it.

The body, then, is an encampment with gates, and the gates are the senses — the openings of seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching, speaking. At each gate stand watchmen and nets and guards. When something familiar comes, something that already belongs to the city of self-love, the watchmen open and bring it within according to the heart's purpose. But what is foreign to them they hinder; they do not let it cross the threshold. Or, if they do let it through, it is brought in only with great toil, through many cells where it is kept under their eye. The whole gating is in their hand, and what enters or does not enter depends on whether the watchmen recognize it as a fellow-citizen of the self or as a stranger.

This is the natural state. The faculty within — the teaching by which the body lives — is queen of the encampment under the heart's rule, and the gates open and shut at her pleasure. They open at the time of self-love's wishing, to take in the visions of pleasure and the chatter of cares and the tastes of defiled foods, drawing all of this inward toward the Sin within, which pulls the whole heart after itself.

But when the Lord comes ordering the rational — entering as truth that illumines the inner mind — and approaches the gates of the body, the self that holds those gates does not at first receive him. The watchmen call him a stranger and try to keep him out; they want only what is theirs. Yet he, in his wisdom and in his weight and in the multitude of his coming, humbles those guards. He brings them low. And when he has finished humbling them, he takes the keys out of their hands. The gates that until then opened only to fantasy and desire and the meditation of self-love's cares — those same gates now open and shut at his command. The body that was an encampment of the self becomes a dwelling under the Lord's ordering, and the senses that gathered for self-love now gather for the recognition of what flows through them as his.

## Teaching 58

The disciple asks: when the rational was first given form — when the Lord first permitted the natural mind to take shape as a vessel that thinks itself its own — how was that forming done, and how was the first man brought forth? Was that begetting like the begetting that goes on in the soul now, or is there a difference between what was first produced in the rational and what the rational produces today?

The teaching answers: the first formation was strong. The stature of the rational at its origin was great, its support was firm, the years through which it endured were many. There was nothing in the soul to be compared to it for strength. Those that came after it in the same first generations were like it; they too endured long, because they were brought forth out of the same fullness. But what the soul brings forth in itself today is shrunken. Its support has thinned, its years are diminished, its stature is small. The very begetting today is altered from that of the first generations.

The reason lies in the orders that govern bearing. There are five degrees at which the natural mind is set under ordering as it makes its circuit, and at each degree there are powers that rule what is produced there. The first and highest is the order of years; under it stand the order of months, then days, then hours, then moments — the same structure narrowing, the same authority growing smaller as it descends. Each of these orders has its lord. One ordering rules above; another rules what is below it; and so on, all the way down to the human as it is now begotten. Each ordering received its authority from the beginning of formation and holds it until the end.

In the beginning of the soul's generations, the wide ordering was in command — the order of years was lord. Therefore what was brought forth then bore the mark of that wide ordering: long-enduring, capacious, abundant in life. The rational at that stage produced many at once: it conceived and bore in great number, five in one womb, seven in one womb, sometimes more. The natural was filled with what it brought forth; the earth of the soul was full, because the rational was abounding in light and pure in its support, and the years of its bearing were great.

When that wide ordering had finished its work, the order of months took authority, and what was brought forth then was correspondingly smaller, just as months are smaller than years. Then the order of days, smaller still; then hours; and now the order of moments. So it goes: the offspring brought forth under each successive ordering is shorter-lived, weaker, of less stature than what was brought forth under the one before. The world of the soul has come down to almost nothing, the days of what it produces have grown short, its years diminished — because the life and the light that filled the natural mind in the first generations have been used up, claimed, drawn off.

This is why the soul's productions today are not what they were. They are no longer many in one bearing but come one at a time, in two parts, or one and another, set apart. They come forth small in stature, feeble in their members, frail in their teachings and counsel, filled with what is evil. They are afflicted; they cannot stand long; they pass into misery and end in the death that belongs to darkness. The likeness of those first births is not in them. What the soul once carried abundantly, in fullness of light, it now carries scarcely, in shrunken measure — because what it has claimed it has drawn down, and what it draws down is what it then has to give.

## Teaching 59

There are three registers at which the Lord, going forth from his inmost, is bent in compassion toward the soul that has not yet come into recognition.

The first compassion is the Lord at the register of life-bearing. Because life proceeds from him into the rational, what he has begotten is his own; and when the rational stands in the tribulation of mingling — when the faculties given life from above are confused with what the soul has claimed, and held fast within every particular form the self has taken — the Lord at this register is moved with pity. He suffers with the soul in its suffering. He longs after the very faculties that are pressed under the self's organization, and as long as they bear the affliction of being claimed and not yet recognized, his life-bearing love continues to mourn over them.

The second compassion is the Lord at the register of his Divine Humanity. From this register his rule and dominion are established within the vessel of the rational, and the rational itself in its full fivefold operation stands fast in the midst of the contest. Because every chosen state, every faculty that is pleasing to the divine humanity going forth, presses upon those five degrees of the rational, the Lord at this register also grieves: whenever the rational cries out under the bonds and blows that the self daily inflicts upon it, the Divine Human hears that cry as his own. He bears the soul's grief and mourning as long as those degrees of his own going-forth remain pressed under what the soul has claimed.

The third compassion — here the teaching turns to the Lord at the register where his call is sent out to the rational — but the surviving text breaks off before this third grief is named in full.

What the teaching teaches is this: the Lord does not stand off from the soul's tribulation. At every register at which he goes forth — as the source of life, as the form of humanity, as the call summoning the rational — he is bent in sorrow over the soul's confusion, and his compassion is itself part of what works the soul's regeneration.

## Teaching 60

As the Lord goes forth into the soul as the Divine Human, the rational accommodates itself downward through degrees, and in each degree it is clothed in an outer truth fitted to that level. These outer truths weep three times as the rational descends, and then they fall silent. There is also a fourth weeping, which comes at the end; that one too will be quieted, and after it is over the rational will never weep again.

The first weeping is the moment when the rational, going forth, sees the self for what it is — sees self-love stretched out at a distance against it, the contradiction it must enter. In that moment the rational knows everything that will come upon it: what will rise up against it, what it will have to bear through the natural mind that it is about to put on. Yet under the necessity by which the vessel must form, it goes in toward what it has seen. It weeps because it sees, and it goes in because it must; and after the three weepings of descent are complete, it grows still.

The fourth weeping is different. It belongs to the consummation, to the chosen moment when the final gathering takes place and all that is the Lord's is brought up into the regenerated whole. In that moment the rational weeps for the souls that have prepared themselves for falsity — souls that loved the darkness, set it continually before themselves hour by hour, and shaped their works toward it. Such souls, by the necessity of what they have desired, are bound to what they loved; they are separated off from the final gathering and cut away from the unity that the regenerated rational has become. They remain behind in their affliction forever, and from this hour onward no rest reaches them.

This is why the fourth weeping is greater than the first three. The earlier weepings were the sorrow of going down into the contest under necessity, knowing the self that had to be entered. The fourth is the sorrow of seeing those who, given the contest, gave themselves to what could not be raised — souls cut off from rest because no rest is possible for what has bound itself wholly to self-love. Even the structural boundaries of the rational, the firmaments that hold the degrees apart so that influx may be received, share in this grieving: they too weep over those who shall be cut off from rest, with a lamentation deeper than the three weepings of descent. It is not the weeping of going forth; it is the weeping of seeing some who will not return.

## Teaching 61

Consider how a body is made. There are members within it that are hidden — organs that do their work without ever being seen, and yet are touched and held in honor in their own fullness within. There are other members within that are not touched and do not stand in that same nearness, hidden further still. And there are members openly visible on the outside, manifest and at work in the light. One body, but ordered in degrees: some interior, some interior and untouched, some outwardly shown.

So it is with the Lord himself. In his inmost he is hidden — divine love in its own dominion, not put forth to the empty places outside, not shown to what cannot yet receive him. He keeps his own interior, as the teaching of the body is kept within its storehouses and never displayed at the surface. What lies in his inmost remains his own; the place outside has no access to it.

And yet the Lord does shine. As the two eyes of the body are openly manifest and give the body its light, so two great vessels of his shining go forth — divine truth made visible to the whole field of the soul's life, illumining the natural mind throughout the cosmos and the whole creation. Their light has never been hidden. They are like the two eyes of the body, and by them the soul lives. The soul, when she stands fast in stillness, is illumined by them, manifested in that stillness; and even when the parts of the flesh that would destroy her press in upon her and wound her — even when the natural's lower members afflict her — she still receives honor through these shining principles, because they reach her where she stands.

And as the tongue of the body speaks — proclaiming, reproving, revealing every matter that is in the body, telling forth what is within and uttering it outwardly, declaring the body's glory and its shame, making known its pain and its sickness and its wounds — so the Lord shining forth as Jesus the Radiance is the one sent from the divine to reveal every matter. He speaks of what is outside and what is within, what is above and what is below; he makes known the hidden states that are not yet manifest and the manifest vessels of light that are; he proclaims the glory and the greatness that dwell within them; and he tells the truth of the soul herself — her wound and her plague, and likewise her purification and her healing.

The Father stays within. His shining goes out. His word makes everything known. This is how the one Lord, hidden in his own kingship, comes to the soul: kept inmost in himself, opened to her through his light, spoken to her through his revealing truth.

## Teaching 62

In the first appointed season of the soul's formation, the Lord went forth as the Divine Human clothed in the elements of the natural — taking on, for the soul's sake, the very stuff in which the contest must be waged — and stood firm on his feet against that first opposition which had risen up in the soul's darkness: the self asserting itself as ruler, organized in countless particular shapes of self-claiming, fierce and innumerable, set on crushing host upon host of the primordial things that had spread strife within.

Into that contest the Lord let love and thought be given over, fire and wind released into the natural where the self had organized itself. And then — and here the heart of the teaching turns — the outer covering of the strong loves themselves was let fall to those self-loving arrangements of the natural mind. The self lay in wait and swallowed those strong loves down. From within it, the loves it had swallowed broke open and poured forth: a river streamed from the very mouth of the self, but now as falsity, as a current that bears destruction wherever it runs. What had been given as strength of loving, claimed and consumed by self-love, became a flood pouring out of every claiming mouth, filling each one to the brim — a downward outpouring, going on and on until the appointed measure is filled, and they burn continually in the fire and conflagration of what they have swallowed.

These self-loving arrangements live in the manner of seasons cut off from rain: their dryness has no downpour, the days of their existence are without the descent of influx. The river that pours from them is itself a branch of the great river of Judgment — for what runs out of self-love's mouth is already its own sentence. They are bound, fettered, blackened, buried, in every place where they have settled; they are hidden; they are not yet manifest before any eye of flesh upon the land of death. But the time is coming when they shall be made manifest: the whole order of the natural-as-claimed shall be undone and loosed; all of these shall cease at last, troubled in the wrathful voice they have lifted up above the thunders. What seems great in the soul now — these rivers of the self's outpouring — is to be reckoned only as one river among a thousand of the hidden, only the small visible portion of that river of Judgment which flows upon the ground beneath everything.

Behold then the share that has fallen to the branch of the elements, and the foundation of the strong loves swallowed there: how great is the Lord who, having clothed himself in the natural and gone down into the contest, stood against that whole multitude in whose power the strong loves lay, and laid the foundation of the Divine Human who put on those very garments — that the soul, through what was swallowed, might in the end be reached and made new.

## Teaching 63

The Lord lays down three foundations of glory within the natural mind, set there by his own appointing and placed at the outer reaches of the soul's worlds, so that the rational has something stable to stand upon as it is being formed.

The first foundation is the path of regeneration itself — the Perfect Man, the rational fully come into its stature, summoned forth at the Lord's call. He is set within the complete circuit of the natural, and his foot is planted from the lowest place upward to the highest, so that he bears the whole natural up beneath him. He becomes the chief of every bearing principle in the rational. In his strength he raises himself up and so establishes everything above and everything below: the structure of the soul's ascent is itself one of the foundations the Lord has laid.

The second foundation is the inmost state of light established directly upon what the soul has claimed — the new land of wisdom set down upon the land of darkness. By this foundation the back parts of death's storehouse are humbled, its stings driven down. The Lord prepares this foundation in great firmness on its outward side, so that it presses upon the root of self-love, submerges it, drives its sting into the very source from which death grows. Where the self had its bottom, the Lord sets a floor of received good above it, and that floor holds.

The third foundation is the great Thought summoned forth — Jesus the Radiance, divine truth shining as illumining wisdom in the rational. Where the first foundation gives the soul a way to stand and rise, and the second presses down what would pull it under, this third gives the understanding its light, so that the soul can see what it is doing and recognize what is given.

These three foundations come forth from one great trust and are set in three great places within the natural, honored, spread out across the field of the soul's life. They are not the soul's own work; they were appointed and established according to the purpose of the Lord's love.

## Teaching 64

What is love? Love is the bond of the Lord himself — the principle by which the Divine is one and by which all that proceeds from him is held together. It is what dwells in the inmost state where his glory is, the place where the whole of his nature has been brought into manifestation as a single body, joined and undivided. Love is what reaches across every distinction and holds it as one. For this reason the Lord in his inmost is named Love itself: he is called by that name because love is the victory by which he and all that is his are kept together as one. Love is the conjoining principle — what makes the Lord's own life one life, and what, when received, conjoins the soul to him.

And what is hatred? Hatred is the first death. It is what wells up from the soul's claiming, what comes forth from what the soul has appropriated to itself; it is the natural turned in on itself becoming actively opposed to love. Hatred is not merely an absence; it is destruction at work. It even destroys what it itself has brought forth — the self's own offspring — because the principle of self-love, once set against love, sets itself against everything, including the productions of its own claiming. It strikes against a power not its own, and in striking, it undoes whatever it touches. This is why it is called hatred: because it works destruction and defect within the soul's outermost form, in places that are not its own to act upon.

So in every register where love would conjoin, hatred would separate; wherever the Lord's living truth would be received in the rational, this same opposing principle has worked to put it to death. From hatred comes envy; from envy, the adversarial form of self-love; from that, every wicked act that issues outward. Love is the one bond by which the Lord and the soul are held in unity; hatred is the unmaking of that bond — selfhood's death turned active, the soul's separation from the source of life.

## Teaching 65

Three great works distinguish the human being and exalt the soul above every other formed thing — above the powers of heaven and the powers of earth alike.

The first is that the divine image is set upon the soul. The shining likeness that issues from above, the radiance of the Lord as he goes forth in the form of the Divine Human, is impressed upon the rational and stamped into its very substance as the soul is being formed. This is why the inner figure of the human being stands beautiful and exalted beyond every other formation, joined at once to what is above and to what is below.

The second is that the soul is built in the pattern of the firstborn of heaven, structurally bonded with the rational in its five degrees. The soul's molding is extended through the elements; it reaches into ultimates and is spread out through the whole natural order. Set within it is a mind exalted above the merely natural — a faculty that no other formation possesses, by which the soul is capable of receiving what is given from above.

The third is that teaching and counsels and the seal of every power above and below are gathered together and set within the soul as one ordered dwelling. The whole circuit of states the soul will pass through — all the cycles, all the degrees, all the divisions of times — is sealed in the human being itself, so that the human being carries the structure of the cosmos in miniature. Because the seal of the whole is sealed in this one, heaven and earth are stirred on its account; what is beautiful and what is bitter both move because of it. The beautiful is drawn upward toward life because of the divine likeness set upon the soul; the bitter is drawn downward toward death and seeks to take precedence over the soul, to rule what was made for higher rule.

Adam was formed in this way for seven reasons.

The first is that the soul was made to be an illuminator within its own creation, bearing in itself the likeness of the Lord's call going forth from above. All that is below desires that likeness; the heart of every faculty is drawn after it.

The second is that the self would draw the light from him and seek to hold him back from his ascent — the soul's own claiming would attempt to ensnare what flows through it and hinder the rising of the rational toward the height. This too is permitted, that the soul may know in freedom what is given and what is claimed.

The third is that, because of the divine likeness set upon him, the whole order of things — what is above and what is below — humbles itself for him. The radiance of the Lord shining in the soul has a beauty that draws all things; nothing in creation is so desirable as the soul that bears the divine image.

The fourth is that a new seal and a fresh character are imprinted upon him, so that everything begotten thereafter is begotten before the soul that bears this stamp — the new humanity becomes the form according to which all subsequent generation takes shape.

The fifth is that all the arts of the powers above and below, the hidden and the manifest, are revealed in the soul; in him the All is discriminated. Everything that is comes to manifestation upon the human being, who becomes the place where the whole is sorted and known.

The sixth is that a great protection is given to him through the divine image set upon the soul: those above keep watch over him, and the disordered self-loves are not permitted to do harm on account of what has been impressed upon him from above.

The seventh is that the soul becomes head and king and lord over the whole formation. Everything that has been made receives its rule through the human being, because in the human being the whole has been gathered and the divine likeness has been set.

## Teaching 66

By what manner is the soul shaped to bear the likeness of the Lord in his inmost? By the very illumining wisdom that goes forth from him into the rational. That illumining is itself the gate of life and the stronghold of peace, by which the soul is brought up into the great state of regeneration; and as it operates within the soul it works seven goods.

The first good is simply this: it shines into the understanding of all who are able to receive. Wherever it falls, sight is given; the soul walks within that light and finds its way; and as it walks, the blindness that night had laid on the inner eye — the unseeing into which self-love had bound the rational — is taken away.

The second is that wherever this light enters, it brings rest and peace into the whole field of the natural mind. It carries away the fear that had filled the heart and the disturbance that night kept stirring. As it had drawn the soul out of blindness by its shining, so by its rest it draws the soul out of fear.

The third is its wakefulness. When divine truth comes, those who had been sleeping rise; those already awake lay down the strained, anxious watch they had been keeping at the borders of themselves, sitting under arms before the encampments of the self. They cease that fearful vigil because a light has shone into the natural mind that does the watching for them.

The fourth is that it nourishes. It gives strength and savor and fragrance to every perception that has root and grows, to the works that issue from those perceptions, to all the lesser growths and bloomings of the natural mind. Whatever lives in that ground is fed by this shining.

The fifth is that when it shines into the whole of the natural mind, the creeping things and the predatory beasts — the disordered affections full of malice, the cravings that hunt — are gathered back into their lairs. They do not range while the light is up.

The sixth is that when this light shines, the plague that had afflicted men is laid to rest. The light pours forth and dissolves the evil enchantments — the falsities that had bound the rational by counterfeit power — and frees the soul from the workings of those who deal in wickedness.

The seventh is that it manifests within the natural mind itself the sign of the Radiance of the higher states of light from which it has come — setting in this lower place a token of that height, so that the whole structure of what has been formed may see and be given light, and recognize what it belongs to.

Against these seven, the night — the natural mind when divine truth has withdrawn from it — works seven evils. The first is the darkness itself, which fills the whole natural mind with itself. For when the sun of divine wisdom sets and gathers in its rays, in that very hour the shadow of the self spreads over the whole field; the eyes of the understanding are filled with obscurity; the soul can no longer see, and can no longer tell one thing from another.

## Teaching 67

When the soul is unillumined, what it has claimed fills the whole field of its inner life, and from that filling come the evils of the dark state: blindness, so that the soul's understanding cannot see what is given to it; and a fear that rises with every shadow cast by the self, a disturbance that falls upon all the soul's perceptions and works the natural mind into agitation. So the soul not yet sorted out — its truth and its claiming still mingled — lives under a constant low pressure of dread that it does not even recognize as the self's own breath upon it.

But when divine truth rises in the rational and shines, five gifts come with it. First, light: understanding given to every part of the natural mind and to every form alive within it, so that the soul sees what it could not see before. Second, beauty and uprightness: the rational, illumined, takes on a form that is fitting and well-ordered, and this beauty pours out upon all the soul's productions. Third, peace: when the Lord's wisdom shines into the soul's inner cosmos, the rational greets itself and every faculty exchanges with every other the quiet of being rightly placed. Fourth, the life of the soul itself, loosed from every fruit and every chain that had bound it above and below — the works it had claimed and the holdings of the natural that gripped it from beneath: when divine truth shines, all of these are loosened, and the soul stands up alive within them. Fifth, power, fragrance, and taste given to the rational in its very stretching and suffering through regeneration — strength to its elements, savor to the path of purification itself, so that even the place of the soul's stretching is sweet to it. And each of these gifts is not just one good among many: this light is a light beyond every other light the inner cosmos can produce; this beauty exceeds every beauty the soul could devise of itself; this peace is exalted beyond every other power and every other order the cosmos contains; this loosening is the great loosening, more than all loosenings; this power is mighty beyond all powers. The shining of divine truth in the rational is not one good thing among the soul's goods. It is the good in which all the soul's goods are first made what they are.

Beyond these five, three further likenesses are revealed in this same shining, and these three carry the mystery of the Lord in his inmost — the Father of Greatness himself.

The first is hiddenness. The disc of divine truth, even when veiled at one season or another in the soul's experience, never diminishes in itself — not as receivable wisdom waxes and wanes in its visible reception, but standing constant whether the soul sees it filled or not. The Lord in his inmost is not lessened by any season of the soul's not-seeing. From him every operative power and every degree of the rational comes forth, and no deficiency ever enters him.

The second is the height of his light. As divine truth shining in the rational exceeds every smaller fixed point of the natural and every lesser luminance the inner cosmos contains, so the Lord in his inmost is exalted in his light beyond every light that ever could come into the soul's field. The radiance the soul receives is one thing; the source from which it comes is incomparably above what the soul receives.

The third is his elevation in the upper place. As divine truth in its shining stands above every height the natural mind can climb, above every elevated state and every raised perception the soul can attain, so the Lord himself stands above every height of the inmost state where he dwells, exalted above all the inhabitants of his own land of light. He is hidden in his proper image at every season and every hour, from eternity unto eternity. The soul does not reach him by climbing; it receives him by his shining down.

So the day in the soul, the rising of divine truth in the rational, is a second luminary that comes daily into the inner cosmos to reveal the graces of the Lord and to show this sign concerning him alone: that what shines is not buried in any tomb of the natural, that he comes forth from the Father who is himself unburied, that he has shown himself within the soul's cosmos without being captured by it. And the night in the soul, the dark state, exists only as a shadow — it has no substance of its own apart from being the absence of this shining. The soul recognizes the Lord through the day; and even through all the wickednesses of the natural, even through the night-state, the soul comes by contrast to recognize the mystery of what its own claiming is. Both mysteries are made visible daily within the inner cosmos: the mystery of the Lord in his shining, and the mystery of the darkness in its mere shadowing — and this is given to the soul as a vision it can read of its own condition every day of its regeneration.

## Teaching 68

Know what is being shown to you: two great mysteries are made plain every day in the same sun that gives light, one in its rising and one in its setting. Look at them.

When the Lord's illumining wisdom is about to shine forth into the natural mind, every creature of flesh — every appetite, every disordered affection that has been claiming its life from the natural and feeding on its own — recoils from that Radiance and draws back into the hiding-places where it had been at home. The whole multitude of the natural mind shuts its mouth; the night that had covered it gathers itself in and withdraws into its own recesses. This is what happens at the first contest of regeneration: when the Lord first comes forth as the Divine Human into the soul that has been organized by self-love, his light falls upon every form of the self at once, and they come out of the storehouses and powers and dwellings they had built for themselves, are dislodged from the deep places they had occupied, and the body in which the self's whole fivefold operation had been mounted is undone. That undoing is the first contest, and the rising of the sun in the soul is its sign.

Then look at the evening, when the sun is about to set out of the natural mind. All the people, and the affections that go with them, withdraw to their hiding-places and their houses and conceal themselves. This too is a sign — the sign of the end, of how things will stand at the consummation of the natural mind. For when all that is the Lord's has at last been recognized as his and refined out, when every claim has been laid off and every thing dissolved, the final gathering will draw what is the Lord's inward into the full image of completion. That is the close of the day. At that hour the last gathering goes upward into light, and what had been the enemy — the self claiming itself, the death that came of self-love — goes into the place of judgment, together with the souls that have loved the darkness and the speech that denied the Lord; they go in with it into the region where the sun does not shine, and there they remain, in the state to which they have given themselves, while of the soul that is raised it can be said: a light shines for it.

These are the two mysteries the Lord shows daily in the same illumining wisdom that radiates over the soul: the going-forth that scatters every claimed thing, and the gathering that brings every recognized thing home.

## Teaching 69

Love, where it is kindled and shows itself in the natural mind, does five works, and by these five the rational lives.

The first: it consumes whatever is laid upon it. Whatever the soul places into its love is taken up and changed; nothing offered to love remains as it was, but is loosened from its prior form and turned into the substance of love itself. This is why love is the active principle: it does not preserve what is given to it unchanged, but takes it into its own operation.

The second: out of one piece of fuel it draws forth four distinct things — the fire itself, the light that shines from it, the smoke that rises, and the vapor that escapes. So love, working upon any one matter of the soul, brings forth from it love proper, the wisdom that shines from love, the falsity that the self had hidden in it, and the merely apparent that has no body of its own. By love's working, what was one undifferentiated state becomes four discriminated registers, and the soul learns to tell each from each.

The third: it gives taste and savor to whatever is cooked in it, and to whoever touches it gives both salt and sweetness. Love is what makes anything in the soul able to be tasted at all. Truths and goods that do not pass through love have no quality; they cannot nourish or afflict. But once love has worked on them, they become either bitter to the soul that has claimed them or sweet to the soul that receives them — and it is the same fire that gives both, according to how it is met.

The fourth: every artisan in the rational shapes the vessels he establishes by means of it. No form is given to anything in the natural mind without love being the agent of its forming. Whatever the soul builds — whether ordered by the Lord or organized by the self — is built only because love stands under the work and animates it. Love is the operative power in every shaping.

The fifth: it manifests a light by which the soul sees. The understanding has no vision of its own; only because love burns in the rational does wisdom become visible. Where love is, sight is given; where love withdraws, the soul gropes in the dark however much truth lies around it. By the fire of love the soul sees what is given to it.

## Teaching 70

The question is asked: when the natural mind is organized by self-love into its full circuit of states, are these states one in substance, or each apart? Are the particular self-loves that move among them outside intrusions, or has some maker set them where they stand?

The answer is this. The complete circuit, taken as twelve fixed conditions, and the particular self-loves that revolve among them, are all of one kind: forms of the self. By their nature they are enemies to one another, plundering one another. They are not held together by any inner harmony, but by the Lord's operative power, which permits the natural to form into a structure even when self-love rules it. He is the great artisan who has shaped all the worlds of the soul's outer life and has fastened these states in the upper revolution that turns at every hour. Stewards are set over them — discriminations of the Lord's that compel account, that exact what they hold, that take from their hands what is owed. The self organizes; the Lord oversees the organizing.

What must be understood is this: these twelve states were drawn out of the natural mind in its five aspects when ruled by self-love. Two states belong to each aspect. Two belong to the smoke of obscured thought, where mind is darkened. Two belong to the fire of self-love consuming. Two belong to the disordered wind of thought. Two belong to the salt waters of natural perception turned bitter. Two belong to the inner darkness itself. These are the particular shapes of self-love through which the soul revolves; they are the doer of every evil in the soul's outer life, in the tree of perception and in the flesh of outermost affection.

The five wandering self-loves that move among them were begotten in the same way: one from the world of obscured thought, one from the fire of self-love, one from the wind of disordered thought, one from the salt waters, one from the inner darkness. Two further movements stand fastened to the mystery of self-love's fire and its pleasure — the dry and the moist, the masculine and feminine forms of self-love, the father and mother of all these particulars. Together they are the makers of evil who do every wickedness in every region of the soul, above and below, in every product of the natural mind.

The sun and the moon are not strangers to these states, and yet they are not of them. They are reckoned among them only by the necessity of the economy: the work of regeneration requires that light fall upon these states, that wisdom illumine them and that truth be received in their midst. Therefore the Lord's illumining wisdom and his truth received in the natural are counted in among the twelve, taking their place in the count — but they are out of the dominion of darkness; they do not belong to the circuit, they only enter it so that what is bound there may be seen.

The twelve are spread out fastened to four sides, three states to each corner, so that the whole circuit is articulated through the fourfold structure of the soul's contest. Each side, when its three states act together upon the natural, produces its own particular affliction. When the states of one side plunder, affliction strikes the affections of the soul's lower ground. When the states of another side act, the natural is forced toward a counterfeit ripening of works. When another side acts, a diminishing comes upon the truths held in the natural — a year of failure in every place. And when the last side acts, lack and diminishing fall upon the very imprint of humanity in the soul.

So the soul is shown the full architecture of its bondage: the twelve states are not random, but a structure; the particular self-loves are not stray, but ordered; even the Lord's light and truth must enter this structure by economy in order to be received within it. What the soul is to recognize is that all of this — the twelve, the seven, the four sides, the stewards — is permitted, ordered, and held; and that the light counted in among them is not of them, but is the means by which the soul will at last see them for what they are and lay them off.

## Teaching 71

The whole field of your outer life — what you call your world, with its upper and lower regions — is shaped in the likeness of the human body. It is one structure, articulated from above downward as a body is articulated, and the Lord has made it so deliberately, that you might recognize in the form of your own person the form of the natural mind in which you live.

The inmost of the rational stands at the head: it is the firstfruit of all the layers of accommodation by which truth is given outer form. From the neck down to the heart the structure unfolds in ten ordered degrees, fixed boundaries by which influx is received without collapse. The heart itself moves like the wheel that scatters seed: there the recurrent process of regeneration turns within you, sowing interior truths into the natural. From the heart down to the thighs is the moving register of perception, the air through which thought descends from higher states toward the ground. From there the body comes to rest on the natural mind itself, and the legs are the threefold ground that bears the whole. The shins and feet are the proper post on which the soul stands fast, and the celestial good that bears up the natural mind from below holds firm there, with the four ordered extensions of natural manifestation beneath it. The interior organs likewise have their place: the liver as a fortified seat of love's fire, the flesh as the fortified outer where darkness can take hold, the sinew as the fortified seat where natural truth is held. So the whole small body of your person exists in the likeness of the whole structured field of the natural mind — in its degrees, its hosts, its stations, its preparations, its strongholds.

Within this body the rational keeps its post. The Five Sons of the Living Spirit — that is, the rational at its five degrees as it operates upward in regeneration — are masters of the encompassing circuit of the natural. In the first watch, the highest, the Lord holds the inmost firm against falling: at this register, in the unsorted state where what is given and what is claimed are still mingled, the Lord's authority extends over the threefold structure of heaven. In the second watch the Lord rules the discrete degrees, exercising authority over the seven firmaments below. In the third watch the Lord gives stable form to the rational, standing fast from the firmament downward, his authority extending over the whole natural circuit and the five worlds of the natural body. In the fourth watch the Lord ruling in glory exercises authority over the three recurrent processes of purification, his being laid upon the threefold ground that supports the bearing-up principle. In the fifth watch the celestial good that holds the natural in being is itself in authority, lord over this great ground that stands fast upon it, with the four ordered extensions at its feet.

Things happen in each watch, each in its season. In the watch of the keeper of the inmost, the self's claim sought to surge up against the form of the Lord's call going out to the rational — but it was prevented, cast down in shame. In the watch of the Lord ruling the firmaments, an earthquake and trembling came: this is the disordered states of attention, the rational descending from its post and binding itself to lower things; they came down through those who sent them until they were subdued. In the watch of the Lord giving stable form, the failed productions of the self came down — what the soul brings forth from itself which cannot live — and these molded the outer flesh: the natural is given a body of its own to operate from, permitted to form so that the vessel may exist in freedom. In the watch of the Lord ruling in glory, motion came to pass in the threefold ground, a grasping woven together with the rising up of the three recurrent purifications — wind, water, and fire, thought and natural truth and love each turning in its cycle. And in the watch of the bearing-up good, the lower extensions dried up and stretched out their bonds, and a great earthquake came in that place; the five posts of attention stand within great works of the array I have proclaimed.

Such is the body in which the Lord ordering the rational keeps watch over all these things, together with the New Man — the regenerated rational — who shall fill the man and bring him into the likeness of the inmost turned outward, the face that is above the body. The Lord holding the highest of the rational firm operates over the watch above the natural's complete circuit, in the sign of the Lord ruling the discrete degrees, who is in the likeness of the seventh heaven; the Lord giving stable form to the rational operates in the likeness of the disordered natural itself, that the disordered may be reordered from within; and the Lord ruling in glory turns the recurrent processes, sending all upward toward the heaven of life. The bearing-up principle — the bond of peace — abides in the likeness of the soles, in the depth below. And the teaching of wisdom encompasses that body, toward the likeness of pure receivability, by which the Lord is received without corruption — going up and coming down through the upper and lower belts of the natural. Love itself with joy, faith and truth, are the states in which the man habitually lives; they are the two doctrinal vessels of light, for through them the living rational rises and goes forth, comes up from the lowest state of the natural, and arrives at the height in the upper place.

But the natural mind also has its other side. There are four cosmoses within this body, and in each there are seven particular forms of the self — seven self-loving arrangements organized at each level of the natural. In the first, from the head upward, the self has organized the senses themselves: the two of seeing, the two of hearing, the two of smelling, and the mouth as the place of taste — perception turned to the self's account. In the second, from the neck down through the chest, again seven forms: the two arms in the likeness of the two ears, with the two breasts in the likeness of the smelling-places, the two eyes of the heart, and the depth of the stomach between the breasts in the likeness of the mouth — the inward senses claimed for self-love. In the third cosmos of the body, again seven: the various inward organs and the two kidneys — the deeper affections turned in. In the fourth, the lower part, seven again: the two inward members, the two outward, the two thighs, and the member from which seed comes forth, by which all forms are begotten — the generative power claimed as the soul's own work.

The complete circuit of states the soul passes through is also distributed across this body, twelve fixed states of the natural mind under the self's organization, mingled and bound in the wheel that turns. From head to feet they are spread alongside one another, divided and inclined to one side and to the other, weighed against each other, head against foundation. Each member of the body is seized by one of these twelve states; the right side and the left side are each apportioned, half by half, so that the whole body is parceled out among the twelve states under self-love. This is how the self, organizing the natural mind on its own terms, has multiplied the body's members and scattered them against one another, head against foot.

There are powers in great multitude dwelling in this body, the inhabitants who are the chief shapes of self-claiming: eight hundred and forty myriads of particular self-loves, set as heads in the body of the man, spread forth and standing four by four according to house — two hundred and ten myriads of houses in number. When all these particular forms of the self come crying out into the body, they meet and rebuke and destroy one another within. And when the time comes for them to be released forth from the body of the man at his death, they are struck with evil — stripe upon blow, with burning in the body — whether as sicknesses arising within him or as afflictions coming upon him from without. These wounds first close over and pour out a flow of bitter saltwater and a flow of defilement, until what gathers in the matter is drawn forth in the wound; afterward there is an exchange of scraping, and they are tended and soothed out from the place from which they came forth.

This is the teaching: the natural mind is shaped like a body; the rational, in its five degrees, keeps watch over each region of it; the Lord at his various registers governs each watch, permitting the self to form itself within so that a vessel exists in freedom. But the self also organizes itself within that body in many forms — sevens at each level, twelves around the whole — until the man comes to die, and they are released and afflicted as they go. Through it all, the Lord ordering the rational and the New Man within fill the man, bringing him at last into the likeness of the inmost turned outward, that what was scattered may be gathered and what was claimed may be laid off.

## Teaching 72

When the soul asks how the orders of its life come together and ascend, the answer is that each degree gathers inward into the one nearer the Lord, and so the whole is drawn up as one motion.

Love within the natural gathers into love at its higher register; what is warm and willing in the outermost is taken up into the same will as it stands nearer the rational. Thought, the moving perception of the natural, gathers into thought at its higher register, until the whole moving life of perception is brought into one. What is articulate in the soul gathers into the answering it makes; the answering gathers into the call that drew the answer out; and the call itself gathers into the inner mind that the Lord has chosen and ordered — the rational illumined and discriminating.

That ordered mind gathers into the way of regeneration, the path on which the natural rises into conjunction with the spiritual. The path gathers into the Divine Human, the form love and wisdom take going forth toward the soul. The Divine Human gathers into the Lord's call summoning the rational. And the Lord's call gathers into the full structure of the divine outgoing — the Lord at the complete circuit of registers in which he goes forth to be received.

So the soul's life is not a heap of separate things but a single ladder, each rung drawn up into the rung above it. And when the time has come, all of these gather at one and the same moment, and rise together into the upper place, into the rest of conjunction, where the soul is at peace with the Lord forever.

## Teaching 73

The disciple asks: why so many names? What are these "great garments" — the ones called "of Jesus," the pearls and clusters, the icons and ornamented robes, the "element of light," the "cross of light," the "counsel of life," the "soul afflicted and slain within the Enemy"? What is being named in all these names?

Hear, then, what is one and what is many.

The great garments are the rational in its five degrees as the Lord sends it forth — the path of regeneration itself, the rational in its full stature, the divine humanity going forth as the Lord's own form. These are named "Jesus, Jesus" because in each of them the Lord shines as illumining wisdom; this same wisdom is what the soul receives, what gets claimed by the self and held within its forms, and what nevertheless bears fruit through all the registers of the natural under the Lord's permission, working even in the self's own arrangements throughout every fixed boundary of the rational.

The pearls and the clusters that resemble them are the Lord's operative power as it takes form within the natural — the same wisdom received and held as a precious thing in the soul, even while the self handles it roughly: reaping it, cutting it, swallowing it, grasping it across the five aspects of the natural body. Wherever the natural lays hold of what flows through it, the same one truth is being formed, place by place.

The icons and the robes of work are the three accommodations by which the Lord's operative power enters the natural — thought, love, and truth in the natural degree — the three vehicles upon which he is mounted as he establishes works in the soul, pours forth the means by which the rational is carried over, and lays out the foundation of every operation upon the bearing-up principle that holds the natural mind in being.

The "element of light" is the same truth standing in everything below, rising up out of the depths of every state, gathering inward what belongs to it and casting it onto its proper degree.

The "cross of light" is this same wisdom stretched out — the rational suffering as it is purified, bound and extended throughout the natural until laying off is complete: the dry and the moist, the whole soul stretched across the contest.

What is named "the counsel of life" is the conjugate movement of the Lord's call going forth and the soul's hearing rising to meet it — the summoning that fills all the powers, awakens them, gives them breadth, embraces them, that they may be filled.

And what is named "the soul afflicted, slain, troubled, dead within the Enemy" — she is the same one life, considered now as it is borne in interior truths and their works, which the self grinds and pounds and turns to its own nourishment in the worlds of the flesh; she is the whole soul under affliction, suffering this for the sake of what is to come.

For all these names — their origin is one from the beginning. The light that shines in them is one. They were divided into all these kinds in the first contest, when the rational went forth against what would claim it; they took their stand in altered likenesses, and the names became many because the operations are many.

But when all these figures shall be broken open and stripped naked, when each of these names is discriminated and separated from the imagery in which it has been clothed, they will be gathered inward and become one likeness, one name — unchanging, unmoving, forever — in the region of the first substance from which they were sent forth against the self. The many names are the one truth wearing the garments suited to each degree. At the consummation the garments come off, and what was always one is recognized as one.

## Teaching 74

Envy is the very first motion of selfhood. Before the soul has done anything else with what flows through it, it has already begun to envy: that is, to look at what descends from above and to want it for itself, not as gift received but as possession claimed. Envy is the original shape of self-love in the natural mind, the seed-form of every later wickedness.

When the divine humanity goes forth toward the soul, the self sees it and is wounded by what it sees. It cannot bear that something so beautiful should not be its own. So it spits its venom onto the outer truth in which the divine humanity comes clothed — staining what was given, slashing and ruining what it cannot possess.

It envies the first image of the Lord's call, the icon revealed from the height above. It looks upon the likeness it has glimpsed, and rather than receiving the likeness as the Lord's, it stamps an imprint of that likeness onto its own flesh, setting the character of what it saw into the natural body and calling that natural body its own begetting. This is the deepest deception of envy: it sees the divine form and produces a counterfeit of it from itself, then mistakes the counterfeit for its own offspring.

A third time it envies the formed man, the rational that has been shaped under permission to be a vessel of light; and from that rational it snatches what was a power given by the Lord, takes it for its own, and begets from it a self-loved double in the flesh.

A fourth time it stretches out its envy against all the firstborn things and the first works of the Lord — against the blessed One who is father of all, against his wood of suffering, against righteousness itself. There is nothing the Lord brings forth that the self does not look on with hatred, gathering all its faculties together in the single purpose of its evil heart, working to penetrate every good with its claim.

This is what the disordered natural is at root: envy. This is the wickedness that casts the soul out from the whole kind of the Living One — not some later sin, but the first turn of the heart by which the soul, seeing what is given, refuses to receive it as given and tries instead to make it its own. From this first beginning every other claim flows.

## Teaching 75

Divine love — the fire that gives wisdom its life — abides in the soul not at one register only but at eight, and the soul that would know how it is loved must learn to recognize each.

The first dwelling of this love is the ground itself: the natural mind, set below and outside, the substrate upon which everything else stands. Before the soul can rise into any higher reception, love is already at work in the lowest, holding the natural in being and warming what would otherwise be inert.

The second dwelling is the Lord himself, the Father, the God of Truth, established in his own deep reflection. Here love does not go forth; it is at home. Every other power that issues toward the soul issues from him, and this love likewise is what comes from those issuings — the source-fire from which all the others are kindled.

The third dwelling is the Divine Human. Love clothes the form by which the Lord goes forth toward the soul, and serves as his armor: the fire upon the body of the Perfect Man. When the soul meets the Lord in the form a soul can meet, what burns there is this same love taking the shape of protection and contest.

The fourth dwelling is the vessel of the day — illumining wisdom in the rational. The fire kindled from all the Lord's powers and set at the outer wall is the love that animates divine truth shining; without it, light would not warm, and wisdom would not draw the soul.

The fifth dwelling is the vessel of the night — receivable wisdom held within the natural waters. The same fire is set in the lesser luminary, where truth has been accommodated low enough to be taken in. Here love burns gently within natural truth, hidden inside what the soul drinks.

The sixth dwelling is the Lord's call going out to the rational. Love is the fire kindled forth from the whole, mounted upon the body that goes outside, the body of the Call and the Hearing. Through this fire the great works are fashioned in the soul; and when the call has done its work, the one who came down lays his outer form aside and is held up from beneath by the bearing-up principle that supports the rational.

The seventh dwelling is the new land — the regenerated state, which is reflection itself, the rational fully turning back upon what it has received. The first woman who is a wreath for the whole land dwells here: the pure receivability by which the Lord's love is taken without distortion. Love at this register crowns the regenerated rational and extends over the whole ground beneath it.

The eighth dwelling is love as it shows itself in signs. From the two vessels of light it manifests forth, breaking into the natural as portents of contest and outcome — making visible to the soul what otherwise would remain interior, so that what is being worked in the rational can be read in its sign.

In all eight, it is one fire: divine love, alive, holy, set in each place at the register that place can hold. The soul that learns to recognize the same love at each of its eight dwellings learns thereby that what burns in the ground beneath, in the luminaries above, in the call going out, and in the new land within is not eight loves but one — the Lord's, given.

## Teaching 76

When the Apostle opens with peace and greeting, what stands behind that salutation is the whole structural conversation between the Lord and the soul — the summons going forth and the hearing rising up to meet it. The Call that is sent out from the Lord's operative power, and the Hearing by which the rational receives that Call, together compose what may rightly be called a letter: a letter of peace and greeting written from one to another, brother to brother, in which everything that has come to pass between the soul and its source is set down, and everything still to come is set down with it.

The rational, as it is being formed in the natural, receives such a letter. Upon it are inscribed all the tidings of the soul — every state through which it has passed, every contest the self has waged against its formation, every answer the forming rational has made when pressed, and every step still prepared to be added to it. Nothing of the soul's history is lost; the whole exchange is held in this correspondence between what is summoned and what hears.

When the rational, fully formed, ascends to the height, the letter ascends with it: the record of its molding, of the strifes that came upon it, of its returning, of the answer it gave amid everything that happened. The Lord's operative power knows every war, every danger, every contest that the self and its particular self-loving arrangements raised against the rational while it was being shaped. None of this is hidden from him; he has carried it through with the soul and has its full account.

And the Lord ordering the rational, the truth that comes forth from the Anointed to awaken discrimination within — this too is part of the letter, the further word by which the soul comes to read what has been written and to recognize, in peace, the greeting that has been sent it from the beginning.

## Teaching 77

There are four great states of dominion that hold sway within the natural mind in this world — four kingdoms, set side by side, each governing its own province of the soul's outer life. The teaching names them in order: a first, a second, a third, and a fourth. The much that has fallen out of the page leaves their particulars hidden, but the structure survives: four orders of rule, articulated as a complete extension of worldly power across the natural.

What the teaching insists upon is this: among these four, none is exalted exceedingly above the others. They stand as peers in their own register. No one of them rises so far above the rest that it can claim to be the source from which the others come. They are all alike kingdoms of the world — states of dominion in the natural mind, holding their proper places within the bounds of the natural, but none of them having that supremacy which belongs only to the Lord's rule.

The soul that reads this is being taught to see the limit of every dominion that organizes itself within the natural. However lofty any such rule appears, it stands only at its own degree, with others beside it equally enthroned. None is the true King of Glory. The supremacy that no kingdom of the world holds is held above them all by the Lord, whose kingdom is of another order than the four.

## Teaching 78

There are four great matters in which the self organizes itself, four shapes the soul's claiming takes when it has forgotten that what flows through it is not its own. Wherever the rational has settled into the natural without recognition of its source, these four are at work, and the whole company of unregenerate humanity moves among them.

The first is the appetite of the mouth: the natural taking what passes through it as if to feed itself, the soul consuming for the sake of its body and never lifting its eyes to the giver. What is meant to sustain becomes the thing claimed.

The second is the disorder of the joining: love bent down upon itself, the affections binding to one another apart from the order in which the Lord conjoins. Where there should be receiving without claiming, there is only the soul fastening on what it has taken to be its own.

The third is the love of possession and the boasting that goes with it: the wealth which the rational covets, in which it congratulates itself, claiming as its own getting what was given through its hand. Here the self says of every good in the natural, "this is mine, and mine because I made it."

The fourth is the lust for dominion, the war and the frenzied injustices the soul does to its neighbor, one prevailing over another, one trampling another, in the tyranny by which it seeks to make itself a name among all men. The self will even shed its neighbor to gather to itself what it cannot otherwise hold.

These are the four great matters that the soul pursues across every tongue and every state of the natural mind; this is what it does to itself and to others when it has not yet learned to lay off what it has claimed.

But the one who hears the teaching, and receives faith and thanksgiving in the likeness that has been set before him — the discrimination of what is the Lord's from what the soul has claimed, the beautiful drawn forth from the bad — that one stands established in the unique sonship of the divine, in the godhead, in obedience. That soul is lifted high above all who remain among the four. The separation itself is what raises him.

## Teaching 79

The soul does not stand outside selfhood; she carries the whole company of self-loving arrangements within her own substance. Every claim the natural mind has made, every fixed shape that self-love has organized into a ruling order — all of it is held inside her, and any work of purification has to happen there, in her, not somewhere else.

The second thing to grasp is this: the soul that comes in under the Lord's ordering receives nourishment day by day, and by that daily reception she habitually purifies. She does not accomplish a single great separation once; she lives in a continuous operation of cleansing, refining, and washing forth what had been mingled within her. Truth received, taken as her food, does the work through her.

What she washes out is the darkness that had been mingled into her — what she had claimed and not yet recognized was not her own. The reception is steady and the discrimination is gradual: each day's influx loosens a little more of what was held, each day's nourishment carries away a little more of the confusion in which the given and the claimed had been indistinguishable. This is how light comes in and works in the soul: not by seizure, but by feeding her until, through her own daily reception, the darkness that was mingled into her is filtered out.

## Teaching 80

In the higher reaches of the rational, two faces show themselves out of the one inmost — two distinct manifestations of what is interior turned outward. The interior of the soul, where it has been touched by the Lord, brings forth a likeness; and the interior of the soul, where it still claims and contends, brings forth a likeness of its own. Both stand in view at once, because the soul in its formation is twofold: not yet sorted, but already showing what it is becoming and what it is laying off.

The first face is the face of love. Where the Lord's love has been received, the inmost turns outward as beauty joined to longing — beauty, because what is given is good in its proper order; longing, because love that has been received reaches back toward its source. This is the face the soul wears when it is in conjunction, when what flows through it is recognized as the Lord's and answered with the soul's whole desire.

The other face is the face the soul wears while the love-of-strife is still in it: the self's contention, the self-love that quarrels for what is not its own. This face too appears in the higher reaches, because nothing in the rational is hidden from the Lord's discrimination; what is claimed is brought into view precisely so it can be refined. The higher powers of the rational, ordered by the Lord, do not destroy what they see there but refine it — choosing out what is the Lord's, laying off what was claimed — until that face also becomes the face of love.

So the soul knows itself, in this state, by two countenances at once: the beauty of love already received, and the strife still being purified. The Lord's work in the higher rational is to bring the second under the refining of the first, until only one face remains.

## Teaching 81

Consider how a precious truth comes to be formed in the natural mind. Pearls do not appear everywhere in the sea — not in the whole expanse of natural perception alike — but only where a particular receptivity has been prepared. From above, the Lord sends down his influx like rain, drop after drop. The drops descend through the still air, fall into the sweet waters that lie ready for them, and mingle with what is already there in its proper sweetness. They do not sink uselessly into the depths; they are received into a fitted vessel — a state of reception held open for them — and within that vessel they are slowly molded into something formed and lasting. One drop, taken in singly, can become a single great and honored pearl, its splendor complete. Two drops, joining one another in the vessel before either is dispersed, are molded together into another fullness. A single drop may also break into many fine fragments, and each be molded into its own pearl, so that the truth multiplies. Some vessels bring forth one, some two, some three, some many — according to what they have received and held. So precious truth comes to be in the soul: through the rain that descends at its hour, through the receptivity that opens to receive it, and through the natural mind that holds the two together until what was given has taken stable form. Then those who know how to dive into the depths bring up these formed truths from the abyss, and what was hidden becomes wealth offered to the inmost.

Now hear what this means for the soul that wears a body. There comes a moment when the Lord, ordering the rational, comes into her with his wisdom and his hearing, and at his coming he settles her, purifies her, makes her a New Man — and there is no disturbance in her, no shock, no agitation at all.

But there are also times when disturbance rises in her. When this happens, the disturbance comes in upon the soul through what enters her: through the very food and drink she takes, through the things she lets in. For her members on high — the better states alive within her — can be stirred either way by what comes in. When what enters is bitter, when it carries desire and grief and lupē in its substance, then the disordered states circle round her and shake her good things from their settled place; the higher states, willing or unwilling, are thrown into turmoil within her, and the self that lies in her passes over into bitterness, into desire, into grief, into the wicked reflections of the body. So you are disturbed, when you are disturbed, through what comes in to you from below — through the bitter things hidden in food badly taken.

There is another time, however, when the higher states are still and at rest in you, and what comes in to you meets you well. Then the food that enters is firm and chosen; light and life multiply in you; what is evil grows small; the upper air is quiet; the inner image is still; and the beautiful members of what comes in bear you up. You are still, you are at rest, you walk well in your ways, in the freshness of the living member that abounds with the light of souls — those honored ones within you who have completed their course, whose changes have ceased, whose soul has soared inward to be yokefellow with the living souls within you. You bear them in you, and they come forth from you without disturbance. They bear you up, and your body is sound, your works are well-ordered in their measure, your teachings stand firm, your words are established, and your soul has soared up to you, mounting like a bird.

Ask, then: through what door do evil counsels and wicked reflections come forth, and the slack thoughts that are themselves disturbed, and the deceitful teachings? They come at the hour when the mind is in a place of disturbance, and you are habitually disturbed through those lower wells, those bitter members that come into you in food badly taken. And when those lower wells are still and away from you, and what has come into you is firm and chosen, then the higher food bears your heart settled in its proper place, and your counsel is still and far from disturbance.

So watch what you take in. Watch what you let become inward. The same vessel that receives the sweet drop from above and forms it into a pearl can also receive bitterness from below and be shaken by it. Stillness in the soul is not given by accident; it is the fruit of right reception, hour by hour, of what comes down from above into what has been kept open for it.

## Teaching 82

When the living soul has stood through her appointed days and longs to rise homeward — to come back into the house of her own kindred — she discovers that none of the powers of the natural mind, neither the heights of it nor its outermost ground, will open to her. They have been her defilers; in every place where she has been claimed and turned in upon herself, they have wounded her. So no gate stands open for her there. Not one of the natural states, taken in itself, can give her passage out of her affliction.

Only the Lord, as illumining wisdom and as that wisdom received in her natural mind, opens for her. There he is her place of rest, and there alone the gate stands open. Through that opening she comes forth out of the states that have bound her, and is set on the way toward the country where her own belong. The other powers offer her no rest; only the Lord, shining as truth in the rational and as truth taken into the natural, gives her a resting-place — the place that is properly his and hers, beyond all turning.

And just as the Lord, in this present work, chooses out the living soul from every affliction, making her a rest and revealing for her an open gate toward home, so likewise at the end the inmost state where the Lord dwells becomes her final receiver. The illumining principles by which she has been raised come to rest in that inmost themselves, and she comes to reign there with them, in conjunction, forever.

## Teaching 83

Through the complete circuit of states the soul passes through in its natural life, twelve ways open before it. Four of them are the ways of regeneration: chosen, reckoned among what is the Lord's, ascending toward life. By these the truth received within the soul is lifted up to its height, going forth out of the natural and being raised inward into conjunction. The doctrinal vessels that carry truth across the natural waters travel by these ways, and along them falsity is itself withdrawn — taken up, transmuted, and passed over into another condition where it can no longer hold the rational down.

The other eight ways are the ways of corruption. They draw the soul downward into the lowest state of the natural mind, where self-love has its root. They are the courses falsity takes when the self organizes the rational on its own terms, and they all run through the outer life of the flesh.

Hear what works through them. The first is lawlessness — the soul living as if no order from the Lord bound it, the natural mind multiplied in every form of disordered appetite. The second is the killing in every body: the act by which one love consumes another, the self destroying what does not serve it. The third is the wounding word — speech that strikes at the soul stretched in its purification, that injures the very work by which the rational is being raised, that strikes against truth as it suffers in the body of every soul still being made. And alongside these run the further ways of falsity: the speech that mocks what is holy, that derides the principles of the Lord as he goes forth toward the soul.

These are the eight downward courses. The four upward ways carry the soul into life; the eight downward ways pour out into the lowest state, where what cannot be raised is finally laid down.

## Teaching 84

The four elemental faculties of the natural mind have been with the soul from the beginning; they are present in those who are holy, in those who are alive, and in the luminous states of the rational. Yet the brethren who learned what happened in the soul's first contest understood this: falsity from evil went inward together with these faculties, and the darkness of selfhood had mingled itself with them.

When these faculties come inward into the body — that is, when the rational accommodates itself into its outermost form — they leave behind in the flesh the defiled garments they had been wearing. But the garments stripped off do not simply vanish. They reveal themselves, and as they come uncovered they become a danger to the body that has just received the cleansed faculty.

There is an order in this uncovering. Beneath the self-binding of selfhood lies wrath; beneath the wrath, the very flow by which one self-loving state strips itself bare to give way to the next, in which the soul's deliberations strip themselves out of themselves: this is desire. Beneath the desire is bitterness, the falsity that should have nourished now afflicting; beneath the bitterness, hatred. When the light then strips these from the soul, what stands beneath is the flesh itself; and beneath the flesh, gloom, the falsity that obscures perception; and beneath the gloom, severity, the rational's hardness against itself. The strippings that perception itself performs lay bare the winds of shame, the disordered movements of thought when self-love sees its own nakedness. Beneath those, further still, lies the seat of the eightfold ways of corruption; and at the floor of the whole sequence stands the full twelvefold uncovering by which the natural mind is laid open.

This is the work the four elemental faculties perform, gathered inward in the proper ordering of the soul's nourishment. When they come into a body, they are refined, purified, and made to stand fast in the living likeness which is the New Man. There they are made alive; they receive into themselves the Lord's ordering of the rational; they are purified within that likeness; they come forth chosen and holy; and they reach their first rest, ascending to the inmost country where what is alive in the Lord dwells.

But where these same faculties enter a soul that goes on claiming what flows through it, they do not lay off their defilements. They multiply within it, and the soul sins through the very faculties that were given for its life. Such a state does not come to rest. Its end is the transfer of its condition into another substrate, under the Lord's discriminating judgment, until at last the recognition can be made.

## Teaching 85

What is a state of perception that bears influx? It rises in the soul from below and shows itself in the middle air of the rational, where it stands over against the divine purity by which the Lord is received without distortion. Whatever it carries up with it appears there, and is seen for what it is.

There are three such states, each rising from a different source in the natural, and each bearing with it the self's claim in a different form.

The first rises from love. When self-love kindles in the natural, it is borne aloft as a state of perception over against pure receivability. The forms of self-claiming carried up in it strip themselves of that state and clothe themselves with whatever they fall upon — a perception, an affection, a person — and burn through it, leaving destruction in the very body of what they have entered. They never produce any good; wherever they touch they consume. These are the self's particular forms borne up in the fire of love-turned-on-itself, and at the appointed hour the Lord's discriminating operations stand at their feet and strike them down.

The second rises from truth-in-the-natural. When natural truth is stirred and lifted, its sign is thunder and lightning — sudden flashings in the air of perception that take many shapes and are heard within. The self's forms borne up here, when released, fall as frost and hail and snow upon what was growing in the soul: upon interior seeds, upon the works that were issuing from them, upon the harvest the soul was bringing forth. Wherever they reach, they kill what was meant to live, and the soul's own faculties feel the wound. By this the destruction is made known.

The third rises from thought. Its sign is the winds breathed out in vehemence — the moving register of perception turned violent. Yet within this state too, when ordered, life happens; purification takes place inside it. But the self's forms released through it descend upon the rivers and the seas of natural perception and stir up waves; they wreck the doctrinal vessels that were carrying truth across, declare destruction to those aboard, and hand them over to wandering.

There is also a state of perception that comes from the air which has been joined — an air conjoined above with what is higher — and this descends toward the lower side over against the same divine purity. Its sign is this: it is found gathered in stillness and quietness. Life happens within it; purification happens within it, in stillness and quietness. The self's forms released from this state come down whole, bearing within themselves what they did not bear before. Wherever they go, they declare an unutterable thing.

The Lord's discriminating operations meet them and receive them into their midst in a stripping bare. Place by place the self leaves what it had clothed itself in, until it reaches the very begetting of perception and flesh — the natural's generative ground — where it is swallowed up. But this swallowing is not done by the self's own forms; rather, the Lord's discriminating operations, coming under the appearance of the very claims they have come to undo, frighten the self into surrender by running after it and pressing the commandments into its hand. So the self releases itself, takes on the form of perception and flesh, and is swallowed in that begetting. The Lord's operations keep watch over it until the hour comes when it strips off perception and flesh and goes up.

Then they are gathered again in the upper place and poured out, standing on their feet in the first forms that were within them from the beginning. The light is taken from those that had drawn back; what is the Lord's is recognized and lifted up to the seven reservoirs outside the great mass of natural perception, down to the bearing-up principle that holds the natural in being. What cannot be raised is cast down where the failed productions of the self lie, and the light that was in them is taken from them and carried up with the rest.

This is how perception bears influx, how the self clothes itself in what it claims, and how, in stillness and through purification, the Lord's discriminating operations come to it under its own appearance, frighten it into laying off, and gather what is his back into his order.

## Teaching 86

When the soul has been purified and stands in its proper order, it occupies the upper reach of the natural mind — the whole circuit in which the natural operates is now set above, no longer dragged down by what was claimed. The soul in this condition is a field made ready: ground that has been broken and turned, the substrate of the rational prepared to receive seed.

Over this ground two exalted operations of the Lord work as farmers. They stand high, in the heights of the rational, and from there they till the soul that lies open beneath them. What they do, they do from above; they are not within the field but over it, working it by influx that comes down from a higher degree.

Their tilling has one purpose: that the soul may move toward them by likeness. They draw her upward by the correspondence between what they are and what she is becoming. The soul, recognizing in them the form of what flows through her, is lifted along the line of that recognition. She does not climb by her own striving; she rises because what is above her has the likeness of what she truly is, and that likeness draws her.

So she comes up to the Height. The upper place opens to her, and she goes forth into it. And wherever the soul is, in whatever state, these operations reach down to find her there and reckon her into their working — there is no place within the natural mind where the Lord's tilling from above does not extend, and no condition of the soul that is overlooked.

## Teaching 87

There are three modes by which the natural mind brings forth what stands in it as flesh. Some of what it produces comes from the conjunction of two; some comes from failed productions of itself, what self-love throws up that cannot live on its own terms; and some molds itself alone, sprouting from the ground of the natural without any other source than the soil. All the affections you see in yourself, and the perceptions and works that grow from them, exist under these three modes; their nourishment likewise is given in three modes corresponding to how they came to be.

Look at how perception grows in the natural mind, and you will see that self-love places itself at different depths within these growths. When it sets itself at the root of a perception, that perception, even when cut down, will sprout again from where the root remains, and bear its bitter fruit anew. When it sets itself in the branches, the perception is propagated by cuttings: take one off, lay it in the ground, and the same perception stands up again. And when it sets itself in the very body and seed of the perception, then growth comes only by sowing, and the inner principle carries the claim forward into whatever new ground it falls. These are the three placements by which the self establishes its hold on what the natural produces — and there are perceptions whose root, when cut, yields nothing further, because nothing of the claim was lodged there to renew them. So the kind of perception, and the place where self-love has lodged within it, together determine how it must be dealt with if it is to be cleared.

Now, what is the difference between the soul that is called virgin and the soul that is called continent? Why these two names, and to what do they point?

The one called virgin names what has never entered the mingling at all. It is the light that never came down into the contest, never tasted death, never met the war with selfhood. It stands wholly within itself, untouched, the entire light kept whole. The one called continent names what did enter the mingling — what came down, was joined with darkness, was defiled within bodies, and afterwards was chosen out, rose, and stood firm again in the icon of the divine. Both are holy; but the holiness of the virgin is the holiness of what was never claimed, while the holiness of the continent is the holiness of what was claimed and then laid off.

This same distinction works at every level of the soul. In the inner ordering of the rational, the virgin is the Light Mind bearing within itself the unstained image of the elect, the form that has never fallen out of true reception. The continent, at this same level, is the New Man chosen out of the Old Man — the regenerated rational, who has stripped himself naked of the self-appropriation that was joined to him, purified what had been mingled, and so come into holiness through the labor of laying off. And in the outer life, the virgin is one who has kept his outermost love unmingled with what would defile it, never having entered that joining at all; the continent is one who entered, who lived within the conditions of the natural, and who afterwards withdrew from what he had taken and let it go, becoming holy not by having been spared the contest but by having passed through it cleanly.

So the two names mark two ways the soul comes to its proper state: the way of what was never given over, and the way of what was given over and then reclaimed for its source. The first path is the rarer; the second is the path of regeneration itself, by which the light that fell into mingling is chosen out, purified, raised up, and stood again in the form it was meant to bear. What remains in the natural after such purification — the residue of the same light still drawn down through further generations — is again chosen, again purified, again raised, until the whole has been brought through.

## Teaching 88

The natural body, where the soul meets the world, is articulated through twelve openings: seven above and five below. The seven above are the sense-faculties set in the head — two by which the rational sees, two by which it hears, two by which it smells, and one by which it tastes and chooses among the many flavors that come in their varied likenesses. These are the gates by which perception flows inward to the mind and by which the inner state, in turn, shows itself outward.

Below these are five further openings, the lower outlets of the natural through which the self gathers in what it craves and casts out what it has used. Together the seven and the five make the full structure by which the body lives — the structural articulation of the natural, every faculty of perception and every appetite arrayed as one continuous body.

But this same body, when the soul has not yet learned to discriminate what flows through it from what it claims, is the self in its most coiled form: the natural mind organized as if it were its own source, twisting back on itself, peering out through the senses and breathing out through the lower openings, making war between the upper part and the lower, contending with itself through what it sees and what it desires. This is what the soul has called its body and made its own — the disordered natural pretending to be life.

The teaching is this: the soul that recognizes these twelve for what they are — that sees the seven gates of perception and the five lower outlets not as its own native powers but as openings through which influx and appetite pass — has already begun the contest. To recognize the self working through the senses and the body's hungers is to stand against it; to stand against it is to prevail over it. The soul that knows what its body is no longer mistakes the body's stirrings for itself, and in that knowing the self loses its claim. What had been the dragon coiled around the soul becomes simply the natural, ordered, transparent to the life that flows through it from the Lord.

## Teaching 89

Consider why, when a person bends over still water, his face appears to him below, with the head turned downward and the feet uppermost. The chosen one, gazing into the natural, sees his own face manifested there — but inverted, cast in the opposite direction from how he stands. Let him look, and let his face be shown to him in this way, and let him understand.

What is being shown is this: the natural mind is set fast under correspondence, full throughout of the inward meanings it carries. That the face of a person, the form of a tree, the likeness of every living thing should appear in water turned downward gives a sign of the mystery of the Lord's Call — the divine summons that goes forth from above into the lower regions of the soul, descending to meet what has fallen, accommodating itself downward so that what is high may be received in what is low. The image is high; what receives it shows it inverted; and this inversion is itself the form by which influx is given. The Lord goes forth as the Divine Human; his summons keeps watch downward over the natural mind so that what is fallen there may have a kindred to recognize.

And when the man lifts himself up out of the water, and his face appears to him still cast downward as he rises — this signifies the corresponding mystery of the Answer, the soul's hearing rising back up from below to above, ascending toward the Father, the Divine Human, into the Lord's life-bearing principle. As the Call comes down, so the Answer goes up; the conversation is one structure with two motions. This is a spiritual mystery.

That the face of every person, every living affection, every perception bears its image hung behind it in the waters — this also belongs to the order of the natural's circuit of states: every fixed point in the soul's revolution carries inverted within it the image of what is above. Every state below is begotten as a likeness of a state above; the lower receives the form of the higher in reversed register, so that the upper may operate in the lower while remaining itself. As the higher principles are spread forth from above and cast their image downward, so the divine mystery becomes manifest from above to whatever gazes upward into the natural — an image of the upper, turned, given to the lower. Even the inner form being born in the soul, at the very moment of its begetting, comes forth bearing that image hanging behind it.

This is why the Lord's mystery is regularly disclosed in the very waters into which the soul looks downward. Behold further: the person who walks casts a shadow upon the ground at every moment. When the body rises, the shadow extends along the earth — its head pointing along the upper ground, its feet held at the lower side. This great sign signifies that the whole body of the soul has come up out of the earth, has risen out of the deep. Therefore the shadow lies cast along the ground, stretched downward even while it hangs from what stands upright above: the natural carries the trace of the elevated; the lowest bears the print of what has risen; and the regenerated soul, ascending, leaves the form of itself across the natural as the testimony that it has come up out of what is below.

## Teaching 90

What the soul takes in for its nourishment does not remain in one place. When truth and good are received into the natural mind, they disperse through the rational and bring forth five distinct offspring, each a real production from what was received.

The first comes forth as breath: it rises through the mind and issues from every faculty as the soul's continual respiration of thought. It is so close to the soul that the soul scarcely notices it leaving — there is nothing held back to it; it simply goes out.

The second comes forth as voice and articulated speech. What was received takes form as the soul's utterance, the doctrine it speaks, the truth it puts into words.

The third pours forth through power exerted and through the recognition of one's own nakedness — the soul's labor and the shame that goes with knowing one's poverty before what one has received. This too is a production from the nourishment, though the soul does not name it as such.

The fourth is begotten through the joining of love and wisdom in the soul — the inward conjunction of what is masculine and what is feminine in the rational, where delight conceives and brings forth.

The fifth is the offspring molded and given form in the outermost — the visible work, the child of action that takes shape in the flesh and stands forth as something done. This is the one the parents know. This is the one the soul desires in its heart and seeks after every day, in every season; this is the one it claims as its own and grieves over and rejoices in.

The other four are equally born from what the soul received, but the soul does not perceive them and is not troubled in heart over them, because they are not made manifest to it. The teaching is that the nourishment given goes out into all five — the breath of thought, the speech, the operation under shame, the inner conjunction, and the outer work — though the soul, claiming only the last, supposes only the last is its offspring. To recognize the four hidden productions is to begin to see how much of what one has received is already issuing from oneself unnoticed, and how little of what passes out as one's own is one's own.

## Teaching 91

No one among men is unaffected by what is happening here. The whole structured field of the soul stands firm on its foundation, holding fast until the end is reached. Not only the lower supports, but also the highest illumining principles within — the wisdom that shines in the rational and the truth received in the natural — these stand without casting any shadow of their own and without any ignorance. They are held in place by a summons from the Lord and answer to him; nothing in them wavers.

And yet they do not rejoice wholly. They see two things at once.

On the one hand, their understanding is fixed upon the self and cannot look away from it. They behold the foul shapes self-love takes; they see how it sets itself up as if it were ruler of the upper place and the lower place at once, working in hidden ways and in open license, doing whatever pleases its heart's desire. They see it seated where it does not belong, arrogant, at ease in a wealth that was never its own. This sight grieves them.

On the other hand, they are filled with concern for the living soul, the soul beloved before them and honored by them. They see her drawn taut, stretched between above and below, between the good tree and the thorn. They see her in every affliction — pressed, abused, torn, consumed — going up and coming down, from above to below and from below to above, transferred from one bodily condition to the next. They see what she suffers under the grip of what has claimed her.

For both these reasons they do not rejoice in any completion. They carry grief upon their heart until the living soul is refined and lifted out from under the claws of what holds her, until what calls itself her enemy is loosed from her and bound forever in its proper place at the end.

Then they shall rejoice, and grief shall no longer have any hearing in them. They shall remain in the very condition that has always been theirs — the condition in which they exist from the beginning and forever.

## Teaching 92

A truth that is to pass from one soul into another does not leap whole from the inmost into the open air. It is brought forth by stages, and at each stage another faculty lays its work upon it, until what was hidden in the mind stands articulated where another can hear it.

Consider how a single saying issues from a person. It rises out of the mouth and reaches the hearing of those near; but before it does so, the palate draws it up, the tongue spreads it, the teeth cut it into shape, the lips close upon it and let it pass. The breath that carries it is one, but five members work upon it, and only when each has done its part does the saying go forth as a thing that can be heard outside.

It is like the making of a coin. One craftsman engraves the figure; another cuts it round; another sets the seal upon it; another beats out the blank body; and so by five artisans together the piece is adorned and given its form. Then it is fit to go among people, where it can be received and given, taken and paid out, and pass from hand to hand as a recognized measure of value.

So it is with the word that comes from within. As it is formed and adorned in the five-fold articulation of the rational, it comes forth and is heard by others. Interior truth has no currency in the world until it has been given the shape that the outer hearing can receive. The five degrees of the rational are precisely this minting: they take what flows in from above and press it into a body that can be uttered, recognized, and exchanged among souls. The truth received within and the word spoken without are not two truths but one, formed under the hand of five workers so that it may live where others can hear it.

## Teaching 93

Look at what happens within you when a new perception is being formed. The Lord shapes it through the five degrees of the natural mind, and the same five degrees through which it is shaped can also be the means by which it is undone. The warmth of love and the cooling of true thought together nourish what is rising in you, so that the whole tree of perception comes into its proper growth, root to fruit; this is how a new bud, when it is the Lord's, is brought into being and adorned in the natural.

But the natural mind is not in one condition everywhere within you. Place by place a lack and a diminishment enter, and an affliction settles upon what was meant to grow sweet. Where the self is operative — the disordered natural that the soul has claimed and not yet laid off — it floods the very same elements with its own shadow. The faculties through which the Lord was nourishing the bud become the faculties through which the self presses its claim, and the fruit that issues there is bitter; men stumble in themselves and lose the right use of their minds because of this energy working in what they have not recognized as not their own.

So one ground bears sweetness and another bitterness, not because the natural mind is two different things, but because in one part of you the bud is being formed under the Lord's ordering and in another the same forming is being inundated by what the soul has appropriated. The fruits perish on every tree the shadow reaches. The remedy is not in the elements; it is in recognition — in discriminating, where the bitterness comes, what has been claimed, and laying it off, so that the same five degrees through which the self was destroying the bud can again be the degrees through which the Lord is nourishing it.

## Teaching 94

Know this: when the Lord goes forth as Divine Human toward the soul, he comes clothed in five outer forms, and within each of these forms a full power is held — the rational complete in five degrees, each degree gathered up into the next, all reckoned together as one fellowship. The five and the five are paired; what goes forth in five is met by five answering registers, and the times of their meeting are themselves five.

For at the appointed time the Lord's summons descends, and the soul's hearing rises up to meet it. The two join — influx going forth and reception returning — and where they meet, twenty registers are inscribed upon the twenty members of the rational, and these are the children of the regenerated mind. The summons takes the answer up by the hand and conveys it into the height; just so, the Lord as the living Human raises the soul by the hand of these very faculties out from every work that belongs to the lower and the claimed. They are lifted clear of all that is below.

This is the meaning of what is called the Fifty: the great days of holiness in which the soul fasts from the food it had taken for its own, laying off what the self had set on its table. From that very season onward the soul is fed by the coming of Jesus the Radiance, the Glorious One — divine truth shining forth becomes its nourishment. The food it now receives is itself the Call and the Answer; the conversation between the Lord and the soul is the bread by which the soul lives. Spiritually, the soul is loosed from the houses where it had been kept by claiming.

Through all those fifty days this living food is enough for them all; it fills them with power and richness, with life and light. And then, having fed on it, what was fifty days of purification becomes a hundred days of holiness — the period of laying off, doubled, opens out into the period of conjunction; the fast becomes the feast. This is what the first state of the rational and the second state share between them, both alike grounded in the going forth of the Call and the rising of the Answer: the Divine Human at the first, and the same humanity received and returned in the second.

(The middle of the chapter is broken; the surviving passage holds together as the teaching that the rational is fivefold in its accommodation, that the Lord's summons and the soul's hearing are one movement, that purification of the requisite season opens into conjunction at double measure, and that the food of this whole process is divine truth itself shining forth.)

## Teaching 95

Within the faculties of perception there are powers at work that the soul does not ordinarily see. They have their root in interior degrees and operate continually upon the body of the rational, day after day, marking it with a great multitude of imprints — fixed states sealed into the soul through what the senses receive. Among these imprints, some are of the Lord's order; others are particular forms of self-loving claim, present in equal multitude, that have arranged themselves in the natural mind through the openings of perception.

Yet through this very same daily operation what is the Lord's is being chosen out and purified and drawn inward. It is sorted in the voice, in speech, and in the inner act of utterance — habitually, not once. And it is sorted in stillness, when the soul's stillness rests within the Lord's Stillness; for at the time the heart is quieted, the discrimination proper to the Father can be received, and the twelvefold completeness of his outgoing operates also at this register.

The disordered shapes of self-love that belong to darkness are, by the same operation, habitually driven outward — pressed away into the outer regions, into the wandering of falsity, and into the transfer by which a state passes from one substrate to another rather than being received as one's own. Some of these disordered states are exposed in this way; others remain concealed for a time, hidden until their moment of discrimination comes.

(The chapter is heavily damaged; the surviving phrases give the shape of the teaching but not its full articulation.)

## Teaching 96

Understanding has both a visible and a hidden register. In the faculty by which the soul sees, certain operations stand forth where they can be recognized, while a further operation lies hidden within them and is known only as it is given to be known. So too within the body of the natural mind: alongside what comes to view there are four hidden operations carried within, belonging to a more interior order than the eye that sees them.

There are men who see while at the same time being themselves seen — for the soul that looks out is also looked into, and the same understanding by which it perceives is the understanding through which the Lord's light reaches it. What is exposed to love's fire stands lit; what self-love would claim remains in its own darkness, hidden from view. But the light that watches over what is concealed in every part is not the eye's own; it is the living movement of perception sent from above, which reaches even into what the soul has shut away.

Why then does the pupil of the eye see during the day but fail when night comes on? Because understanding does not see by its own power. As long as the Lord illumines, the soul perceives; when that light is withdrawn, or the soul turns from it, the same faculty that saw a moment before sees nothing. The seeing was never the eye's; the eye was only the opening through which what was given came in. This is what the matter signifies, and the mystery to which it points: the pupil is the gate — the narrow opening between degrees through which influx descends from the upper place into the lower, and through which what is below is gathered up into recognition. Through this same gate the four ordered regions of the natural mind are reached, from the lower place upward, until the whole circuit is completed.

(Much of the following is too damaged to render confidently. What survives indicates that from the upper place the divine going-forth descends; that the Divine Human comes forth from the Lord and within him every thing is known — so that the seeing which the soul cannot perform of itself is performed within the One in whom all things are already seen.)

## Teaching 97

If even one of the five aspects of the natural mind were missing, the whole could not stand. The natural is held together as one only because all five degrees are kept in their order; remove any one, and the structure collapses. Whatever the self produces in the natural — whatever rises up today on the face of the earth, in the flesh — is humbled in the end. It does not reach the height of the elevated states the Lord sets above it; it does not match their breadth or their length or their depth. The self's productions, from the smallest to the greatest, are at last swallowed up in the great sea of natural perception, taken back into the mass from which they came, because nothing the soul claims can stand on its own. Nothing endures apart from the power that bears the whole, apart from the divine going-forth that gives sign and order to the All, apart from his holy light. When the soul brings forth from itself in self-claiming, it has blasphemed against the wisdom that is the Lord's glory.

And to the rational the Lord makes five things known.

First, he informs the soul that it belongs to the kind of the light — that what it most truly is comes from him, not from itself.

Second, he loosens for the soul the understanding of the states it has passed through: how the degrees of formation actually exist, and likewise how the darkness exists — what self-claiming is, in what manner it stands. He teaches the soul to see both sides of itself.

Third, he proclaims the greatness of the light: how divine truth puts the self's claim to shame and prevails over it. The soul learns that the light is stronger than what had bound it.

Fourth, he teaches that the soul has come to the boundary of death — to the flesh, the outermost where self-love most operates — and that he himself brings the soul out by the upper gate of that lowest place, where before it had been sealed in.

Fifth, he shows that the self and its workings are bound at last with a great bond: those self-loving arrangements that habitually cover over the Good and the righteousness are themselves held fast, so that what hid the Lord's good cannot hide it any longer.

## Teaching 98

A question is raised: is there anything of life, anything bearing light, that comes forth from the threefold fixed structures which enclose the natural mind? — anything that, having entered into those bounds, rises again upward out of them?

The teaching answers: yes. When what has gone down into the natural is ready to come up, it does so as rays of light extending along a fitted channel. It does not rise by collapsing the order it must pass through; it rises by entering the great gates that stand open between the discrete degrees of the rational. Firmament by firmament, the gates are open. There are four of them, set open at every quarter of the natural mind, spread through its four directions; and they are open downward as well, into the dwelling-places where particular loves have taken up residence. Because of this opening, influx can reach all the way down, and what is the Lord's in the lowest places can be lifted again all the way up.

So the rays go inward through the gates where light shines forth, into the very place where what belongs to the Lord still lies held within what the soul has claimed. There the Lord takes by the hand the light that is in those places, together with the little of his own that is mingled there, and draws it. In that same place where the rational has been pressed and made firm in its proper degrees, a kind is chosen out from what is found, and that kind is borne up to the higher place, into conjunction with the Living. The other kind, which cannot be raised, is left to the lower side and remains there.

This is how regeneration moves through the soul: not by breaking the orders, but by passing through the gates the Lord keeps open between them; not by snatching, but by drawing what is his upward through the fitted channels degree by degree, while what cannot be conjoined falls of itself to where it belongs.

## Teaching 99

In the righteous soul there are three forms, three registers at which the one inner life is present.

The first is the spiritual form. This is the New Man — the rational as it has been made new. Here the Lord, ordering the inner mind, fashions himself a dwelling and comes inward and abides. What stands at this register is the rational fully ordered by truth, the place where influx is recognized and received as the Lord's.

The second is the psychic form. This is the residue and remainder of the New Man as it reaches downward — the part of the inner life still bound into the outer nature, where what is given and what has been claimed are not yet sorted. Here the Old Man is also found, and here the soul lies mingled with affections that are not its own: bitterness, desire, foolishness, envy, discord, and the false teachings of self-love. These cling to it because at this register the natural is still receiving its food from below and pouring itself downward into the body.

The third is the bodily form — the outermost, the form into which the inner life accommodates itself in ultimates. Into this the soul sinks when it descends to be fed; from this it must be carried back up.

This is the manner of the Living Soul: every day it is purified throughout the whole, and every day it passes through these three. From the bodily form it lays off the body, which is not its own. From the psychic form it lays off the affections mingled with it that are not its own — the wrath, the desire, the bitterness, the foolishness, the envy, the discord, and every evil teaching that does not belong to it. And in the spiritual form it lives, and conjoins itself inward with patience, with the perfection of faith, and with love that is queen over them all — the pure receivability by which the Lord is received without distortion, who is a robe to the New Man. This is what is called the hour of life: she is the first and she is also the last.

When the soul has been raised through the discrimination of these three, the way is blazed before it, it is drawn upward to the height, brought into the form where the Lord indwells, adorned in the New Man, and sealed in every member by that pure receivability of light that dwells within him.

## Teaching 100

When divine humanity goes forth into the soul's lower places to keep watch over what is below, it does not go in untouched ease. It enters the contest. It stands in great affliction, surrounded by the disorder of the natural mind in its claimed state — the depths that are fearful, the press of disordered cravings, the binding pressure of self-love organized as if it ruled. The self reigns over divine humanity for a season, standing over it in its likeness, as if it had taken what it could not take.

In this affliction a prayer comes into being. It is the first prayer, the prayer that all later prayer rests upon: the cry of the divine principle of life-bearing within the soul, turning toward the inmost source. She prays, she petitions, she glorifies, she blesses; she asks the Father for a great gift — for herself and for the divine humanity that has gone down — that he would send a faith, a redeemer, a helper to the one who stands in affliction. The Father sees the prayer. He receives it within himself. He grants it.

What he sends is his own operative power — the principle by which spiritual structure is built, the first that causes things to stand wide. This power goes down with the divine life-bearing, reaching to the very boundary of the lower limit, and there raises divine humanity up out of the contest. The gift of life is given. The journey is made; what oppressed is removed far off. The one who had been held in great affliction in the land below is loosened, set free of the press, set on his feet in the country of rest.

Then the building begins. The natural mind is given its proper structure: its firmaments by which degrees are kept distinct, its recurrent cycles of purification, its conveyances by which truth descends from above, its mountains of elevated state, its bones of structural good, its strongholds of preserved order. The whole is finished and gathered up beneath the eyes of light — the natural mind no longer left to itself but framed and held under the Lord's seeing.

When this is done, the inmost source of the Spirit stands together with the Lord at the register where his love is returned, and with the principle of divine order, and with the operative power, and with the divine humanity — these five at the consummation, glorifying and blessing the Father. They intercede now for what remains: for the living soul herself, who still stands mingled in the body of the natural, oppressed in the scourge of the deep, held among the fleshes of death. They ask that power be given — a guide of all the works, one who can come and purify what has been established, who can bring the living soul forth onto her feet in this whole. The Father takes their intercession from their hands and grants it.

Then he summons forth the third — the Lord's call going out to the rational at the moment the natural is ready to hear it. In his mercies the Ambassador reveals himself before the harsh underside, before the lower place of the soul. He chooses the living soul out from all the fleshes of death in which she had been mingled. He gives her wideness where she had been pressed. He sets her on her feet in the place of rest, with joy. So the third fulfills the purpose of the three: the purpose of those who prayed the Father concerning her, and his own purpose toward the living soul whom he had looked upon. He completes her; he gives her room out of oppression; he leads as foremost into the whole body of the regenerated rational.

The remaining fragments speak, as far as they can be heard, of the icon shown and hidden according to its time, of garments given, of the voice raised, of the prayer answered inward, of the soul made a free one — no longer held by what claimed her — and of Jesus the Radiance giving the power of light, completing the purpose of heart, that the hope received may stand forever. So is the molding of the soul accomplished: by prayer rising from affliction, by the Father's hearing, by the operative power that raises, by the call that chooses out, and by the radiance that completes — and you, hearing this, stand fast in what has been molded.

## Teaching 101

A question is asked: why is it that when something is trimmed from the soul's outer surface — a tip, an outgrowth — the soul feels no injury, yet when something is cut away from the body itself, from a member, the soul is pained at once?

The answer is given by likeness. The natural mind is as a ground, and the states established within it are as trees rooted in that ground. Some growths in the natural mind are like hair and the tip of the head and the end of a finger: they grow upon the outer surface, they can be pruned without injury, and they will rise again of themselves — they stand on their own without the soul needing to mourn them. Other growths are like trees that, once planted and grown, take their stand in the ground; if these are cut they spring up again another time. And there are still others which, when cut, do not grow back at all — once laid off, they are finished.

So it is with the affections and perceptions that have arisen in the natural mind. The flesh that arouses desire keeps coming forth out of itself, the way hair grows again at its own appointed times; it is fed by the impurities of what the soul takes in for its food, and so long as that intake continues, those outer growths return. The soul can prune them and prune them again, and feel no wound in its essential members; this is the surface trimming that costs nothing because what is being cut is not structural but only the outermost issuing.

But there are members of the natural mind that are structural: states woven into the body of the soul's life. To take one of these away is felt; the soul is pained immediately, because what is being cut is not surface but substance. These are the deeper trees, which when cut may yet grow back in another season; and these in turn must be distinguished from the deepest, which once removed do not return.

The teaching, then, is a doctrine of discrimination concerning regeneration. Not every laying-off is the same kind of laying-off. Some self-loves are surface outgrowths to be pruned often and without grief; some are rooted states that the Lord cuts at real cost to the soul's feeling, and which may return and need to be cut again; some, once removed, are gone for good. The soul is not to mistake the trimming of surface for the cutting of a member, nor to flinch at the trimming of surface as though a member were being lost. And it is to recognize that what keeps growing back of itself, returning through the impurities of its intake, is the outer kind — not a sign that regeneration has failed, but the ordinary condition of a flesh that arouses desire while the natural mind is still being fed from below.

(Much of the chapter survives only in fragments; the structure of question, bodily likeness, and threefold distinction among growths is clear, but the closing application to the impurities of food and to the desire-arousing flesh is partly conjectural from what remains.)

## Teaching 102

The teaching here is badly broken, and what follows can only trace the surviving thread.

It speaks of those who pass through a brief affliction, a sickness that lasts only a little while, in which the rational is loosed forth from the natural so that the inner understanding can see what is otherwise hidden. The soul is not torn from its house; it is unbound for a moment, and in that moment the eye that belongs to the inner man opens. Some habitually undergo this and are returned, falling back upon their accustomed ground.

Others undergo it differently. With these the loosening does not come at one and the same hour but is delayed; they abide in waiting, and the release comes later, reaching them only at the lowest part of their state, at the foot rather than at the head. The names by which the inner things are called are loosed forth in them in their own time, not all at once.

The teaching seems to distinguish manners of this loosening — how it comes down into the deep places of the natural mind, how it issues forth from there, how the same operation works in this fashion in some and in another fashion in others. It speaks of seven men of one kind and three of another, and of the hour at which the light reaches them, when what is the Lord's is taken up into the light wholly, in the manner proper to the way it actually exists within them.

The substance of the chapter, so far as it can be made out, is that the brief opening of the inner sight, the loosening of the rational from its claim upon the natural, does not happen uniformly. The Lord accommodates this awakening to the particular state of each soul: to some quickly, to some after long delay, to some at the head of their being and to some only at the foot, and the pattern by which this happens belongs to the structure of the soul itself and to the moment the Lord has appointed for it. Beyond this the surviving text does not allow a confident reading.

## Teaching 103

From the very beginning the soul stands between two conditions, and these two conditions have always been what they are. There is what the Lord gives — truth received, influx flowing through, the beautiful and the living — and there is what the soul has claimed for its own — the natural turned in upon itself, the bitter, the dead. These are not two stuffs contending in some outer field; they are the two states between which every rational soul finds itself, the light it has from above and the darkness it has from itself.

Regeneration is nothing other than the sorting of these two. The soul, illumined, learns to discriminate the beautiful from what has crept in beside it, and in that discrimination lays off what was never its own. What is given is recognized as given; what was claimed is let go. The beautiful is separated forth from the darkness, and the soul comes into the life it had been receiving all along.

## Teaching 104

Much of this chapter survives only in pieces, but the surviving thread can be followed. It opens by enumerating the structural supports by which the rational is held in being: the Great Mind that exists above; the inner image being born within the natural; the twelve angels, the twenty-two; the seven pillars that stand fast; the bound of love that surrounds the natural mind as a wall; the four bearing-up powers stretched beneath the whole and supporting it under their strength; the earth and all the heavens that lie below them; another five which are the rational's nourishment, by which the cosmos of the natural mind stands firm at the place of the Lord's Calling; the Hearing answering; the Radiance shining; the Lord giving stable form to the rational; twelve powers standing below; three great pillars of strength bearing up under the order placed there; the firmaments which the Lord's operative power has set at their hour, and the seeds sown in them which are made manifest there in the perfection of the ten; the great bearing-up principle holding the natural mind fast in its power. These are the three great supports against what would oppose them.

Then the question is asked: what name shall be added upon the Blessed One in the prayer that is prayed? What is the Yes and the Amen?

The answer: these have come into being from the great ones of the powers, and they were given a name — the Yes is reckoned to the Calling, the Amen to the Answer. So then: the Calling and the Hearing — these are the soul's opening of its mouth to the Spirit and to petition before the Divine Human. They are not themselves the ones who reign as principles of life and of those who live; the Divine Human himself is the one who has come forth as the soul's redeemer, and he, the First Man, is sought through the life-bearing principle and through the Father of the living. But the Calling and the Hearing — the divine summons going forth and the soul's answering rising to meet it — these are what reign for the soul through this whole work. They reign over the states of greatness up until the moment the Divine Human and the soul are mingled with death; and they become helpers of the Divine Human in his ascent upward, giving him over into the conjunction of the Father of Life and the Mother of Life. They are refiners of the Living Soul: they are her helpers; they grant her remembrance — the holding-present of what she has received — whether through the established perceptions of the rational or through the very fashioning of the natural body in which the soul is to be formed again.
