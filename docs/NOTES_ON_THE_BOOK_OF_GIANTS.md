Based on the sources provided, here is the text of the "Book of the Wars of YHWH" (or "Wars of the Lord") as it appears in the three primary recensions of Numbers 21:14–15.

The text referenced is a fragment of an ancient, lost victory poem used here to establish the geographic border of Arnon.

### I. THE MASORETIC TEXT (Hebrew)
**Source:** *Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia*

**Hebrew Text:**
> אֶת־וָהֵב בְּסוּפָה וְאֶת־הַנְּחָלִים אַרְנוֹן׃ וְאֶשֶׁד הַנְּחָלִים אֲשֶׁר נָטָה לְשֶׁבֶת עָר וְנִשְׁעַן לִגְבוּל מוֹאָב

**Translation:**
> "Waheb in Suphah, and the wadis of Arnon, and the slope of the wadis that extends to the seat of Ar, and leans to the border of Moab."

*   **Notes:**
    *   **Waheb in Suphah (אֶת־וָהֵב בְּסוּפָה):** The Masoretic Text treats *Waheb* as a proper noun (a location) and *Suphah* as a location (likely related to *Suph*). The grammar is elliptical; the verb is missing, implying "[He took] Waheb..." or "[He conquered] Waheb...".
    *   **Wadis (הַנְּחָלִים):** Refers to the riverbeds or torrents.

***

### II. THE SEPTUAGINT (Greek)
**Source:** *Septuagint*

**Greek Text:**
> Τὴν Ζωὸβ ἐφλόγισεν καὶ τοὺς χειμάρρους ᾿Αρνών· καὶ τοὺς χειμάρρους κατέστησεν κατοικίσαι "Ηρ, καὶ πρόσκειται τοῖς ὁρίοις Μωάβ.

**Translation:**
> "The Zoob he consumed with flame, and the torrents of Arnon; and the torrents he established to inhabit Er, and it lies upon the borders of Moab."

*   **Notes:**
    *   **The Zoob (Τὴν Ζωὸβ):** The Greek translators transliterated the Hebrew *Waheb* as *Zoob*.
    *   **Consumed with flame (ἐφλόγισεν):** This is a critical divergence. The translators read the Hebrew *b'suphah* ("in Suphah") as deriving from a root meaning "to burn" or "consume" (possibly associated with *saph*). Instead of a geographical location, the Greek text describes an act of divine incineration.
    *   **Er ("Ηρ):** The Greek renders the Hebrew *Ar* (the capital of Moab) as *Er*.

***

### III. THE SAMARITAN PENTATEUCH
**Source:** *The Samaritan Pentateuch*,

**Samaritan Hebrew Text (Transliterated):**
> *et-Wa'eb b'Sufa w'et-annā̊lǝm Arenan...*

**Translation (Literal):**
> "Wa'eb in Sufa, and the valleys of the Arenan, which he caused them to inherit, and which extends toward the dwelling of a city, and leans to the border of Muwwab."

*   **Notes:**
    *   **Wars of Shema:** The Samaritan text cites the book as the "Wars of Shema" (The Name), utilizing their standard substitution for the Tetragrammaton.
    *   **The Midrashic Reading:** While the literal Samaritan text aligns with the Masoretic geography, the **Samaritan Targum** (the interpretive translation used by the community) reads the verse entirely differently:
        *   It interprets *Wa'eb* not as a place, but as derived from *y-h-b* (to give/love), translating it as **"Love."**
        *   It interprets *Sufa* not as a storm or location, but as the **Red Sea** (*Yam Suph*).
        *   **Targumic Reading:** "With [God's] love in the Red Sea, and with those who pass the Arnon..."

### FORENSIC SUMMARY OF THE VARIATIONS

1.  **MT:** Lists specific, obscure geographic coordinates ("Waheb in Suphah").
2.  **LXX:** Describes a supernatural war event ("Zoob he consumed with flame").
3.  **SP:** Matches the MT geographically in the written text, but interprets the "War" theologically as an act of Divine Love at the Red Sea in their oral/Targumic tradition.
Based on the sources provided, the specific text of the "Book of the Wars of YHWH" (Numbers 21:14–15)—whether in the Masoretic "Waheb in Suphah," the Septuagint's "Consumed by flame," or the Samaritan's "Love in the Red Sea"—does not appear in these Manichaean texts.
However, there are significant thematic echoes and "broken variants" regarding the context of that biblical passage (the wars of the Giants and the use of Fire) within the provided sources.
Here is a breakdown of why the specific text is missing, but where the mythological concepts overlap:
1. The Absence of "Waheb" and "Arnon" (Geography vs. Mythology)
The Manichaean texts provided do not concern themselves with the specific political geography of Moab or the Arnon wadis mentioned in the Masoretic Text. Instead, Manichaean geography is cosmic.
• The Manichaean "Wars": Unlike the "Wars of YHWH" which were territorial skirmishes in Numbers, the "Wars" described in these sources are cosmic battles between the Sons of Light and the Sons of Darkness. The text explicitly lists "The Five Wars" that the sons of light waged against the darkness.
• The "Book of Giants" Connection: The biblical passage in Numbers 21 appears just before the defeat of Og, King of Bashan (a giant). While the specific poem about Waheb is missing, the Manichaean sources contain extensive fragments of the Book of Giants, which features the giant Ohya (or Sāhm in the Iranian versions). Ohya is traditionally identified with the biblical Og. Therefore, while the geography of Numbers 21 is absent, the characters (the Giants) associated with that biblical era are present in a different narrative tradition.
2. The "Consumed by Flame" Variant (LXX Parallel)
The Septuagint (Greek) translates the Hebrew place name Suphah as an event: "he consumed with flame" (ephlogisen). While the Manichaean texts do not cite this verse, they contain a very similar apocalyptic image of a "Great Fire" used as a weapon of war and purification.
• The Great Fire: The sources describe a "Great Fire" (ʾdwr wzrg) that will burn for 1,468 years.
• Fire as a Weapon: Just as the LXX reads the "War of the Lord" as an act of burning, the Manichaean Book of Giants describes angels using "fire, naphtha, and brimstone" to battle the demons and giants.
• The Sea of Fire: The Samaritan tradition reads Suphah as the Red Sea (Yam Suph). The Manichaean texts describe a cosmic "Sea of Fire" or a giant of the sea formed by fire and lust. While not the biblical Red Sea, the imagery of a watery abyss consumed by or mixed with fire is a shared "broken" motif.
3. "Love" (Samaritan Parallel)
The Samaritan Targum interprets Waheb as "Love" (via the root y-h-b, to give).
• Manichaean Parallels: The concept of "Love" is central to the Manichaean divine structure, though not in the geographic sense of Numbers. The "Beloved of the Lights" is a specific divinity mentioned in the Kephalaia.
• However, there is no textual evidence in the provided sources that connects this theological concept to the specific verse in Numbers 21.
Summary of Findings
You will not find the verse "Waheb in Suphah" in these texts. The Manichaean tradition generally rejected the specific history of the Jewish Torah (Moses's wars) in favor of the "Enochic" tradition (the history of the Giants and Watchers before the Flood).
Therefore, the "War" mentioned in these texts is not the conquest of Moab, but the Primordial War of the First Man against the Darkness, and the "Giants" mentioned are the ancestors of the enemies Moses fought in the biblical context of Numbers 21.
